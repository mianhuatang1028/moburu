Zheng

Chapter Fourteen: Zheng

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Zheng” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Western Mountains, Book Three”), on Mount Zhang’e there is a beast shaped like a red leopard, with five tails and a single horn, whose cry sounds like stone striking stone. As the saying goes: five tails on one body — one horn charging alone.

It took exactly one month from start to finish for Zhang Jingyu and Zheng Zhiyong to get divorced. First he moved out of his home overnight, packing seven or eight cardboard boxes and carrying all his personal belongings out. This was where he and Zhang Jingyu had lived together for nine years. In it was every calm, still-water day from their son’s birth until now — the boy growing bit by bit from a tiny baby to a babbling toddler to a long, skinny schoolboy — all the family’s shared memories. Now all of it was stored in a short-term rental storage unit; a space of less than two square meters held the past twenty years of Zheng Zhiyong’s life with room to spare.

A few days later Zheng Zhiyong transferred the white BYD in his name to Zhang Jingyu. A transfer between spouses meant submitting an application online, but the procedure wasn’t complicated. Now he had truly, completely walked away with nothing. Not only that, he had to pay his ex-wife and son fifteen thousand yuan a month in maintenance, and over the next few years pay his wife and son a total of three million in compensation. All he had left on his bank card was this month’s newly paid salary. It was the last shred of dignity Zhang Jingyu had left him.

For the next stretch he first flew to Changsha on business and spent two weeks working on projects with Zhao Bin and the others, then came back to Beijing, days and nights all blurred together. He had nowhere to go, so after getting off the plane he took a taxi to the gate of Diao Kexin’s compound. It was only a kilometer from what used to be his home, which made him feel all the more how changeable the affairs of this world are, and he sighed without end.

Diao Kexin came out to meet him. “You can stay at my place for a few days first,” she said. “Get your breath back, and we’ll find you a place to rent nearby together.” For the past several days Diao Kexin had been comforting Zheng Zhiyong day and night on WeChat and on the phone. She truly hadn’t expected this man would really divorce for her, and do it so cleanly and decisively. Compared with the other one, the old man Gao Fei, for whom she’d had a child and who still wouldn’t divorce, Old Zheng’s sincerity toward her could hardly fail to move her. And this man was younger than Gao Fei, only four years older than she was, in good physical shape, and naturally far better than Gao Fei in bed. On top of that, he’d graduated from a top university, was professionally trained and honed by years of systematic technical work in the state system, gifted, diligent and willing to put in the hours; the technical strength in his hands was a match for a mid-sized company. The only pity was that he had no money now — actually penniless, the kind of broke where you have nothing to your name. Ah, what a pity that was.

Old Zheng walked into Diao Kexin’s home. It was a big four-bedroom flat decorated in the traditional American style that had been fashionable more than a decade ago: elaborate décor, furniture in a white base with gold trim everywhere on the wood, gold hardware on every handle and pull, solid workmanship and materials. The living room had a brown leather sofa, likewise with fussy decorative piping stitched along the arms. The overall effect was ornate and lavish but not at all classy. There was a study decorated entirely to a man’s taste: against the wall stood a row of bookcases with glass doors inlaid with gold trim, and in it sat a heavy American-style desk and a big leather executive chair. Diao Kexin’s daughter was at kindergarten and not home. There was only a nanny from Guizhou who had just come out of the kitchen. She came from Diao Kexin’s hometown in Guizhou, a distant relative on her mother’s side, and Diao Kexin called her “Auntie Xiaoxia.”

“Auntie Xiaoxia, this is my friend Zheng Zhiyong. He’s just come to Beijing for work and doesn’t have anywhere to stay, so he’ll be in the guest room for a few days.” And with that Diao Kexin led Old Zheng openly into the guest room. Nobody normally slept in this little room; now and then, when Diao Kexin’s parents came to Beijing, they stayed in it. It was simply furnished: two single beds side by side with an aisle between them, and a small wardrobe by the door.

The two went in, and Diao Kexin closed the door. She guided Old Zheng to sit on the edge of the bed, then straddled his lap, put her arms around his neck and began to kiss him lightly.

At first Zheng Zhiyong felt a bit stiff — after all, this was a room completely strange to him, and there was a stranger just outside the door — but Diao Kexin’s kisses went from gentle to fervent, and soon his little general was standing to attention in full martial splendor. So he swept her up in his arms, laid her down and pressed her beneath him, and tore off her clothes; she offered no resistance whatsoever, and in that little room the two of them did battle without restraint for several hundred rounds.

When it was over, Diao Kexin lay in the crook of Old Zheng’s arm and said slowly, “I’m still worried — worried Zhang Jingyu will keep making scenes at the company. If Gao Fei finds out about you and me…”

Old Zheng sighed softly. “That’s what worries me too. The reason I agreed to all her terms and walked away with nothing was to placate her as much as I could, so she wouldn’t spread this any further, to Yuanwang.”

Diao Kexin said, “So is she satisfied now?”

Old Zheng said, “I’ve done everything I can. I don’t even know whether I’ll be able to pay that three million in the future.” The moment the words were out, Old Zheng felt a stab of regret. The woman in his arms loved his ambition and his drive; a man without even the confidence to earn a few million was simply not someone Diao Kexin would look at twice. So he hurried to patch it up: “But I’m confident. Since it’s come to this, it’s Heaven giving me a push — I can, and I must, make something of myself.”

Diao Kexin was clearly very pleased with this declaration. She kissed Zheng Zhiyong lightly on the lips. “For now we still need to build up our strength at Yuanwang. Next I’ll hand my client relationships over to you. Tonight, come with me to a dinner with clients.”

Zheng Zhiyong nodded. Strange to say: all these past years, thanks to Zhang Jingyu’s coolness, their married life had long since gone flat — once every few months, as a matter of routine. But beside this woman he felt as if he’d been pumped full of chicken blood. They’d only been talking for a few minutes and he felt ready again, so he started kissing Diao Kexin again, and the woman was perfectly in tune with him: seeing Old Zheng perk up, she closed her eyes, and at the height of it she too enjoyed another round of churning the rivers and seas.

They went out together before dinner, and on the way out they ran into the little girl, just brought home by the nanny. This girl of nearly seven wasn’t lively and cheerful like most children; she had a timid air about her, and didn’t seem especially happy to see her mother either; she just lowered her head and said a soft “Mama.” She didn’t dare greet the strange uncle, and once her mother said she could, she ran off into her room by herself.

And so the two of them spent several shameless days together in Diao Kexin’s big home. A few days later, Old Zheng settled on a little one-bedroom a few hundred meters from the compound. After paying three months’ rent and a month’s deposit up front, more than half the cash in his bank account was gone. When choosing the place, Old Zheng picked one closer to his former home — perhaps deep down he still felt a deep attachment to Zhang Jingyu and their son, an attachment he couldn’t tell anyone about now, that could only be buried deep inside him. Gao Fei was coming to Beijing tomorrow, so Zheng Zhiyong had to move into his newly rented place today. He couldn’t be bothered to fetch his things from the storage unit; he just took a few changes of clothes and moved into the single one-bedroom, which had the basic necessities and not a breath of life.

The next time he saw Diao Kexin, she said, “We should hurry up and get our own company going now. I have some client relationships — there’s a project opportunity we can put into our new company right away.” Zheng Zhiyong said okay.

Diao Kexin said, “The shares in this company: seventy for me, thirty for you. We both find clients; you organize the outside supply chain; you control all the technology and quality; profits, after deducting the company’s running costs, get split according to our agreed shareholding.” Zheng Zhiyong said okay.

Zheng Zhiyong asked Diao Kexin, “If our company gets big, will you be willing to leave Gao Fei?”

Diao Kexin said, “Of course I would. Everything I’m doing now is so that someday I’ll have the means and the backbone to leave him. But,” she added, “there are so many ties between him and me. Even if I want to leave, it isn’t that easy. If — I’m only saying if — it turns out you and I can’t end up together after all,” a flash of sorrow crossed her eyes, and she went on, “at least we’ll still have this company connecting us. Our abilities complement each other perfectly; as long as our hearts are together, we can definitely run this company well. Even if one day something goes wrong and we’re forced apart, I hope we can always be inseparable partners in business.”

“If, one day, I have no choice but to go back to Gao Fei for a while, I hope you’ll build a company bigger and better than Yuanwang — and then you can steal me away,” Diao Kexin said slowly.

The next day Gao Fei came to Beijing on business. When Diao Kexin saw him, she told him as usual that a certain official needed a hundred thousand for “business expenses,” and without a word Gao Fei went to the bank counter, withdrew a hundred thousand in cash from his own account and handed it to her. That night Gao Fei went back with her to her place in Beijing and stayed.

The day after that, Diao Kexin brought this hundred thousand in cash, a single tight bundle, to see Zheng Zhiyong. She took the money out of her little bag, had him hold out his hand, put the tightly bound hundred thousand into his palm, and tilted her head, smiling at him.

“This is the money to start our company.”

A wave rose in Zheng Zhiyong’s heart. His future career, his own destiny, were made solid in that moment in this bundle of banknotes. From now on this company would bind him and this woman together in a clearly visible future — a future of working hard and fighting side by side for their business. He suddenly felt strength surging through his whole body. Diao Kexin said, “Neither of us can appear in this company under our real names right now. If you agree, we’ll have my brother and sister-in-law hold the shares for us and register the company in their names first, and once you’ve quit, we’ll transfer it to you. Hurry up and think of a name for the company.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Everything we deliver these days, software or hardware, has to be combined with AI. I can get suppliers to deliver all of those product lines. Our company doesn’t need to hire or support staff — I’m a whole team by myself. So let’s call it Qingneng Technology: qing for light, neng for capable. How’s that?”

Diao Kexin answered Old Zheng with a kiss. The two of them went to the bank together and transferred the money into her brother’s account; once the company was set up, the money would go into the company.

Registration came through in less than a week. Zheng Zhiyong looked at the brand-new business license, his heart surging. This gave him far more of a sense of fighting for his own cause than the last time he’d started a business in the university incubator — especially since Diao Kexin was the major shareholder. “So what? These days I’d hand my life to this woman, never mind the shares,” Zheng Zhiyong sighed to himself.

Diao Kexin quickly landed the company two subcontracts, one worth seven hundred thousand and one worth 1.5 million. The seven-hundred-thousand order wasn’t difficult; it was just that the requirements for supplier qualifications were extremely high — registered capital of over ten million, plus military security clearance and manufacturing qualifications. Zheng Zhiyong solved this with a few casual phone calls: through a friend of a friend he borrowed a shell company to act as a channel, which would bid, sign the contract and issue the invoices, while suppliers Zheng Zhiyong knew well did the production and delivery and he handled the technical design and quality control. The two sides signed a contract with a twelve percent channel fee, and they still had a forty-five percent profit margin on the order.

The other order, for 1.5 million, came from a different client: a subcontract for a custom electrical component. Because it was technically quite difficult and not the general contractor’s specialty, they needed a suitable outside company to help develop and deliver it. Diao Kexin had picked this up over dinner with the client and passed it on to Zheng Zhiyong, and as soon as Zheng Zhiyong got in touch, a brief exchange was enough for the other side to see that he was exactly the old hand with the most experience in that component.

It has to be said: Zheng Zhiyong really was a rare talent when it came to technology. He went to an equipment maker in Dongguan that he’d worked with years ago in the system, when he was working on the lunar rover. The owner took technology seriously and had dozens of experienced engineers on staff. Zheng Zhiyong spent more than two weeks at the factory working it out with them day and night, and at last they produced the schematics. Then Qingneng signed with the client, and he signed a second-tier purchase contract with the factory, and after that it was a matter of waiting for production and delivery, expected in about two months; in the meantime Engineer Zheng would only need to visit the factory a few times to check the work in progress. This order would give them a fifty percent gross margin.

After that he flew to Changsha again and stayed another two weeks, helping Zhao Bin solve several technical modules in one of Yuanwang’s own big bids, and while he was at it recommended several suppliers to them, solving the whole delivery problem in one go.

In meetings at the Changsha office with Gao Fei and Sister Yan’er, Zheng Zhiyong could feel the boss was as friendly toward him as ever — Sister Yan’er especially, who beamed at him when she saw him and seemed even more respectful and polite than before. Zheng Zhiyong knew that, for now, he was still safe at Yuanwang. Both his relationship with Diao Kexin and the company the two of them were running on the side seemed well hidden; for the time being, nobody knew.

Having gotten a taste of success from her own company’s first smooth contracts and advance payments, Diao Kexin began trying to steer one of Yuanwang’s orders into it. The requirement had originally come from a Yuanwang client, and Diao Kexin smoothly recommended Qingneng instead. The contract was worth 2.4 million, and it happened to be something Old Zheng had done for the company in the previous phase, so delivery would be easy. He went to his supplier and agreed the delivery process, supplied the drawings himself, gave technical guidance and set things up at the factory, and directed the supplier to custom-make and deliver the product. This contract would give them a sixty percent gross margin and had to be delivered within ninety days.

With business going so smoothly, Old Zheng and Diao Kexin both felt time flying by. Soon it would be the Mid-Autumn Festival.

And this Mid-Autumn Festival was the deadline of Diao Kexin’s ultimatum to Gao Fei.

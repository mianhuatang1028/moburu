Chapter Thirty-Eight: Juru

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) Last updated: 2026-11-12 16:39 © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Juru” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Central Mountains, Book Eleven”), on Mount Yidi there is a beast shaped like a fei-rat, with white ears and a white snout, called the juru; when it appears, its land sees great war. As the saying goes: white ears, white snout, a rat’s body that keeps itself hidden — when it appears, troops rise; it moves before it makes a sound.

Cong Shasha had been in Beijing for a week.

While Old Zheng was seeing Diao Kexin, she was making the rounds at the Seventh Academy. Section Chief Wang came out of a meeting, saw her sitting in the coffee shop downstairs, walked over and said, “Little Cong, have you been waiting long?” Cong Shasha stood up and said, “Section Chief Wang, I’ve only just got here myself. That thing you mentioned last time — I went back and worked through it, and there are a few points I’d like to ask your advice on.”

Section Chief Wang said, “Come on, let’s talk upstairs.” The two of them sat in his office for half an hour. Cong Shasha spread a process chart on the desk, marking out every step from project approval to tender to evaluation to award. She asked her questions one by one, and Section Chief Wang answered them one by one. When she got to the evaluation stage, Section Chief Wang said, “On the evaluation panel, Dr. Xiao has the final say on the technical side, and Old Zhou in the project office runs the process side. Old Zhou — don’t go by the fact that he’s not very senior. When it comes to holding people up, he’s a master. Get him on side, and the process will move.”

Cong Shasha said, “I haven’t made a connection with Director Zhou yet.”

Section Chief Wang said, “I’ll put in a word for you. Whether it works is up to you.”

Cong Shasha said, “Section Chief Wang, that word from you is worth more than anything.”

That evening Deputy Director Liu took her to dinner. Halfway through, Deputy Director Liu said, “On that night-vision project, Dr. Xiao has her worries. She says the technical direction’s fine, but the people who’ve really done this kind of product don’t have enough technical depth, and they may not be able to pull it off.”

Cong Shasha said, “Director Liu, is that what Dr. Xiao said?” Shasha was startled inside — this didn’t match what Old Zheng had told her. In fact the assessment was quite objective: they really were short on experience overall.

Cong Shasha said, “Director Liu, we have a few outside heavyweights too, people with project experience. As long as Dr. Xiao gives us good guidance, we’ll definitely get it done.”

Deputy Director Liu smiled and said, “This project was going to be joint development with everyone anyway. You’ll go through evaluation again. The other bidders are pretty strong too.”

“Well, we’re certainly confident. Once we’ve got the thing made, can the production come to us?”

“Production — once the volume goes up, all the documentation will definitely have to be opened up, and several suppliers brought in together. The technology’s something you’ll have cracked, so as long as your factory’s qualifications are in order, you can be the primary supplier and take half the orders. That shouldn’t be a problem.”

Shasha replied with an “Okay.”

It was a very important meeting. Old Zheng was waiting in the room for Shasha to come back, waiting to ask her what Deputy Director Liu had said. When Shasha got back to the hotel, she lay straight down on the bed and sighed, “Being ‘on’ for the leaders is so exhausting.” Old Zheng sat on the edge of the bed and said, “Thanks for all your hard work. How did it go tonight?”

Cong Shasha rolled over, sat up, rested her hand on his chest and said, “Brother Yong, on this project, if Dr. Xiao doesn’t come in with us, we can’t pull it off.”

Old Zheng said, “I know. Of all of them, she’s the only one with prior experience. She understands it better than anyone on the team right now.”

Cong Shasha said, “Right. Dr. Xiao understands the technology, and you understand delivery and the supply chain. Only with the two of you together will this project be solid.”

Old Zheng said, “She’s the client.”

Cong Shasha said, “Client people can become our people too.”

Old Zheng said nothing. He lay down beside Shasha and looked at the ceiling.

Cong Shasha said, “Go see her again tomorrow. This time don’t talk technology — talk partnership. Someone like her, if you beat around the bush, she’ll look down on you.”

The next afternoon Old Zheng arranged to meet Dr. Xiao again. The same teahouse, the same private room toward the back.

Dr. Xiao got there before him. When she saw him come in she looked up and said, “Mr. Zheng, did you look over the proposal before you sent it to me?”

Old Zheng said, “I reviewed it myself.”

Dr. Xiao said, “It’s full of problems in the details.” She took out her laptop to open the file and show him her annotations; the file was already covered in red comments.

Old Zheng sat down and ordered a green tea. He said, “Dr. Xiao, let’s not look at the file yet. I have to admit something to you: on this project, we don’t have enough experience.”

Dr. Xiao glanced at him. “It’s a new project. Nobody has all the experience.”

Old Zheng said, “Our proposal is technically feasible. Hailu is strong on mechanics and the supply chain, but the infrared algorithms are more than he can chew. If we really do win this project, after it there’s commissioning into service, there’s mass production. We have gaps.”

Dr. Xiao said, “And so?”

Old Zheng said, “And so I’d like to ask you — not to advise us, but to build it with us.”

Dr. Xiao put down her teacup and said, “Mr. Zheng, I’m Seventh Academy.”

Old Zheng said, “I know. One person playing two parts. If you take this on, we’re one team — we build it together, we bid together. If the bid succeeds, I won’t route it through your accounts or anything in your own name. I’ll do a technical-services contract; you name an outside company, and my side will sign with it. Five percent of the whole business’s revenue is yours, as a hidden share. If the bid doesn’t come through, we each put in our own effort and each take our own losses, every one of us at our own risk.”

Dr. Xiao looked at him for a good while. She said, “Mr. Zheng, do you know what you’re saying?”

Old Zheng said, “I know. I’m recruiting someone on the client side to do our technology.”

Dr. Xiao said, “That’s against the rules.”

Old Zheng said, “It is. But on this project, without you, we can’t do it well. If we can’t do it well, the Seventh Academy will have to switch suppliers. If they switch, all the time and all the energy you’ve spent so far will be wasted. You believe in my proposal, you believe in me, and so far we’re the ones with the most technical strength too.”

Dr. Xiao said nothing. She picked up her teacup, drank and put it down.

Old Zheng said, “Go back and think it over. When you’ve decided, send me a message. If you don’t want to, just act as if I never came today.”

Dr. Xiao glanced at him and said, “I’ll go back and think about it.”

He rushed back to Xi’an that evening. Before the plane took off, Old Zheng got a WeChat from Dr. Xiao, just one word: “Okay.” Old Zheng glanced at Shasha in the next seat and said, “Dr. Xiao’s on board.”

Sure enough, Dr. Xiao threw all her brains into the project. She couldn’t come to Xi’an, and Old Zheng couldn’t keep running to Beijing. The two of them worked out a method: for project meetings they’d use WeCom, with Dr. Xiao’s account showing a fake avatar and a code name that gave away neither gender, her voice run through a voice changer so that she sounded like a middle-aged man.

The first time they held a meeting this way, Zhang Hailu was taken aback. He heard an unfamiliar man’s voice coming from the phone, saying “The algorithm architecture in part three won’t do,” and he looked up at Old Zheng. Old Zheng said, “A college classmate of mine, Engineer Wang. He’s CTO at a company in Beijing, helping us look over the technology part-time.”

Zhang Hailu said, “Hello, Engineer Wang.”

The voice said, “Hello. Carry on.”

After that they got used to it. Every meeting, Zhang Hailu would present the technical proposal, and when he got to a certain point, that voice would pop up out of the phone: “This parameter’s written too high. They can’t make it. Bring it down two notches.” Zhang Hailu would note it down and change it. When he’d changed it, the voice would say, “Fine. Next.”

Sometimes Engineer Wang would argue with Zhang Hailu too. Zhang Hailu would say the structural strength was fine, and Engineer Wang would say the strength was fine, but had he considered the assembly tolerances? When it was made in batches on the production line, the tolerances would add up, and this structure of his would have to be reworked. Zhang Hailu would be silent for a while and say, then I’ll run the numbers again. Engineer Wang would say, run them, and send them to me when you’re done.

Sometimes Zhang Hailu would ask Old Zheng, “Mr. Zheng, this Engineer Wang — which company’s he with?”

Old Zheng would say, “Out of town. You wouldn’t know them.”

Zhang Hailu said, “He’s very good.”

Old Zheng said, “He’s all right.”

And Zhang Hailu stopped asking.

Apart from the meetings, Dr. Xiao would also send messages late at night. Old Zheng would be lying in bed and his phone would light up — it was her: “Your quote’s too low. If it’s low, they’ll think you’re cutting corners. Raise it a bit, but not too much.”

Old Zheng replied: “Raise it how much?”

Dr. Xiao named a figure.

Old Zheng said, “Okay.”

Dr. Xiao said, “Also, spell out the amount of fine-tuning data clearly. Don’t be vague. They’ll ask about it at evaluation.”

Old Zheng said, “Okay.”

Dr. Xiao asked, “Where’s the bid document at?”

Old Zheng said, “Almost done. Before the bid meeting next week, it’ll be finalized.”

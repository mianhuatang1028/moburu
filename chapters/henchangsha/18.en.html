Chongming Bird

Chapter Eighteen: Chongming Bird

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Chongming Bird” — an auspicious bird of old with two pupils in each eye; it can drive off fierce beasts, and its light shines like a candle. As the saying goes: double pupils can see far, yet find it hard to see into one’s own heart.

A few days after seeing Lu You, Old Zheng received the hundred thousand Lu You transferred to him. He paid off all the microloans and credit cards he owed and felt a touch of ease he hadn’t felt in a long time. He went to a barber, had his hair washed and trimmed and his beard shaved clean, and bought himself a few seasonal clothes at the mall.

He felt Lu You was right: if he could still win his wife back, that really was the best choice right now. The scale of his feelings had always swung back and forth; even at the moments he loved Diao Kexin most, he had never stopped missing Zhang Jingyu, never stopped missing his son, Zheng Ruixi.

Besides, Diao Kexin had only ever treated him as a spare tire — as a stepping stone, even — trampling his dignity, his future, everything he had, just to reach her own goal, without caring whether he lived or died. Right now he honestly couldn’t tell whether he felt more hate for her or more love. It seemed he hated her to death, and hated most of all that she’d left without caring whether he lived or died.

Was she happy now? Had she gotten the marriage she wanted? Sometimes he still couldn’t help wondering.

But he knew the thing he should do most right now was find Zhang Jingyu. He dutifully got a bunch of red roses and went back to wait outside his old home. From afternoon into night, he never saw Zhang Jingyu’s car pull into their parking space. When night fell and he saw the light in their flat come on, he used his access card to open the building door and couldn’t help going upstairs to his old front door. Good heavens — it had only been two months or so, and there was a red “double happiness” character pasted on the door.

He was stunned. There was a roaring in his ears.

After hesitating again and again, he rang the bell.

A young man opened the door and asked in surprise, “Who are you looking for?” From behind him came a young woman’s voice: “Isn’t it the takeout?”

“Oh, oh, sorry to bother you — I’m looking for the family who used to live here. They’re good friends of mine. I came to visit.”

The young man said, “The previous owners, you mean? We bought this place two months ago. The family who lived here moved out last month, after the handover.”

Old Zheng felt as if the earth were shaking and the mountains swaying. He fought down his trembling and went back downstairs.

At first light the next morning he went to wait at his son’s school gate. From the first child through the gate to the last, he waited until the gate was shut and the bell rang for class, and he never saw Zheng Ruixi.

Once again Old Zheng felt the earth shake and the mountains sway. His head was foggy and heavy. He couldn’t hold back any longer and called Zhang Jingyu, more than ten times in a row; no one answered.

His scalp went numb. After a long while Zhang Jingyu typed a few words on WeChat: “If there’s something, leave a message on WeChat.”

“Jingyu, I went to Zheng Ruixi’s school. I didn’t see him. Is he all right?”

“He’s fine. What do you want?”

“Jingyu, I want to see you both.”

“If you have something to say, leave a message on WeChat.”

“Jingyu, can I call you?”

She stopped replying.

Zheng Zhiyong wrote a very long message of remorse and apology and sent it to Zhang Jingyu’s mother, his former mother-in-law. The moment it was sent, he found she’d already blocked him.

He sent the same message to Zhang Jingyu’s father, his former father-in-law. Same thing: the moment it went, he found he’d been blocked.

Zheng Zhiyong called his mother-in-law. She didn’t answer and rejected the call, and when he tried again there was nothing but the short busy tone — blocked again, it seemed. Calling his father-in-law’s phone was the same: rejected first, and then he could never get through again.

Then a call came up on Zheng Zhiyong’s phone: Zhang Jingyu’s cousin, now a police officer in Tongzhou. Zhang Jingyu and this cousin were both only children, the only two of their generation in the family living and working in Beijing, and the two families saw a lot of each other, often getting together for meals at holidays. Yes — this was the very cousin who had come along with Zhang Jingyu to catch him in the act; his phone still held the photo of Zheng Zhiyong and that woman caught in their sordid little scene.

Zhang Jingyu’s cousin said gravely on the phone, “Zheng Zhiyong, I’m giving you a serious warning. Stop harassing my sister and her family. Do you understand?”

“Xiaojie, I just want to know how they’re doing. I know they’ve moved.”

“Is that any of your business?” Xiaojie answered coldly, and hung up.

Zheng Zhiyong quietly ran through, in his head, everyone he could think of. He called a friend who had joined the academy with the two of them in the same graduating intake. She and Jingyu had later both had boys in the same year, and the two boys had ended up at the same primary school, in the same class. The families saw a lot of each other; this spring they’d even taken the kids camping together outside the city.

Old Zheng called her, and she answered. Zheng Zhiyong said, “Little Ding, hi, it’s Zheng Zhiyong.”

Little Ding: “Hello, Engineer Zheng. What can I do for you?”

Old Zheng: “It’s a bit embarrassing — I’d like to trouble you to ask something. You know Jingyu and I are divorced?”

Little Ding hesitated a moment, then said quietly, “Mm.”

Old Zheng: “Is Zheng Ruixi still in the same class as your Gaogao?”

Little Ding: “Zheng Ruixi changed schools at the end of September.”

Old Zheng: “Changed to where?”

Little Ding: “I heard somewhere in Xicheng.”

Old Zheng: “Do you know which school?”

Little Ding: “That I don’t know.”

Old Zheng: “Did you know Jingyu sold the flat?”

Little Ding: “I knew. She moved to Xicheng.”

Old Zheng: “Do you know where in Xicheng?”

Little Ding: “That I don’t know.”

Old Zheng: “Thank you, Little Ding.”

Little Ding was silent for a few seconds, hesitated, and hung up.

Old Zheng sank into agonizing recollection. In those few months when he and Diao Kexin were stuck together like glue, Zhang Jingyu had done it all — the divorce, selling the flat, buying another, moving Zheng Ruixi to a new school. He had been completely unaware of any of it; in all those months he hadn’t seen his son even once. How he missed Zheng Ruixi now.

He sent Zheng Ruixi a red packet. This time the person at the other end didn’t tap to claim it right away.

Before he knew it, Old Zheng’s face was once again streaming with tears.

But he knew nothing.

If he’d known that Zhang Jingyu had put a GPS tracker in his white BYD, which was how she’d found out where Diao Kexin lived and seen him giving that woman red roses, he would never have brought red roses to apologize while he waited to see Zhang Jingyu. Or should he have put a tracker in her car too? Then at least he might know where mother and son were now.

He went to the gate of Zhang Jingyu’s workplace and waited from before the end of the workday until it was pitch dark, and never once saw the family’s white BYD drive out of the gate.

Zheng Zhiyong, oh, Zheng Zhiyong — he knew nothing.

Zhang Jingyu had sold that white BYD secondhand too. She’d bought a brand-new black sedan, and changed the license plate as well. She was determined to live a completely new life — a life with this man no longer in it, a life lived over from the start.

Zheng Zhiyong, oh, Zheng Zhiyong — he knew nothing.

The next morning Zheng Zhiyong lay in wait again along Zhang Jingyu’s route to work for the family car, and of course came away empty-handed again. Deep in his heart, he finally gave up his futile search.

He couldn’t help thinking: if one day he could take a bank card with several million in cash on it and slap it down in front of Zhang Jingyu, would this wife who’d been with him for twenty years give him one more chance to start over?

Or, if one day he could take a bank card with ten million on it and slap it down in front of her, would she be willing to give him one more chance then?

He couldn’t help thinking…

At this moment Zheng Zhiyong needed, more urgently than ever, a career of his own — a career that was entirely his own — to save his broken, tattered life.

And today was the day he was due in Xi’an to meet the channel company that had taken the seven-hundred-thousand order.

Zheng Zhiyong held out outside the workplace until ten in the morning without catching Zhang Jingyu’s car, then, bitterly disappointed, had no choice but to take a taxi to Daxing Airport and fly to Xi’an.

By the time he arrived it was afternoon. From Xi’an’s airport, after a roundabout journey, he came to a detached four-story building in a technology park. On its roof a large stainless-steel sign stood out boldly: “Xinghuan Times.”

Old Zheng walked into the building — a very impressive little building — and at the front desk a very pretty young woman phoned through to say the visitor had arrived.

Before long a very young-looking, slim man in glasses came out, and when he saw Old Zheng he greeted him with a warm smile and invited him in. “Mr. Zheng, hello!”

He wore a loose light-cyan cashmere sweater with no visible brand but obviously fine quality, equally loose navy jeans, and light-gray Onitsuka Tiger sneakers. The whole man had an air of easy comfort about him.

“Mr. Wu, hello, hello! I never expected you’d be so young in person!”

“Not that young.” The two compared ages and found that Wu You was four years younger than Old Zheng, but right now they looked as if they were ten years apart.

“Let me show you around the company first,” Wu You said.

So the two toured the company. It had four floors, more than a thousand square meters in all, beautifully fitted out from top to bottom; but only on the second floor were there a few people, scattered among the empty desks, and the third and fourth floors, though fully equipped, clearly saw nobody go up most of the time. Wu You’s own office was in a corner of the second floor — a very open office with floor-to-ceiling windows on two sides; sliding open the office door took you straight out onto the second-floor terrace, where a few leisure tables and chairs and a sun umbrella were set out.

“It’s winter, so we don’t use the terrace much. In summer it’s the nicest place in the building,” Wu You said, following Old Zheng’s gaze.

The two traded some basic background. As Wu You told it, the company had been founded eight years ago. It started because a few of his childhood friends in Xi’an had industrial and military business connections, and they wanted to build a fully qualified defense company and do something in the real economy. More importantly, Wu You hoped to do something real in his hometown of Xi’an, where he had grown up until he went to the United States to study. His father had been president of another well-known university in Xi’an and was now retired.

“I’ve just always had this dream of the real economy. Quant trading makes money, but sometimes I feel I’m not contributing anything of value to the world — just making money off an edge in information and algorithms — and that feels a bit unsettling,” Wu You said. His main business was quantitative trading. More than ten years ago he’d finished his university education abroad and worked for a few years at a quant trading firm on Wall Street; as his parents got older, he decided to come home and settle near them, so he came back to China to do quant here. His first pot of gold was money he’d made on Wall Street. “Xi’an’s not actually a good place to do quant, but it’s a hub for the defense industry. My quant team is in Shanghai; our office is right next to the exchange — we’ve run our network cables right into the same building as the exchange. Speed is everything,” Wu You said.

Since coming back, Wu You had multiplied his original assets several dozen times, and having made a name in the field, he had many family offices hoping to hand their money to his firm to manage. “With quant, more capital isn’t necessarily better, so I’m very cautious about taking other people’s money. As a rule it’s mostly my own money and my two partners’. We’ve got lots of math graduates from the world’s top universities, and apart from them it’s basically kids from Tsinghua, Peking, Fudan and Shanghai Jiao Tong. Those kids work mainly to manage our own capital.” Wu You said all this in a very plain tone, without the slightest attempt to dress it up.

Old Zheng said he didn’t really understand quant trading. Wu You said, “Quant is ultra-high-frequency trading — using algorithms to judge where stocks are heading, buying and selling at ultra-high frequency, and making money on the probabilities.”

Wu You said, “But my ideal has always been to build a real company, do something solid in industry. Xi’an has had great opportunities these past few years. I love my hometown, and I want to build something of my own here and put down roots. That’s why four other shareholders and I each put in some money and set up this company. But in the past eight years, apart from collecting a pile of impressive qualifications, its annual sales have only ever been two or three hundred thousand yuan.” Wu You laughed at himself.

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I have a soft spot for Xi’an too. I graduated from Northwestern Polytechnical. Xi’an’s my lucky place.”

Wu You said, “That really is fate. My mother used to work at Northwestern Polytechnical; when I was little I often played on the campus. No wonder Mr. Zheng gets things done so efficiently — a Northwestern Polytechnical high-flier.”

Old Zheng: “You flatter me, Mr. Wu.”

Wu You: “Mr. Zheng, I won’t hide it from you. This time, from negotiating the channel deal to collecting payment to delivering the product, the whole thing went so smoothly, start to finish. I hear the client’s feedback on the test data lately has been ideal. I think you’re genuinely very capable. Before you came, my shareholders and I discussed whether there might be a way to work with you more closely in future. Honestly, if we hadn’t met you, we might have started looking for a chance to sell this shell and call it a day.”

Old Zheng caught this and said to Wu You, “Mr. Wu, I believe everything comes down to fate. Don’t laugh, but right now I’m at the lowest point of my life. These past few months I’ve been through some of the most turbulent things I’ve ever lived through…”

At this Wu You said, “Mr. Zheng, look, it’s dinnertime. Why don’t we talk over a drink?”

So the two left the company together and rode in Wu You’s Mercedes van to a Hunanese restaurant near the company. They ordered fish head with chopped chilies, lotus root with pork ribs, loofah with scrambled egg and stir-fried chili pork, and Wu You had brought along a bottle of Moutai especially. Cups clinked, and after a few cups of baijiu Old Zheng told him the whole story from beginning to end, in every detail: his career, how he had joined Yuanwang, how he had met Diao Kexin, walked away from his marriage with nothing, then started a business with this woman, been dumped without warning, and fallen to having nothing at all — and how, in his despair, he had received Mr. Wu’s invitation to come to Xi’an, and now asked for nothing but the chance to fight with everything he had.

Wu You was deeply shaken by what he heard. He raised his cup. “Mr. Zheng, say no more. This is Heaven setting you a test, putting you through a trial of love! This is one of life’s required courses. Get through this trial — you have to hit the bottom of the valley before you can bounce back, and the harder you fall, the higher you’ll rise!”

These words fired Old Zheng up, and he drained his cup. Wu You went on, “Mr. Zheng, you’ve got the passion and the talent for business — all you’ve lacked is a chance. For a man to make his place in the world, his career is the only thing that matters. Women aren’t that important. Once your career takes off, both of those women will come back to you. Then you’ll have to choose. Who will you pick then?”

Old Zheng smiled bitterly. “I don’t dare think about that now. I just want to get through this low point and get back on track with my career as soon as I can. Women and all that — that’s never going to be my goal again.”

Wu You said, “I’ll be frank with you. Before you came to Xi’an, we shareholders talked it over. If you’re willing, we’ll hand this company over to you to run with full authority. Originally we planned a one-year term with three KPI tiers, each matched to the equity we’d give you after a year. As long as you’re willing to come and build this business, the company currently has over eight million in cash on the books and eight full-time employees, seven of them technical and production staff. You’ll interview and assess each of them yourself, keep the ones who fit, let the ones who don’t fit go, and rebuild your own team. Originally we were going to design performance tiers for you — say, annual sales of two million, five million, ten million — each giving you a corresponding share of equity and dividends after a year. But now, having heard what you’ve been through, I feel there’s really something fated about us meeting at this moment. For the past two years we’ve been looking for a CEO and never found the right person. If we didn’t find one, we were planning to shut down or sell the shell after New Year anyway.”

“Having heard your story today, I think Heaven has sent us the right person — and put us in front of you, too. Personally, I respect your nature as a man: you love deeply and you’re loyal. Loving deeply shows you’re kind at heart; doing everything in your power for your wife and child shows you’re loyal, responsible, and willing to shoulder things.”

“Falling this far is Heaven getting you ready for a great task. As the saying goes, when Heaven is about to lay a great task on a man, it first tries his heart. After this ordeal, your only way out is to throw yourself into building a business. I’m betting you’ll make it.”

Wu You thought for a moment and went on, “Here’s what we’ll do. We won’t even bother assessing you — we’ll give you ten percent of the shares right away and register it immediately. If within a year the business makes a net profit in the millions, the rest of us shareholders will dilute ourselves and give you another twenty percent, so you’ll hold thirty percent in all and become the largest shareholder; from then on, you’ll have the final say in the company, including whether to dilute the shares further and how — your call. From then on we’ll all be your helpers. Tomorrow, give me your ID card; from tomorrow all the company’s finances and control are yours. We trust you a hundred percent and give you full authority, and you’re responsible for your own profit and loss. There’s a furnished dormitory on the fourth floor of this building — a proper room you can live in. The lease on this building has three more years to run, with the rent paid up through to the end. You have one goal: build the business. As the business grows, for any supply-chain financing needed for orders you actually land, we shareholders won’t set a ceiling — borrow as much as you need.”

“But as a shareholder you’ll only get social insurance and a base salary of ten thousand yuan until you land your first order here. After that, as long as the company is profitable each year, you can raise your own salary — you decide, within a reasonable range. Before that first order, it’s good to feel a bit of startup pressure. Can you accept that?”

“Also, about last time’s seven-hundred-thousand channel order: the payment’s been fully settled. After deducting the channel fee and taxes, we’ll transfer the rest into your personal account as a loan for now. If your previous company comes asking for the money, you return it to the company and it pays them; if that company never comes asking for the contract money, we’ll just leave it on the books for the time being. I trust this loan can help you through your money troubles for now.”

Tiangou

Chapter Eleven: Tiangou

“Tiangou” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Western Mountains, Book Three”), on Mount Yin there is a beast shaped like a wildcat with a white head, called the tiangou, the Heavenly Dog; its cry sounds like “liu-liu,” and it can ward off evil. A beast that wards off evil, yet always rushes toward evil. As the saying goes: it hears the call and goes, never asking whether the omen is good or ill.

First thing the next morning Old Zheng went to the office. Gao Fei had already flown back to Changsha early. Old Zheng walked into Diao Kexin’s office, and when she saw him come in, she went over and shut the door.

Old Zheng asked, “The chairman’s gone?”

Diao Kexin: “Flew out first thing.” She tilted her head up at him. “You deleted me?”

Old Zheng said, “Zhang Jingyu knows.”

Diao Kexin said, “Knows it’s me?”

Old Zheng said, “She may not know exactly who, but she knows I’ve got someone.”

Diao Kexin let out a breath. “So what are you going to do now?”

Old Zheng: “Honestly, it was too sudden — like having a bucket of water dumped over my head. I don’t know what to do either.”

Diao Kexin said, “You know what? Right now, this moment, is when I love you most. I want to be with you forever.”

Old Zheng: “I signed all the property over to her.”

Diao Kexin: “You’re getting divorced?”

Old Zheng: “No. I signed an agreement that I’ll leave with nothing if it happens again, and I promised her I’d cut it off with you completely.”

Diao Kexin: “So — are you going to cut it off with me?”

Old Zheng: “I don’t know. I’m in a daze.”

Diao Kexin went to her office window and made sure the blinds were angled so that nothing could be seen, then checked again that the office door was locked. Then she suddenly grabbed Old Zheng’s hand, rose on tiptoe and kissed him, and the kiss tasted to Old Zheng like parting. She said, “Darling, Brother Yong, whether or not you decide to leave me now, I have to tell you: you’ve given me the most beautiful experience of love I could ever have imagined in my life. So from now on, no matter what, I’ll understand you, and I’ll go along with whatever you decide.”

Old Zheng: “You know, for me it’s exactly the same. Everything I could ever imagine of love, all the way to its farthest edges, is you. However far I can go with you, whether or not I can go on with you, I treasure every minute and every second I have with you.”

Diao Kexin began stroking and rubbing his hair and head, and then kissed him again, and Old Zheng kissed her back fiercely. It was June by now, and Diao Kexin was wearing a black one-piece dress. They were standing beside the loveseat in her office; she gave Old Zheng a little push, and he pulled her down onto the sofa with him. Diao Kexin straightened up, lifted her hem, straddled his thighs face to face, and sat straight down. Then her body rose and fell like the waves.

“Even if we have to part tomorrow, today I want to be with you,” Diao Kexin said to him, tears glistening in her eyes.

Old Zheng answered her declaration with an even more fervent kiss.

But what Old Zheng didn’t know was that just the day before, Gao Fei had done the same thing with Diao Kexin, in this same room, on this same sofa, in this same position. Diao Kexin hoped this would cover up her own unease; after all, if Gao Fei found out she now had someone else in her heart, her billion-yuan ambition would be shattered along with it.

What Old Zheng understood even less was that, compared with his self-satisfied idea of love, this woman’s goal was utterly unshakable; nobody could stop her resolve to use her body to climb the social ladder, one rung at a time.

When they were done, they reluctantly put a little distance between their bodies, and Diao Kexin said sadly, “Maybe breaking up now is the right thing. You go back to your family, back to respectable, socially approved happiness, and I can get the billion that ought to be mine.”

Old Zheng pulled up his trousers and calmed down. He sat at one end of the sofa, head propped in his hand, and asked, “If I had twenty million right now, would you go with me?”

Diao Kexin didn’t answer.

Old Zheng said, “So you want to break up with me for good too?”

Diao Kexin said, “We crossed a line. The risk is too big for both of us, more than we can bear. I’m terrified Gao Fei will find us out now. I don’t know what he’d do. It isn’t just about money — it’s your safety too. More than the money, I’m worried about your personal safety.”

Old Zheng said, “You still want to marry him?”

Diao Kexin said, “I’ve waited seven years. My child is almost seven. I need to give her an identity. Gao Fei has to help me register her hukou. She’s starting primary school.”

Old Zheng: “Has he agreed to marry you?”

Diao Kexin said, “He keeps stalling. He says he’ll divorce before Qixi this year and marry me.”

Old Zheng: “If I had money, would you be with me?”

Diao Kexin cried. “I don’t need you to have as much money as him. As long as you love me with your whole heart — once your family’s sorted out and your career’s off the ground, we can do business together and grow it big together. All of Gao Fei’s sales were done by me. I really just want to be with you. Even when I’m with him, there’s no love anymore, just a fixation. You know I have too many things to think about right now. I really can’t decide.”

Old Zheng: “So what do we do from here? Are we still together?”

Diao Kexin: “Brother Yong, I don’t want to separate now — now’s when I love you most. But I’m really scared too. Could we try being ordinary friends for a while — no, best friends? Not cutting contact, just not crossing the line anymore. I still want to talk to you. You’re my only soulmate. If I couldn’t talk to you or see you, I don’t know if I could go on living. But we have to keep it better hidden, and be more careful when we’re together.”

Old Zheng: “You’re still choosing him.”

Diao Kexin: “And so are you choosing her. Can you sort out your family right now?”

Old Zheng shook his head. The two of them talked it over and agreed to use Douban to chat from now on, and together they deleted all their call records and the messages left on their computers.

Then they went on to discuss work. Diao Kexin said, “There’s still a lot at Yuanwang worth hanging on to, and right now Gao Fei’s in his honeymoon period with you. I think you should stay here a while longer and build up your strength. I’ll help connect you with as many of our existing clients as I can.”

Old Zheng said, “Gao Fei says he wants to groom me as CEO. He wants me to fly to Changsha this afternoon to sign the options agreement.”

Diao Kexin: “Isn’t that perfect? You know how many years it takes everyone else to get that? You’ve really won Gao Fei over. Stay a while longer and collect everything that’s coming to you.”

Old Zheng nodded.

For about the next month or two, the two of them kept up fairly frequent secret contact, but they really did stop meeting in private. Diao Kexin stayed in Beijing; Old Zheng spent more than a month in Changsha, helping the company win the bid on another project. Throughout that time, Old Zheng checked in with his wife every morning and reported back every night, video-calling his wife and son each evening, telling them his schedule and his progress at work without being asked. Little by little, the atmosphere between the couple relaxed somewhat.

Back in Beijing, Old Zheng spent a week at the office and didn’t see Diao Kexin once. At home during this time he was especially careful. On Douban their conversations came and went; Diao Kexin hadn’t replied in several days. Old Zheng sent message after message and got no answer. He felt utterly lost.

One day Old Zheng had to go to Handan to see a client. While he was waiting for the train at Beijing South Station, there came Diao Kexin and Gao Fei, walking toward him one behind the other — what a coincidence, running into each other at the station.

Gao Fei greeted Old Zheng first. “Mr. Zheng, fancy running into you here.” Chairman Gao Fei smiled. “Ms. Diao and I are going to Shanghai for an industry summit.”

Diao Kexin nodded to Old Zheng as well. “Hello, Mr. Zheng.”

Old Zheng said, “Hello, Ms. Diao,” and, looking at Gao Fei, “Chairman Gao, I’m going to Handan to handle a delivery job Mr. Zhao Bin assigned.”

Gao Fei said, “I know, I know. Thanks for all your hard work, Mr. Zheng. See you in Changsha next week.”

The three of them went their separate ways.

Old Zheng boarded the train to Handan, and it pulled slowly out. He opened the Douban chat window, and just then a message from Diao Kexin drifted in, eight characters long: “Never forget, and there will be an echo.”

The next afternoon another message came on Douban: “He’s gone back to Changsha. I’m in Shanghai. Come find me.”

The moment he saw it, Old Zheng bought a ticket for the next train to Shanghai.

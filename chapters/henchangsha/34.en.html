Jiao

Chapter Thirty-Four: Jiao

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) Last updated: 2026-11-12 16:39 © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Jiao” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Western Mountains, Book Three”), on Jade Mountain there is a beast shaped like a dog, with a leopard’s spots and horns like an ox’s, called the jiao; its cry is like a barking dog, and when it appears, the land has a great harvest. As the saying goes: a dog that grows horns looks docile, but in truth it’s hard to tame.

Since Old Zheng was still in Chengdu, Xiaoya felt an itch in her heart like eight claws scratching at once, so she bombarded him with WeChat messages. The next evening, over and over, she begged to come to his hotel and see him once more.

Zheng Zhiyong was in his hotel room, just out of the shower, wearing the hotel bathrobe, sitting by the window smoking. He took out his phone: there were a whole lot more unread WeChats from Xiaoya, and the last one was a voice message: “Big brother, you’re at the hotel, right? I’m coming, I’m on my way.”

After thinking it over a long time, he replied, “Okay, come on then.” Then he got dressed, ready to meet Xiaoya in the hotel lobby.

In less than half an hour Xiaoya arrived. The two of them sat down in the hotel’s lobby bar. Xiaoya was plainly dressed, in a light-colored cotton dress, her hair tied back, hardly any makeup on, a little shadow under her eyes. When she saw him she smiled, but the smile wasn’t as easy as the day before; there was a bit of embarrassment in it.

“Big brother, sorry to bother you.”

“Let’s talk here. Do you want something to drink?”

Xiaoya nodded. They each ordered a can of Tsingtao, and the waiter brought two glasses of water first.

Xiaoya held her water glass in both hands, head down, staring at the bit of water in the glass for a long while. Then she looked up and said, “Big brother, I went home yesterday and thought about it all night. I know it’s a bit presumptuous of me to come and say this, but I feel like there’s some kind of special connection between us, and if I didn’t see you once more, I couldn’t let it go.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “That’s true. I’m afraid there may be a bit of fate between us.”

Xiaoya said, “What I came to tell you is, I want to work for you. To be honest I’ve wanted to quit streaming for ages, you know. I’m not cut out for it, and I don’t make any money. Yesterday when we were talking, you said you run a company, in engineering. I wanted to ask whether your company has any position I could do. I studied accounting. I haven’t done it for many years since I graduated, but I can keep books, I can read financial statements. I learn fast, and I’m not afraid of hard work.”

Zheng Zhiyong looked at her and didn’t answer right away. He lit a cigarette and said, “I don’t think so. You’re in Chengdu, and our company’s in Xi’an.”

Xiaoya thought he was finding it awkward and quickly said, “I’m not afraid of changing cities. I’ve got a kid to raise — I can’t be without a job, drifting along like this forever.”

The beer came. Zheng Zhiyong opened a can, poured a glass and handed it to Xiaoya, and said, “Xiaoya, I understand you. But my company isn’t in Chengdu, it’s in Xi’an. If you came, you’d have to leave Chengdu, leave your parents, leave your child. And you’d have to rent a place, right? Your cost of living would go up, and what you’d take home wouldn’t necessarily be more than now.”

Xiaoya was taken aback, then said, “My mum can look after the baby for now. She’s the one looking after her anyway.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Your child’s only two.”

Xiaoya stopped talking.

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Xiaoya, it’s not that I don’t want to help you. My company really is short of people right now. But I can’t let you leave your child behind in Chengdu for the sake of a job. What matters most for you right now isn’t work, it’s your child. She’s two. If you leave her, what’s she supposed to do?”

Xiaoya hung her head, her fingers squeezing the glass so hard her knuckles went white. After a while she looked up, her eyes a little red, and said, “Big brother, but I feel like meeting you was somehow meant to be. I won’t lie to you — I couldn’t sleep all last night. It’s the first time I’ve met you, but it’s like I’ve known you for decades. I trust you, I want to follow you. I want to find something proper to do, too. I want my daughter to have a better life later on.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Xiaoya, listen to me. Accountants — our company isn’t short of those.” Old Zheng knew that the company’s finance and accounting people were all Wu You’s people now. He went on, “In your situation, the steadiest thing is to find a job locally in Chengdu, even if the pay’s a bit lower, so you can go home every day and keep an eye on your child. If you like, I can ask around and see if any companies I know in Chengdu need people. But my company — you can’t go there. You wouldn’t know the place or anyone in it, and with your child not by your side, you’d be miserable.”

Xiaoya looked at him for a good while. She said, “Big brother, have you noticed I’ve got a pretty lively personality? I could do sales, you know.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “That’s not it. I just think you have to be a mother first. That’s your most important job right now.”

Xiaoya lowered her head, and the tears fell. She didn’t cry out loud; the tears just dropped one by one into the glass she was holding. Zheng Zhiyong stood up and went over beside her, meaning to pat her shoulder; his hand came up, then went down again. He sat down beside her and said nothing.

After a while Xiaoya wiped her eyes herself, looked up and forced a smile. “Big brother, I’m sorry. I lost it.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “It’s all right.”

Xiaoya said, “You’re right. I can’t leave my child behind. I’ll think of something else.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “When I get back I’ll ask around for you, see if there’s a suitable position here in Chengdu. If there is, I’ll let you know.”

Xiaoya said, “Thank you, big brother.”

She finished the rest of her beer, then stood up and said, “I’ll go, then. You’re leaving tomorrow — I won’t see you off.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Let me call a car to take you home.”

Xiaoya said, “No need. I’ll just get a cab myself.”

Old Zheng looked up the address where he’d dropped her the day before and booked a car. It arrived very quickly.

She walked to the door, turned back and looked at him. There was something hard to name in that look — something like disappointment, and something like resignation. She said, “Big brother, you’re a good man.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I’m not.” Xiaoya smiled, went out of the hotel lobby and got into the car.

Zheng Zhiyong watched her get into the DiDi he’d called; she got in and the car drove off. Then he went outside the hotel, to the trash can in the smoking area, lit a cigarette and looked at the night.

That night he lay alone in the hotel bed, tossing and turning, unable to sleep. He thought of the look in Xiaoya’s eyes when she said “You’re a good man.” He knew he wasn’t. He wasn’t a good man; he just didn’t dare. He didn’t dare pull a twenty-nine-year-old woman with a two-year-old child into his own whole mess. He wasn’t even standing firmly on his own feet yet — what did he have to catch anyone else with? And the two of them had only just met for the first time. Xiaoya had said it was like she’d known him for decades, and it seemed he had that feeling too.

Early the next morning he slung his backpack over his shoulder, checked out and went to the airport.

Back in Xi’an, life went back onto its old track.

Phase one of the Sixth Academy project was delivered. Zhang Hailu had kept watch over it for two months — from production scheduling to testing to packing to shipping, clearing one checkpoint after another. Zheng Zhiyong made several trips to the Sixth Academy to work through acceptance with the project office. Phase one passed acceptance, and under the contract the Sixth Academy was due to pay the second installment.

But the money just wouldn’t come.

Zheng Zhiyong called Supervisor Zhou, and Supervisor Zhou said “it’s going through the process.” He waited a month and called again, and Supervisor Zhou said “it’s with finance now.” He waited another month and called again, and Supervisor Zhou’s tone started to dodge: “Mr. Zheng, please wait a little longer. This isn’t something I can decide.”

Zheng Zhiyong hung up, sat in his office and smoked a cigarette.

He’d worked on defense projects for so many years that he knew this industry’s rules. Defense payment terms don’t follow the contract; they follow “the process.” What step the process has reached, when it’ll finish — nobody tells you. What a small company doing defense work fears most is exactly this: payment terms that drag on six months, a year, even two years. How many small companies had been dragged to death just like this.

He called Zhang Hailu in and said, “The Sixth Academy’s phase-two payment is stuck.”

Zhang Hailu said, “So when can they pay?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “No idea. Supervisor Zhou can’t say.”

Zhang Hailu said, “Then how long can we hold out on what’s on the books?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I’ve worked it out. Three months. After three months, we can’t make payroll.”

Zhang Hailu was silent for a while. The two of them sat in the office smoking, and neither said anything.

Half a cigarette in, Zhang Hailu said, “I’m going out to make a call,” and when he came back he said, “Mr. Zheng, I asked around. Several suppliers’ payments at the Sixth Academy are stuck lately, not just ours. Apparently the academy is adjusting its budget, and this year’s money is being tilted toward a few key projects. Supporting work like ours has to get in line behind them.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Adjusting the budget — when will they be done adjusting it?”

Zhang Hailu said, “Nobody knows.”

Zheng Zhiyong stubbed out his cigarette and said, “So we just wait?”

Zhang Hailu said, “We wait. Or else, in these three months, we look for some other income? Otherwise, when the three months are up and we can’t make payroll, what do we do about the people?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “In Dongguan — does Wang Renhou have any new orders whose money we could collect?”

Zhang Hailu said, “I’ll ask.” He turned to go, and just as he reached the door he looked back and said, “Mr. Zheng, do you know what’s been going on at Yuanwang lately?”

Old Zheng shook his head. Zhang Hailu said, matter-of-factly, “Yuanwang has started blowing up.”

The first to blow was the supplier side. Yuanwang owed an outsourced machining plant a few million in processing fees and had dragged it out for more than a year. The plant couldn’t hold out and sued Yuanwang. Once the court summons was served on Yuanwang, word spread through the circle, and right after that a second and a third supplier sued too. Then came the banks. On the mortgage loan on the Yuanwang industrial park, Yuanwang had missed the monthly interest three months in a row, and the bank had started proceedings.

After that came the investment bet. The seven-year term on that 200-million capital increase from years ago had come due; there was no hope of an IPO, and the buyback clause kicked in automatically. Two hundred million had become four hundred million, and the investors had started coming after Gao Fei for the money. Arbitration had already begun, with the hearing set for next month. Whatever the outcome, Gao Fei couldn’t come up with it.

Zheng Zhiyong pressed him: “How far has it gone?”

Zhang Hailu said, “Suppliers suing, banks pulling loans, the bet chasing the debt. Gao Fei’s mortgaged everything that can be mortgaged and borrowed everything that can be borrowed, and he still can’t fill the hole. I hear he’s going around looking for someone to take over — wants to sell part of Yuanwang’s equity for cash. But at a moment like this, who’d dare take it on?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “And Sister Yan’er?”

Zhang Hailu said, “Sister Yan’er cut herself clean away long ago. I hear she’s left Xiangcheng and gone to Thailand. She’s not even in the country anymore. Yuanwang’s gone under and she hasn’t got a scratch. Gao Fei’s carrying it all on his own now — or maybe Ms. Diao’s carrying it with him.”

Zheng Zhiyong said nothing. He picked up his teacup and drank.

Seeing that Old Zheng didn’t want to talk, Zhang Hailu said, “I’ll head out, then.”

Zheng Zhiyong waved a hand.

A few days later the phase-two materials went in to the Sixth Academy for review. Zheng Zhiyong, thinking he’d find a leader and get a sense of things about the payments and the terms, made another trip to Beijing himself, to the Sixth Academy.

It was July, and the Beijing sun was vicious, scorching the ground white. He got out of the taxi with his backpack and walked toward the main building. Halfway there, he suddenly saw, at the far end of the parking lot, a red Audi parked.

His heart moved, and his steps slowed.

That car — he knew it all too well. In that car he and Diao Kexin had kissed for the first time, made love for the first time. On days beyond counting she’d sat in the passenger seat holding his hand, and whenever he changed gear she’d pout and say, “You’re not allowed to let go.” That car he’d later followed from a distance, watching it drive into Diao Kexin’s compound. That car carried too many memories.

And now that car was parked there, which meant Diao Kexin was there.

He stood in the middle of the parking lot looking at the red Audi. After a while the car door opened, and a woman got out of the driver’s seat.

It was Diao Kexin.

She was wearing a loose dark dress and a pair of camel-colored lambskin flats. She got out of the car and turned around, and Zheng Zhiyong saw her belly.

It was very big, too big to hide. She’d filled out all over since he’d last seen her at the Sixth Academy’s door; her face was round, her belly pushed out the dress — she was clearly in the late stage of pregnancy. With one hand on the car door and the other propped against the small of her back, she straightened up, a little clumsily.

She looked up and saw Zheng Zhiyong. She obviously hadn’t expected to run into him here, much less looking like this, big with child, and a flicker of shock crossed her face.

The two of them stood a dozen meters apart across the parking lot, and neither of them moved.

After a while Diao Kexin walked slowly over and spoke first. Her voice was still that voice, soft, only with a bit more tiredness in it than before, and a little shyness, as it were. She said, “Brother Yong.”

Zheng Zhiyong walked up to her and stopped. He looked down at her belly, then up at her face. She wore no makeup; her skin wasn’t as good as it used to be, and there were fine lines at the corners of her eyes. She looked at him and smiled, a very faint smile, as if she couldn’t quite manage one.

Zheng Zhiyong said, “You’re pregnant.”

Diao Kexin said, “Mm. Nearly eight months.” Counting back, then, the first time he’d sensed she was pregnant, when she’d looked puffy, she really had been pregnant. So what had it been, the second time he saw her looking so thin? Old Zheng’s heart was churning like rivers and seas.

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Gao Fei’s?”

Diao Kexin said, “Gao Fei’s.”

Zheng Zhiyong said nothing. After a pause he said, “At the bid, you looked so thin. I couldn’t tell you were pregnant at all.”

Diao Kexin said, “Back then the morning sickness was so bad I didn’t know which way was up. I couldn’t eat, so I got really thin.”

Zheng Zhiyong thought back. That day, when he’d seen her, her belly had been flat; there’d been nothing to see. And only three months had passed. What Old Zheng didn’t know was that during that time Diao Kexin had needed to work with Old Wu on Yuanwang’s project, and given the years of ambiguity between the two of them, she couldn’t let the slightest word of her pregnancy get out at the academy. So, just as with her first pregnancy at Yuanwang, she’d used a belly band to hide her bump.

Diao Kexin said, “Brother Yong, I reckon I’ll be done in half an hour. Are you free after? Let’s drive somewhere and sit down for a bit. There’s something I’d like to say to you.”

Zheng Zhiyong looked at her, then at the car behind her. He said, “In your condition, you’re still driving?”

Diao Kexin said, “No choice. Let the driver go, to save a bit of money. So — will you come?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “All right. But I’ve got business inside too. You may have to wait for me a bit.”

An hour later, Old Zheng still came out before Diao Kexin. When Diao Kexin came out, Old Zheng drove her car, and the two of them found a quiet restaurant and sat in a corner. Diao Kexin sat down and first asked for a glass of warm water, drank a couple of mouthfuls, and only then slowly came round. Zheng Zhiyong sat across from her, watching her. He remembered a few years ago, in Changsha too, eating together too, she’d sat across from him, chin in her hand, staring at him with hot, eager eyes, saying, “Brother Yong, you’re just so good-looking.” Back then she was thirty-five, plump and round, with a dimple when she smiled. Now she sat across from him big-bellied, bare-faced, with fine lines at the corners of her eyes, like an ordinary, tired woman about to give birth.

The food came, and Diao Kexin hardly touched it. She said, “Brother Yong, you’ve heard about Yuanwang, haven’t you?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I’ve heard a bit.”

Diao Kexin said, “It’s probably even worse than what people are saying out there. Suppliers suing, banks pulling loans, investors chasing the bet. Gao Fei’s mortgaged everything that can be mortgaged and borrowed everything that can be borrowed, and he still can’t fill the hole. Yuanwang Tower may not be saved.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “And you?”

Diao Kexin looked down at her belly and said, “Marrying Gao Fei — I feel like he and Wang Yan schemed against me together. Not long after we married I found out I was pregnant. I thought it’d be great if I had a son this time — Gao Fei would give me more money. People from Hunan actually favor boys too, you know? I only had a daughter, so I still hoped I could give him a son. I got pregnant. But once I was pregnant, I found out he had no money in his hands. All the money, all of it, went to Sister Yan’er. When I asked him, he fought with me — said I was the one who forced him to divorce, so what was he supposed to do? The company’s money has all been poured into the hole. Right now he can’t even make next month’s payroll.”

Here she stopped, picked up her glass and drank a mouthful.

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I hear your brother and his wife joined the company too?”

Diao Kexin said, “Most of the people are about gone. Their salaries are low, and they still hold a few things together; they’ve got nowhere else to go anyway. When Yuanwang goes under, they’ll find other jobs. Either way, they’ve definitely earned more than they used to. I haven’t done wrong by them.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Do you regret it?”

Diao Kexin looked at him for a good while. She said, “Brother Yong, all I can say is that I know now you were the one who treated me best, and I hurt you. I’ve come to where I am today one step at a time. I chose every step, and at every step I knew what I was doing. I chose Gao Fei, chose this child, chose this road. Now the road’s come to an end, and I can’t regret it. I just never thought it would turn out like this.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Never thought what?”

Diao Kexin said, “I never thought Gao Fei would scheme against me together with Sister Yan’er. He had me marry him just so I’d keep doing sales for him, bring in the payments, take the fall for him. He never meant to give me much money. I was stupid too, back then — I thought he loved me and would give me all the money. I thought at least he’d be able to save Yuanwang. And in the end he couldn’t save anything. He can’t even save himself.”

When she said this, there was no hatred in her tone, no resentment either, just a very flat, resigned way of speaking — like someone who had thought a thing through from beginning to end and, having thought it through, stopped struggling.

Zheng Zhiyong said, “So what are you planning to do now?”

Diao Kexin said, “Have the baby first. After it’s born, we’ll see. With Yuanwang, there’s nothing more I can do to help. What I can do is look after my brother and his wife — as long as the company’s still standing, keep paying their salaries. As for Gao Fei, he’ll probably be blacklisted as a defaulter in a few months.”

Here she raised her head, looked at Zheng Zhiyong, and said, “Brother Yong, I’m not telling you all this so you’ll pity me. I want to tell you that back then, when you hated me, you were right to. I used you as a ladder and threw you away when I’d climbed it. Someone like me, ending up where I am today — I deserve it.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I didn’t hate you.”

Diao Kexin smiled. “You did.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I did once. Not now.”

Diao Kexin said, “Why?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Because you’re sitting here today, big with child, telling me all this — the fact that you can tell me all this, I suddenly don’t hate you anymore.”

Diao Kexin looked at him for a good while. Then she smiled, very faintly, so faintly it was almost invisible. She said, “Brother Yong, you mean you don’t hate me right at this moment, is that it?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Right. Just today. I don’t hate you.”

The two of them were silent for a while. The air conditioner in the room hummed, and outside the window was the Beijing sky in July, so hot it had gone white.

Diao Kexin spoke first. She said, “Brother Yong, I hear you’re doing well on that Sixth Academy project. Do it well. Don’t go down Yuanwang’s road.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “It’s not easy either. The payments aren’t coming.”

Diao Kexin said, “Payments are always like that, they keep dragging. But they’ll definitely pay by the end of the year. You can rest easy on that.”

The two of them ate a little more, and after a while Diao Kexin said, “Brother Yong, I should go. I have to go to the hospital this afternoon for a prenatal checkup.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Let me drive you there.”

Diao Kexin said, “No need. I’ll drive myself.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “In your condition, what are you doing driving? Let me take you.”

Diao Kexin glanced at him and didn’t argue anymore.

In that red Audi, Zheng Zhiyong got into the driver’s seat. She sat in the passenger seat, and suddenly turned her head to look at Old Zheng and said, “Brother Yong, even if you take me to the hospital, I’ll still have to drive myself back afterward, won’t I?”

Zheng Zhiyong didn’t answer.

When he got out of the car, Zheng Zhiyong said, “Take care.”

Diao Kexin said, “You too.”

Zheng Zhiyong stood in the parking lot and watched Diao Kexin, big-bellied, walk into the hospital’s outpatient building, and stood there a while. The summer sun beat down on him, burning. He remembered a year ago, in a parking lot, watching this car from a distance as it drove into Diao Kexin’s compound. Back then he’d hated her, hated her through gritted teeth. Now, looking at her back with a sense of having seen it all before, he felt nothing at all.

He didn’t hate her. He didn’t pity her either. There was none of the thrill of revenge at last; what there was, surprisingly, was only calm.

He took out his phone and called Zhang Hailu.

“Hailu, the Sixth Academy’s phase-two payment — let’s not chase it. Chasing it won’t help; when the time comes they’ll pay. And Dongguan — did you ask about Wang Renhou’s orders? Oh, okay, then I’ll ask him myself. In these three months, we’ve got to find the money.”

He hung up and walked toward the hospital gate. At the gate he looked back once more at the parking lot, at that red Audi.

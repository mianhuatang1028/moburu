Zhuyan

Chapter Fifteen: Zhuyan

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Zhuyan” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Western Mountains, Book Two”), on Mount Xiaoci there is a beast shaped like an ape, with a white head and red feet; when it appears, war will follow. As the saying goes: white head, red feet — war begins with words.

Diao Kexin flew back to Beijing from Changsha the night before the Mid-Autumn Festival. Her negotiation with Gao Fei hadn’t succeeded. Her expression was mild and showed no great ripple of emotion, as if this result had long been within her expectations. She told Zheng Zhiyong, “I’ve formally told Gao Fei we’re breaking up — and the kind where there’s no going back.”

Zheng Zhiyong asked, “And he agreed?”

Diao Kexin said, “He has no choice. I’m set on ending it.”

On the evening of the Mid-Autumn Festival, Old Zheng, Diao Kexin, her daughter and the nanny had dinner together at a Cantonese restaurant a few kilometers from home. To outsiders they looked like a normal, warm family of four. When the little girl got to the restaurant she saw the aquarium by the entrance, full of fish, crabs and shrimp, and halfway through the meal she ran off to look at it. Diao Kexin and the nanny kept eating; no one got up to keep an eye on the child. Zheng Zhiyong asked, “Shouldn’t someone go watch her?” Diao Kexin said, “She’s fine, don’t worry about her. She’s always like this — she just likes looking at fish.”

Zheng Zhiyong stood up and went after her. He hurried over to the aquarium by the restaurant entrance, where the little girl had her face pressed to the glass, looking at the big crabs inside; the glass was scratched and cloudy, and from that angle everything looked blurry. Old Zheng picked her up so she could look down into the tank from above, where you could clearly see the king crabs and lobsters inside, opening and closing their big claws. After this time together, the little girl was no longer afraid of this strange uncle; she called him Uncle Zheng and was happy to be held by him. When they were out and she’d tired herself out playing, Zheng Zhiyong would carry her home asleep and lay her gently on her bed.

Every time that happened his heart was a jumble of feelings. His wife, all those years, whenever they took their son out to eat, hardly ever took her eyes or hands off him; often the boy would run off and his wife would chase after to watch him, and she often never got to finish a whole meal. Back then it was usually Zheng Zhiyong who got to stay at the table and finish eating. His wife had guarded their home and their child devotedly, in her own way.

But the little girl he held now was not Zheng Zhiyong’s child. Holding this soft, sweet little girl, he couldn’t help missing his own son. Today was the Mid-Autumn Festival — where was his son right now? Was he out to dinner with his mother and grandparents? Would he think of his father at all?

The little girl was tired out. Old Zheng carried her back to the table. He couldn’t help opening Zhang Jingyu’s WeChat for a look; since the day of the divorce, Zhang Jingyu hadn’t said a single word to him. He opened his son’s WeChat and sent a message: “Zheng Ruixi, how are you spending the holiday?”

Zheng Ruixi didn’t answer. Old Zheng sent him a hundred-yuan red packet, and after a while it was tapped and claimed.

Old Zheng sent another message: “Son, how are you? Can you say something to Dad?”

Still Zheng Ruixi didn’t answer a single word. Old Zheng sent another hundred-yuan red packet, and once again it was quickly tapped and claimed.

Old Zheng’s heart was both thrilled and crushed. Thrilled, because the little hand that tapped the screen to claim the packet at the other end was the little hand of his own son, Zheng Ruixi, which meant that at this moment, in some sense, the two of them were connected again. Crushed, because ever since he’d left home a few months ago, he and his son had not seen each other once or exchanged a single word. As a father, how could he not miss this little heart, this little treasure, whom he’d held in his palm and raised bit by bit?

After dinner Old Zheng paid the bill and drove Diao Kexin, her daughter and the nanny home in Diao Kexin’s Audi. That night, in Diao Kexin’s bedroom, she took out two simple, plain rings of 925 silver, one big and one small; she put the big one on Zheng Zhiyong’s ring finger and the small one on her own.

“This is my Mid-Autumn gift to you,” Diao Kexin said to him.

She held Zheng Zhiyong’s hand and took a photo with her phone of their two hands clasped together, wearing the matching rings, and she even posted it on her WeChat Moments with the caption: “Full moon, family reunited.”

Zheng Zhiyong had been worried all along that as long as Diao Kexin hadn’t broken up with Gao Fei, he might be kicked aside at any moment. He had been living in that trembling fear. But now not only had she broken up with Gao Fei for good, she had announced on Moments that she had a boyfriend. Although nobody in the world knew that he, Zheng Zhiyong, was the man destined for Diao Kexin, he still rejoiced in his heart: it meant he had truly conquered this woman — she was actually willing to give up a boyfriend worth a billion and go out into the world with him, a man with nothing, to fight for their future. A man with nothing had beaten a billionaire tycoon in love, and his self-confidence as a man shot through the roof. He was so excited that he picked Diao Kexin up and threw her on the bed. That night they slept in her bedroom — or, to be precise, they hardly slept at all.

When he got up in the morning, Zheng Zhiyong opened Diao Kexin’s Moments and saw that some mutual acquaintances had liked her post — including Gao Fei. That like, glaring before Zheng Zhiyong’s eyes, seemed to prove precisely that Diao Kexin had broken up with Gao Fei firmly, resolutely, utterly, and once again he was carried away by the joy of victory.

But think about it rationally, brother: Gao Fei liked it precisely because they hadn’t broken up! It was because this man didn’t take it seriously that he could so openly hit like! After Diao Kexin’s Mid-Autumn ultimatum failed and Gao Fei still hadn’t divorced, she had used that as an excuse to pour scorn on him all over again. She had said to Gao Fei, “If you still won’t divorce, I’ll pick some man off the street and have your child call him Daddy — then we’ll see whether you care.” So when Gao Fei saw the photo on Moments, he’d assume that Diao Kexin, to make him angry, had held her brother’s hand — or even clasped her own two hands together — and staged a picture just for him; and Diao Kexin knew perfectly well that her photo would have exactly that effect. Gao Fei would never take it as Diao Kexin really announcing a boyfriend, but as a little game, a little trick for venting her little moods and little jealousies at him; so, far from being angry, he was secretly pleased, and smugly tossed back a like.

Whereas if he had really believed that his woman had broken up with him the day before and announced the very next day that she loved someone else, all this man would have felt was rage and humiliation; forget a like — he’d long since have been on the phone, furious, tearing into her.

But Zheng Zhiyong, on his side, couldn’t see through or think through this simple logic. Seeing himself publicly acknowledged at last, he would only love Diao Kexin all the more devotedly, and pour into her cause all his energy, his skill, his effort and his time.

Diao Kexin, oh, Diao Kexin — one stone, two birds; what a masterful move. Seen this way, Diao Kexin was probably the highest-ranked player in the world at the “green tea” game — the sweet-faced, innocent-looking schemer — and almost no one could top her. Li Fulang, Gao Fei, Zheng Zhiyong — every one of them toyed with in the palm of her hand.

In the weeks after the Mid-Autumn Festival, Diao Kexin’s company received two advance payments, of 210,000 and 450,000 yuan. Diao Kexin and Zheng Zhiyong were thrilled, and riding that tailwind the two of them, with her brother and sister-in-law, rented a small office together. It was tiny — no room for any workstations besides the boss’s desk — but it had a meeting table and a whiteboard wall, and they added an imposing tea table as well. Diao Kexin even thoughtfully put a small fish tank on the boss’s desk, with a dozen or so red fish fry, each the size of a grain of rice, swimming around in it.

Diao Kexin said to Zheng Zhiyong, “Our business is picking up. Hurry up and quit. If Gao Fei ever finds out my boyfriend is you, I’ll be terrified for your safety.” Watching the two of them’s company grow clearer by the day, and the follow-up work pile higher, Zheng Zhiyong also felt that resigning from Yuanwang couldn’t wait any longer.

A few days later Zheng Zhiyong went to Changsha on business. First he went to see Zhao Bin, to find out whether there was any work he could help with. As it happened, results had just come back on the bid from last time: they’d won it. Old Zheng’s two weeks of all-nighters had paid off. Zhao Bin said with feeling, “Mr. Zheng, I truly admire you. Last time you slogged here for two weeks, and you landed us another big contract. I’m really, truly in awe of your technical ability. Tonight, just the two of us, let’s go out for a few drinks and celebrate.” Old Zheng patted Zhao Bin on the back. “Mr. Zhao, we’re brothers fighting the same war — it’s everybody’s hard work that did it. I really do have plans tonight. Let’s find another day.”

Then he dropped in on Zhao Bin’s key people, who happened to be discussing a problem, and he joined in for quite a while. He waited until nearly the end of the day, then walked to Sister Yan’er’s office, knocked lightly on the door and went in.

“Oh, Mr. Zheng’s here,” said Sister Yan’er, seeing him come in. She quickly stood up from behind her desk, came around to meet him and showed him to the sofa in her office. “Some tea?” she asked.

“No thanks, Sister Yan’er,” Old Zheng said, hesitating. “There’s something I want to tell you.”

“What is it?” Sister Yan’er asked.

Old Zheng glanced at the door. It was wide open, and the nearest desk was fairly far away. With no one passing just then, he said, “Sister Yan’er, I’m thinking of resigning.”

Sister Yan’er’s face changed slightly. “Mr. Zheng, how could you bring up resigning? Look, you’ve been here less than a year and we’re especially happy with your work. Last time Mr. Gao even told me he’s grooming you as his successor for CEO. We’re both past fifty, and you’re in your thirties — not even forty yet, right? We both think you could really spread your wings at Yuanwang.”

Old Zheng: “Sister Yan’er, I’m truly grateful for your and the chairman’s trust and support, but there’s something going on in my family…”

Sister Yan’er said, “Something going on at home doesn’t mean you have to resign. What’s happened? Whatever the company can do to help, just say so.”

By now Sister Yan’er had a pretty good idea why Old Zheng was resigning, but she didn’t know he was already divorced. She guessed that after his wife’s scene at the office, she had gone on to demand that he stop working at the company.

Old Zheng: “Oh, private family matters. They’ll take a while to sort out. The company doesn’t need to help with anything — I can handle it myself.”

Sister Yan’er pretended to know nothing. “Is someone at home ill, or has something happened? Whatever the company can help with, we will. Just say the word. We really need you to keep working at Yuanwang — you’re doing such good work now, Old Gao and I can’t do without you.”

Old Zheng: “I really feel I’ve let you and the chairman down.”

Sister Yan’er asked, “Have you told Old Gao about this?”

Old Zheng said, “Not yet. You’re the head of HR, so I thought I’d let you know first.” In fact, ever since Old Zheng had been with Diao Kexin, he’d felt uneasy whenever he saw Gao Fei, and about resigning he felt even guiltier, so this time he went to Sister Yan’er first.

Sister Yan’er had long since quietly counted Old Zheng as a brother in her own trench. She knew Old Zheng and Diao Kexin had become a pair — and wasn’t that just perfect, someone breaking up Old Gao and his little lover for her? So she truly didn’t want Zheng Zhiyong to leave. Sister Yan’er said, “If you haven’t told him, then don’t bring it up. Whatever it is, it isn’t worth resigning over.”

Sister Yan’er went to the door, closed it, came back and sat down by the sofa. She said to Old Zheng, “I have a pretty good idea why you want to leave. Don’t worry — Old Gao doesn’t know about it, and he won’t in future either. You really are the kind of talent Yuanwang needs. Just keep working here steadily; as long as you do your job well, you don’t need to worry about anything else.” At that moment Sister Yan’er was also thinking that Zheng Zhiyong really was someone special: in less than a year at the company he’d solved several problems they’d been stuck on for years, had really shouldered several big bids, worked without complaint and had a good reputation; if he truly left, the company would be losing one of its best generals.

Old Zheng was startled. Did those words mean Sister Yan’er also knew about him and Little Ke? But on second thought, the enemy of my enemy is my friend; perhaps Sister Yan’er wouldn’t hold it against him, perhaps she even saw him as her chance at deliverance. So he grew bolder and said, “It’s true there’s been some upheaval in my marriage. Ever since I started this job I’ve been on the road constantly and never home. If I don’t resign, my wife won’t stand for it.” Never being home was, in fact, the truth: in the past few months, when he’d come to Changsha to rush projects along, he’d spent weeks at a stretch working overtime in the office, often sleeping in a sleeping bag and not going back to his hotel — everyone knew that perfectly well. And it confirmed Sister Yan’er’s guess: his wife must have been unwilling and was forcing him to resign.

“Would you like me to have a word with your family? Your future here really is excellent. Just a few days ago Old Gao and I were talking about granting you more options — up to one percent — and on top of that, from now on, one percent of sales on every project you’re involved in, as project commission. This company really has been looking for a CEO for the past few years, and we both think you’re up to it. You know the business is developing very promisingly, and in this sector, an IPO in the next few years isn’t out of the question. If you became CEO of a listed company — that’s a pretty rare opportunity too, isn’t it?”

Gao Fei really had been looking for someone to take over as CEO for the past few years. He had even told Diao Kexin that if he could find a CEO and drop the burden of running the company on him, he’d take Diao Kexin to settle in Yunnan and live an idyllic life far from the world, and take good care of her and the child. What he never realized was that Diao Kexin didn’t want an idyllic paradise. All she wanted was to be the chairman’s wife.

Old Zheng said, “Talking to my family probably won’t help. Things at home are already a mess. I’ve only got this one road now. There’s no other way.”

Sister Yan’er figured that if Old Zheng left, he’d most likely go back to his family and break it off with this little lover; and the company would truly be losing a heavyweight who could handle both the business and the technology, the kind you don’t come across in years. She couldn’t help sighing. “Oh, Mr. Zheng, think it over some more. Don’t resign yet — take some leave, stay home for a while, and come back once you’ve sorted things out.” In her heart she was thinking: if anyone has to leave, it shouldn’t be Zheng Zhiyong. The one who should leave is that Diao Kexin. If the company couldn’t keep such a good talent because of her, that would hurt the company.

But she couldn’t tell Old Gao right away about Zheng Zhiyong and the little lover either. With his fiery temper, his headstrong, me-first way of thinking, if he knew, he’d fire Zheng Zhiyong on the spot, maybe even get so angry he’d hit someone. And as for that little lover’s tricks — she’d been on the receiving end of them for years. Even if the girl really had someone else, a few sweet words from her and one night in bed and Old Gao not only wouldn’t mind, his male competitive streak might even kick in and he’d be more hooked on his little lover than ever. Thinking it over, she couldn’t tell him for now; it had to stay a secret.

At that thought Sister Yan’er felt she was in a bind too, and sighed again.

“Oh, Mr. Zheng, can’t you stay?”

Old Zheng felt in a bind as well. Sister Yan’er’s offer couldn’t be called anything but tempting. One percent meant equity worth fifteen million; one percent commission on every project meant his yearly income would go from hundreds of thousands into the millions. More tempting still was the CEO job at a twelve-year-old company well known in its industry: once it listed someday, he would become one of the industry’s famous gold-medal hired hands. For so many people an opportunity like that is something you can hope for but never go looking for — and all he had to do now was nod, and it was his.

But over the past few months Diao Kexin had thoroughly brainwashed him. His life now had only one mission: to start his own business, build his own company, and not only grow revenue and make big money but surpass Yuanwang — take Yuanwang’s clients, and take the woman Gao Fei loved.

At that thought, Old Zheng shook his head with difficulty.

That evening Sister Yan’er went home and told Gao Fei that Old Zheng wanted to resign. Early the next morning Gao Fei saw Old Zheng in his office and, as expected, offered him one percent of the equity plus one percent commission all over again, along with a reasonable stretch of leave, as long as it took for Old Zheng to sort out his family affairs. Gao Fei even felt a little guilty, because, working at a distance, he’d come away with the impression of Zheng Zhiyong working desperately at the office all day and sleeping in a sleeping bag, and he hadn’t imagined such hard work would even damage his family life. At that thought, Gao Fei said, “How about the company pays for you to take your wife on a vacation?”

Old Zheng’s heart was a battlefield of turmoil, fear and unease; in the end he hardened himself and said, “Chairman, I’m truly grateful for everything you and Ms. Yan’er have done for me, all your appreciation and support. If I can get through this stretch, I’ll meet you again out in the world someday, and then I’ll come back and repay you for seeing what I was worth.”

And so, after a month’s handover, Old Zheng completed his resignation from Yuanwang.

Chapter Six: Gu

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Gu” — The Classic of Mountains and Seas speaks of things that, eaten, ward off gu. Gu is what strikes a person without their knowing, and breaks out only after many days. As the saying goes: struck by gu, you feel nothing; by the time you feel it, it is deep.

At Spring Festival Old Zheng went with Zhang Jingyu back to her hometown in Anhui for the New Year. As they strolled along a quiet canal in the little city, Old Zheng suddenly said, “I’ve got another job opportunity.”

Zhang Jingyu immediately rolled her eyes at him. “Oh, give it a rest. You’ve been at this company how many months? You’re doing well — don’t switch!”

Old Zheng said, “Aw, honey, hear me out. First, this company’s revenue is on par with Hanhai’s, and they’re at roughly the same stage, but the position they’re offering me is high — COO, reporting directly to the chairman. At Hanhai, apart from meeting the boss when I joined, all I ever see is the sales director and the VP. The boss is too far away. Future promotions, raises, options — all too remote.”

Zhang Jingyu heard him out sulkily. “Why do you want to change jobs again? Can’t you just stay at one company a bit longer? What if you go over and they decide you’re not a fit — are you going to look for a job all over again?”

Old Zheng said, “It won’t come to that. I should be able to do well at this company. They need my help with technology and operations, and the boss really rates me. If I go, there’s a twenty percent raise too.”

Zhang Jingyu said nothing, shot him a look, and quickened her pace.

Old Zheng kept close behind her and went on, “I’ll talk to them some more. Anyway, after New Year I’ll collect this year’s bonus first. There’s plenty of time to think it over.”

When spring came after the New Year, Old Zheng collected his year-end bonus from Hanhai Chenxing. Although he had worked there only a little over four months, the company still paid him a full year’s bonus to show how satisfied it was with his work. Yet two weeks after the bonus landed, Old Zheng resigned without warning. The sales director and the company’s vice president both sincerely tried to keep him, even proposing a concrete number of options, hoping he would stay. But Zheng Zhiyong’s mind was made up; this time the COO position at Yuanwang felt like the summons of the god of fate, and he felt that if he missed it, who knew how long he’d have to wait for another.

Or perhaps, somewhere deeper in his heart, he could see that this time, besides the god of fate, someone else was beckoning to him.

On April 1st Old Zheng officially joined Yuanwang. On his first day he flew to Changsha and went straight into lockdown mode. Zhao Bin’s team happened to be at the crunch stage of a technical project, so Old Zheng simply worked alongside them. The company had stocked the office with fruit, instant noodles and sleeping bags; sometimes Old Zheng slept at a hotel near the office, sometimes he just slept in a sleeping bag at work. Fifteen days later, a technical problem that had plagued the team for a long time was cracked by Old Zheng together with the existing technical team. Old Zheng’s beard and hair had grown much longer.

Gao Fei was thrilled, truly excited. He invited Zhao Bin and Zheng Zhiyong to dinner and drinks. At the table he said to Zhao Bin, “Thank you, Mr. Zhao, for finding me an all-round heavyweight like Mr. Zheng — able with the pen and the sword, a true Zhiyong: zhi for wisdom and yong for courage!” Zhao Bin then said to Old Zheng, “This toast is to the most technically gifted COO our company’s ever had. From here on, the tech team will be counting on Mr. Zheng’s guidance.” Three middle-aged men, kindred spirits, reluctant to part, drank until midnight before going their separate ways.

Zheng Zhiyong wasn’t drunk. He was luxuriating in the joy of making a name at the new company on his very first try. Swaying a little, he collapsed onto the hotel’s big bed. The hotel stood on the bank of the Xiang River; from the window he could see the winding river and the boats on it. As he lay there, his phone pinged and a WeChat message drifted in.

“Little Diao congratulates Mr. Zheng on a victory at the first charge!”

“Thanks, Ms. Diao. Even you in Beijing heard?”

“All the executives know. You’re seriously amazing — Professor Gao is over the moon this time!”

“Thanks, Ms. Diao”

“I knew I hadn’t misjudged you. That day at dinner I was thinking, how can there be a man like you, with so much talent and vision!”

Zheng Zhiyong didn’t know whether he should reply. He stared at Diao Kexin’s last line, wanting to type a few polite words, yet hesitating.

While he was still hesitating, Diao Kexin video-called him. Zheng Zhiyong panicked, switched off his camera and answered in voice. Diao Kexin said over the WeChat call:

“As the hero who brought Mr. Zheng to Yuanwang — now that you’ve made your name at the company in one stroke, shouldn’t you treat me to a meal to show your gratitude?” She paused briefly, then said, “I’ll fly to Changsha tomorrow.”

At the executive meeting the next afternoon, sure enough, Diao Kexin was there. Unlike usual, when she joined the weekly meeting on Tencent Meeting, she was actually sitting in the conference room at Changsha headquarters. She sat at the far end, as far as possible from Chairman Gao Fei and the chairman’s wife, while Old Zheng this time sat in the seat closest to the chairman. The whole meeting revolved around praising the success of Old Zheng and Zhao Bin’s team in cracking the technical problem, and looked ahead to several business moves that could be rolled out quickly on the back of it. Old Zheng looked across at the head of sales, Diao Kexin, at the far end of the room. She hardly looked up, fiddling with her phone, a completely different person from the one chatting so animatedly the night before. When the meeting ended, the dozen or so executives left their seats; Ms. Diao was the first one out of the room.

At the end of the day, Old Zheng got a WeChat from Diao Kexin: “I’m waiting for you downstairs.”

As soon as Old Zheng came down he saw Diao Kexin in a green wrap sweater dress and high heels, looking at her phone in the lobby of the office building. Old Zheng walked over; Diao Kexin looked up, saw him, and broke into a sweet, lovely smile. “How about sour-soup fish?”

Old Zheng said, “Anything’s fine. Your call.”

The two walked out onto the street for a Guizhou sour-soup fish hotpot. Diao Kexin ordered beer and toasted him glass after glass, served him chopstick after chopstick, praised him line after line, until at last Old Zheng’s floodgates opened. He started from his university days, how he’d helped his professors crack technical problems; how at the Space Academy he’d cracked satellite design problems; how he’d maneuvered his way into solving technical problems for industry bigwigs and become their honored guest. Diao Kexin always listened very earnestly, saying now and then, “My god, you’re amazing. How do you come up with all these ideas?”

Later, when the drinking had hit its stride, Old Zheng couldn’t help pouring out his frustrations: the instability of his career these past years; how many of his peers from the same intake in the system had been promoted, while he hadn’t made much money hopping jobs outside; how one Dragon Boat Festival he’d broken his leg playing ball and sat at home out of work for most of a year; how his wife was endlessly unhappy that he couldn’t make money. At that, Diao Kexin laid her hand on Old Zheng’s arm in gentle comfort and sympathy.

After a few rounds, Diao Kexin said to Old Zheng, “Can I call you Brother Yong?”

Old Zheng said, “Ms. Diao, when we’re not at the office, sure.”

Diao Kexin said, “Brother Yong, can you stop calling me Ms. Diao? You should call me Keke. Okay?”

Old Zheng repeated it silently in his heart: “Keke.” He looked at Diao Kexin and said neither yes nor no.

Diao Kexin went on, “Brother Yong, you know what? Besides your great technical skills, your broad knowledge, your sharp mind, you have another strength. Do you know what it is?”

Old Zheng chuckled and shook his head.

Diao Kexin said, “You’re so good-looking. Do you know how good-looking you are? Look — your skin’s fair, your nose is high, your features are so well-proportioned. The moment I saw you I thought, how can there be such a handsome man in the world?”

Old Zheng was knocked senseless by these sweet words. Objectively speaking, Old Zheng really couldn’t be called good-looking — the most you could say was that he wasn’t ugly. He was thirty-eight, of average height, going a little soft, thick-bodied, with a slight paunch, rough skin on his face and stubble everywhere; luckily his hair was still decent and he wasn’t balding. And Old Zheng was a chain smoker. He’d started in college, so he’d been smoking close to twenty years. Whenever Zhang Jingyu saw him smoking, she’d say, “Stop smoking! You’ll smoke out the kid — if you have to smoke, go outside!” So Old Zheng hid on the balcony to smoke, and if he carelessly left a butt behind, he’d inevitably get a scolding from Zhang Jingyu. Years of smoking had stained his teeth yellow, and childhood cavities had left him with a row of gappy teeth, so when Old Zheng smiled, that row of sparse yellow teeth was hard to miss. As for fair skin — that was pure nonsense. Old Zheng’s skin was dark and rough; where would fair come from?

And yet this line knocked Old Zheng senseless. He suddenly felt that he really was the best-looking man in the world; all these years such a handsome exterior had been drowned out by his talent, and nobody had ever discovered this strength of his. No night could have stirred his heart more.

After dinner they went back to the hotel. As they walked into the lobby, Diao Kexin said, “Brother Yong, I ate way too much tonight. Could we go for a walk by the Xiang River in a bit?” Old Zheng said okay.

Diao Kexin said, “Look, I spilled a big drop of oil on my chest. I’m going up to change. Wait for me.”

When Diao Kexin came back down, she had changed into a sleeveless strapless top that bared her shoulders and neck, with a long midi skirt. Her two hemispheres were on the verge of spilling out over the top; the neckline was really rather low and barely covered anything — it seemed even the rosy areolas were faintly visible.

Yet Diao Kexin walked up to Old Zheng as openly as you please and even took his arm to walk out. At the door Old Zheng saw the cigarette butts in the smoking area outside the hotel, shook off Diao Kexin’s hand and said, “Ms. Diao, mind if I have a smoke?”

Diao Kexin smiled charmingly. “Don’t call me Ms. Diao, call me Little Ke. Go ahead and smoke — I’ll join you.”

Old Zheng, a pure, straight-laced engineering type, had seemingly never seen many women smoke. He lit his cigarette; Ms. Diao deftly took one from his pack as well and held it elegantly between her index and middle fingers. Old Zheng lit it for her, and the two smoked face to face. As she smoked, Ms. Diao tilted her head to look at Old Zheng, smiling like a flower.

Old Zheng said, “Why do you keep smiling? What’s so funny?”

Ms. Diao said, “I already told you.”

Old Zheng said, “I don’t know.”

Ms. Diao said, “You’re good-looking. You’re way too good-looking. Anyway, when I look at you, however I look at you, you’re good-looking.”

When they had finished smoking, they went on toward the river, and Ms. Diao took the chance to slip her arm through Old Zheng’s again. In late April the wind off the river still carried a touch of chill. Old Zheng took off his sports jacket and put it on Ms. Diao, once again shaking off the arm linked through his.

Ms. Diao held out her arms and let Old Zheng help her into the jacket as naturally as anything, then said, “Your jacket smells so good. It has a really nice smell.”

Old Zheng asked her, “What smell?”

Ms. Diao said softly, “The smell of a man.” With that she slipped her arm through Old Zheng’s once more, and this time Old Zheng didn’t shake her off; he let Keke hold on lightly, and the two walked arm in arm along the river for a long, long time.

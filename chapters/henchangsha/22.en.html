Chapter Twenty-Two: Feiyi

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Feiyi” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Northern Mountains”), on Mount Hunxi there is a snake with one head and two bodies, called the feiyi; when it appears, its land suffers great drought. As the saying goes: one head, two bodies — in the end they go one way.

Zhang Hailu had left Yuanwang two years earlier, for a very simple reason: when it came to the equity split, he learned his share was under 0.5 percent — less even than Diao Kexin’s — and he was very unhappy. So he’d rather go back to his hometown, where he took a technical job at a machining plant a university classmate had founded, with a share of the profits too. His income was lower than at Yuanwang, but at least he was close to home, and in those years he’d married and had a child and lived two or three easy, contented years.

So when Zheng Zhiyong asked him to join Xinghuan as a technical partner, he was tempted. After all, he’d settled down in his hometown, and deciding to change cities was a very big thing. After two weeks of thinking it over, he decided to accept Old Zheng’s invitation and first go have a look at their office in Xi’an. That day, as arranged, he arrived at the company’s small building in the Xi’an High-Tech Industrial Park, and never expected that the person who greeted him would be an old friend, someone familiar and dear to him.

That old friend was someone from his hometown: Cong Shasha.

Cong Shasha was standing at the reception desk on the ground floor, smiling all over her face, waiting to welcome him. The moment Zhang Hailu walked into the office building, Cong Shasha tilted her head, pressed her lips together and kept on smiling as Zhang Hailu walked toward her with a look of complete astonishment.

“You’ve joined this place?” Zhang Hailu asked.

“That’s right, I’ve been here two weeks,” Cong Shasha answered with a smile. “I’ve been waiting for you.”

“And you never said a word to me all this time,” said Zhang Hailu.

“Brother Hailu, you know I’ve only just left Yuanwang, and my client relationships are still all Yuanwang’s. I thought I should keep a bit of an information wall between me and people from Yuanwang,” Shasha answered.

Since Old Zheng had taken over this shell company, he’d fired several of the basic engineering staff and fired the receptionist too, keeping only a very lean little team. Two weeks earlier Shasha had joined the company as sales director, and she also handled all the outside liaison and reception. She was by nature an outgoing, warm, cheerful girl, but what was rarer still, this girl so young had one more special strength: as a senior salesperson in a very particular industry, she knew when to say what — and even better, she knew when to shut her mouth and say nothing at all.

So Zhang Hailu really was taken completely by surprise just then. The two of them had been in touch on and off all this time, and he’d never guessed that Shasha had already been working here for two weeks.

Shasha walked Zhang Hailu upstairs, where Old Zheng was waiting for him in his own second-floor office. The office was right next to Wu You’s huge open-plan room, and it was also a bright rectangular open-plan space with floor-to-ceiling windows all the way across. By the door there was a wraparound sofa set, and inside was a whole wall of whiteboard covered with the tracking threads left over from brainstorms about project process management. Most of the writing was in abbreviations and code names; to an outsider it might as well have been a book from heaven.

But Zhang Hailu was no outsider. He looked up at the writing on the whiteboard and understood something at once, and so he said, “Mr. Zheng, you’ve already started work over here. Looks like this is a Sixth Academy project?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Mr. Hailu is a real pro, all right. From just a few scraps, you can tell it’s a Sixth Academy project.”

Zhang Hailu said, “The Sixth Academy was the foundation Yuanwang was built on. I know their situation pretty well. Where does this project stand right now?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “R&D has already given us their requirements. As a bidding unit, our qualifications are no problem, and in terms of technical capability we don’t have any gaps right now either. The problem is that we have to get them a bid document as quickly as possible. You know how it is — for a bid with a 20-million contract value we need at least a 1,500-to-2,000-page bid document. Time’s tight and the job’s heavy, and the few of us here with our few guns can’t pull it off. So I need to quickly rally every force I can rally to take part in this project.”

Zhang Hailu asked, “Is Yuanwang going to take part in this project?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Right now I have no way of getting that information. I don’t know if they’ll take part. In terms of information channels — if even we can find out about this, then with their network and their information pipelines they very likely know too. So my guess is it’s very possible they’ll have a shot at bidding on this project as well. In other words, our competitor this time may very well be Yuanwang.”

The competitor was Yuanwang — that fired Zhang Hailu up enormously, and so he said to Old Zheng, “How far along is the project now? Which step are you at?”

Old Zheng said, “The few rounds of communication so far have all gone very smoothly. The few tech colleagues at the company and I haven’t been home for three or four days now — we’ve been at the office working on the bid materials, hoping to finish the whole proposal within a week or two. Time’s extremely tight.”

Zhang Hailu went on to ask, “Mr. Zheng, what are you expecting from me?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I’d like you to tell me what you need in order to join, and I’ll work things out to meet it. And what I need from you is for you to join us as soon as possible. The sooner we get started, the better.”

Zhang Hailu said, “I don’t need to settle my salary terms right now. What I’d like to know is whether you’ve given any thought to how project bonuses will be set for the whole team once it’s on this project.”

Old Zheng said, “I can promise that this time the bonus pool shared by the whole team that takes part will be ten percent of the project’s total profit.”

Ten percent was the share of equity Zheng Zhiyong already held at Xinghuan, registered on the books with the authorities, and also the absolute limit of what he could allocate.

Zhang Hailu said, “I understand, Mr. Zheng. I don’t care about short-term returns in what I do. If it’s all right with you, I don’t need to come on as an employee, but I can start working full-time right away. I’m willing to offer a try-before-you-buy deal: I’ll work on this project first, without our discussing any pay at all, and you can see what I’m worth first. How’s that?”

Technical people who’d come on board on terms like that were extremely rare. Generally speaking, only someone with tremendous self-knowledge and technical talent would have the nerve to propose a try-before-you-buy deal. Old Zheng was thrilled inside; at that moment he knew he’d found the right man. Xinghuan’s first heavyweight partner was about to make his grand entrance.

He said, “Mr. Hailu, I promise your contribution will be matched with a fitting reward. How much time do you need to join us?”

Zhang Hailu said, “I need to make a few phone calls.”

Zhang Hailu walked out of Old Zheng’s office onto the second-floor terrace and called his distant uncle — that is, the boss of the firm he was now with — and his own wife, a few dozen minutes each, and then came back into the office.

“I can stay starting today.”

Old Zheng was thrilled. He immediately called the other team members into the office and introduced Mr. Hailu to them. “This is our company’s head of technology,” he announced happily. “He’s joining us and getting to work today.”

Zhang Hailu really was a top-notch expert. After spending a day or two learning the project’s basic information and getting a detailed picture of its progress, he got into the swing of it almost immediately. At the same time he quickly brought in a few of his classmates from Xiangnan University who still worked inside the system, and on certain information and technical matters they acted as off-the-books advisers on the project’s preparation and on writing the bid. After two weeks of intense work, a document of more than 1,800 pages was ready.

And Zheng Zhiyong was about to set off for Beijing, with Zhang Hailu and this complete set of materials.

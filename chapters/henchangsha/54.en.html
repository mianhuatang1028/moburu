Chapter Fifty-Four: Youyu

Translation first published: 2026-11-19 17:46 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-19 17:46 (UTC+8) Last updated: 2026-11-19 17:58 © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Youyu” — in the Classic of Mountains and Seas, the Northern Mountains, in the Peng River of Mount Dai there is a fish shaped like a chicken, with red plumage, three tails, six feet and four heads, its cry like a magpie's: eating it can cure melancholy. Truly, one body bearing many heads, many tails turned in different directions — some things seem to stand each apart, yet before one is aware they have already been woven into a net that draws every strand toward every other.

When Shasha returned to the hotel a little past three in the afternoon, Wu Peng was on a call with Wu You; seeing her come in, he quickly hung up — and sure enough, the girl had brought back a whole string of good news.

Xiao Jing had undergone a single-port laparoscopic surgery two weeks earlier to remove an ovarian chocolate cyst — a form of proactive health management — and was consequently on leave at present. With a month still to go before the New Year holiday, she would not need to go into the office at all; remote work would suffice. This meant that if Xiao Jing joined them, her personal time would be entirely ample. As for her willingness to take part, Shasha reported that Xiao Jing had voiced no refusal.

Wu Peng asked, “Does Xiao Jing harbour any hard feelings about her former relationship with Old Zheng?”

Cong Shasha said: “I asked that too. Xiao Jing told me she didn't think much of the fact that adults had dated for a few months; she said then that nothing could interfere with Big Sister here making money.”

Wu Peng laughed: “She's a very genuine sort of female PhD, isn't she.”

Cong Shasha said: “Yes. Her only remaining concern now is whether her technical ability can cover our expectations and needs — that's the key point for you to discuss when you meet this evening.”

Wu Peng nodded, drew Shasha into his arms and kissed her. “My wife has done it again.”

This was the first time since they began dating that Wu Peng had called Shasha his wife; from yesterday to today, the progress of their relationship had been like the launch of a rocket, pulling off a three-stage jump in one bound.

That evening, the three of them met at a rather upscale Sichuan restaurant.

The restaurant was in a shopping mall on Financial Street. The private room was not large, but it was tastefully appointed: dark wood panelling, warm yellow lighting, a few modern ink paintings hanging on the wall, custom-made celadon tableware, and no superfluous ornament on the table. The waiter first brought hot towels and tea, then handed the menu to Wu Peng.

Wu Peng did not pass the menu back and forth, but first invited Xiao Jing to order.

Xiao Jing turned a few pages and smiled: “You two order. I only need to make sure there's protein.”

“Then let's go with a mix of non-spicy and mildly spicy dishes,” Wu Peng said. “Shasha tells me you've just had surgery, so protein is a must.”

He ordered a chicken douhua, a camphor-tea duck and a plate of 'boiling-water' cabbage, then chose kung pao shrimp balls, douban fish and several seasonal vegetables. He did not hurry to order staple foods, meaning to wait until they were well into the meal. As the dishes arrived one after another, the three talked first about the weather in Beijing, people back home and recent developments. Wu Peng did not launch into business at once; only after Xiao Jing had eaten a few mouthfuls did he gradually steer the conversation toward Xinghuan's current project.

“Shasha told me you're willing to look into our current technical situation. In fact, the difficulties the company has run into of late are mainly due to changes in the original technical team. The Xi'an project hasn't been finally settled yet; the review requires supplementary material, one item of which is the implementation and verification of the algorithm on different hardware platforms. Dr. Lan was mainly responsible for that part, while Zhang Hailu oversaw the coordination and delivery of the whole technical team — and now both of them are leaving.”

Xiao Jing helped herself to a mouthful with her chopsticks and asked: “What platform do you currently train your models on?”

“Mainly workstations; after training we transfer them to the device side. Different devices have different computing platforms — some are CPU, some come with an NPU. The problem now is that a model runs fine on one platform, but switch it to another and issues crop up.” Wu Peng was not being entirely precise. His understanding of the matter came chiefly from these past weeks spent hanging about the technical office listening to the engineers discuss things, and from what Old Zheng and Zhang Hailu had explained to him. He roughly knew where the problem lay, but found it hard to spell out each technical link clearly.

Xiao Jing did not interrupt him, but went on asking: “The problem you describe — is it that the model can't be converted properly, or that it runs after conversion but fails to meet the requirements for precision and real-time performance?”

Wu Peng thought for a moment. “Both. It varies from platform to platform.”

“So do you have a unified model baseline version? For instance, once training is complete you first freeze one version of the model, then carry out conversion and adaptation for the different platforms?”

Wu Peng shook his head. “Dr. Xiao, to be honest I'm not clear on any of these questions. I can show you our rectification requirements. Our real predicament is that of the original team that wrote the bid, two-thirds of its core members have left.”

Xiao Jing took Wu Peng's phone and looked at the rectification notes in the technical document, studying them seriously for several minutes. “The people who left — what were their respective positions before?”

Wu Peng explained the four people's positions one by one, then added: “I more or less understand how the work was divided among them, but I'm not sure how accurate my account is — after all, I really don't understand the specific technical details and how the division of labour meshed.” He went on, “But I can write down the questions you've raised, and when I get back have the team collate answers to them for you.”

Xiao Jing smiled. “It's all right — I'm not testing you. I have a general picture of your situation now.”

She set down her chopsticks, picked up her teacup and continued: “I think I understand your situation well enough. In fact, this isn't beyond remedy. The model itself may not need to be torn down and rebuilt from scratch; the key is first to unify the training baseline, the preprocessing pipeline and the deployment versions for each platform. Then do operator adaptation platform by platform, marking in particular the parts the NPU doesn't support and the parts that fall back to the CPU — you can't just look at whether the model loads. After quantization you have to redo the precision regression; you can't take the metrics of the original FP32 model and treat them directly as the deployment result.”

She paused a moment, then added:

“If there are differences in data distribution caused by different batches of sensors, that part has to be isolated and verified separately. Otherwise you may think the model is performing inconsistently across platforms when in fact the input data has changed. Finally you must test on real equipment — at least a few representative sets of sensors and computing platforms — and measure clearly the precision, latency, memory footprint and power consumption. Just getting it to run in a simulation environment isn't enough.”

Wu Peng nodded. What Xiao Jing said corresponded to the discussions he had heard in the technical office these past weeks — only she had regrouped the scattered problems and, in one stroke, pointed out several easily overlooked places.

“The most important thing for you now is to get a clear grasp of your existing model and deployment chain. Don't rush to swap algorithms, and don't rush to retrain. If the model itself is sound and only the conversion and adaptation have been done badly, retraining will only complicate matters. And all this concrete work will need people assigned to it.” She said this in a very calm tone, without any deliberate display of her own ability.

Wu Peng, however, heard the weight behind it.

He had met many technical people before. Some would use a great deal of jargon when talking about technology, yet when it came to actually landing a project, they always circled back to the little patch they knew best. Xiao Jing was different: she did not rush to judge Xinghuan's technical level, nor did she first discuss how much she could earn. Instead she considered model baseline, input data, operator adaptation, quantization regression and on to real-device verification. She was assessing whether she had the complete capability to take over this work and see it through.

“Then would you be willing to help us out for a while first?” Wu Peng asked.

“I'll need to go back to the company and hand over the current state of affairs to my technical team. After that, you may need to hire one or two people at the company according to my requirements — at most two — and one of them is a former subordinate of mine. She could come over part-time first, and we can consider whether she joins properly later.” Xiao Jing said, “I'm not considering joining the company directly for now; I've just had surgery, after all, and in the short term sitting in the office every day wouldn't suit me. A consultancy-type arrangement, however, I can accept. Give me the project materials, and I'll do a technical assessment first, then arrange my time according to the actual workload.”

Wu Peng nodded. “We're not necessarily asking you to join now. What Xinghuan needs most at the moment is to resolve the technical problems on this project. As for how we cooperate later, we can talk about that later.”

Xiao Jing glanced at him. “There's one more thing I'd like to make clear in advance.”

“Go ahead.”

“For the initial handover of this project, Zheng Zhiyong and Zhang Hailu can participate remotely online; I won't appear in person. But once the handover is complete, Zheng Zhiyong will no longer be in the R&D module of this project, and I will take over the production module in full. In theory the two of us won't have much overlap, nor any relationship of superior and subordinate.” She said it very directly, yet there was no emotion in her tone.

Wu Peng showed no surprise; he merely nodded.

“That's fine. I'll be responsible for coordinating the project; you'll only need to liaise with me. I'll organise the technical plan, the staffing and the schedule. After the initial handover is complete, Mr. Zheng won't take part in the specific work on this project.”

Xiao Jing seemed fairly satisfied with that answer.

The three talked a while longer about the details of the cooperation. Xiao Jing proposed that they first sign a technical-service agreement through an outsourcing company she had worked with before, so as not to disturb her current work and life arrangements. Wu Peng would have the company prepare the contract when he got back: a consultancy fee of 30,000 yuan per month until the work related to this project ended; and if the project was ultimately won, a further technical-cooperation return of ten percent of the project profit.

Wu Peng did not haggle at the dinner table. “I can accept these terms. We'll have legal and finance confirm the specific contract together, and write clearly the scope of work, the delivery milestones and the basis for calculating profit, so as to avoid ambiguity later.”

Xiao Jing nodded. “That's better.”

By the time the meal ended it was nearly nine. As they were leaving, Shasha helped Xiao Jing with her coat, and Wu Peng saw them to the restaurant door and called a car for Xiao Jing.

After the car drove off, Shasha turned round and looked at Wu Peng with a smile. “Well?”

“Quite good.”

The two exchanged a smile and watched the taxi disappear at the intersection.

Zheng Zhiyong had already returned to Changsha that same evening. Early the next morning, Zhang Hailu came to his office and called out 'Old Zheng'.

Over the past few years they had worked together almost every day. Zhang Hailu was in charge of the technical team, while Old Zheng handled the company's overall operations and project direction; much of the time they did not need to spell things out — a single glance was enough to know what the other was thinking. Now, sitting in the same office, they were to discuss a departure formally, which felt somewhat strange.

“Everything settled on the home front?” Old Zheng asked.

“More or less.”

“Any plans once you're back?”

“I'll rest for a while first. Later I'll probably stay in technology — start a small company, take on a few projects, enough to support the family. I don't want to burn the midnight oil every day the way I used to, either.”

Old Zheng looked at him, and after a moment asked: “Hailu, I'll ask you once more — are you planning to go back to Yuanwang?”

Zhang Hailu's expression did not change noticeably.

“Mr. Zheng, I've told you — that's not it. It's simply to go home.”

“I know you did.” Old Zheng nodded and did not press further. That did not mean he had no suspicions. Before Zhang Hailu left, the destination of the whole team had already seemed rather unusual; it was only that he had no concrete information in hand. He picked up the teacup on the desk and took a sip.

Zhang Hailu went on: “Mr. Zheng, over the past few years you've looked after me well. When the company was first founded, there was nothing; we built the technical team up bit by bit, and none of it was easy. Now I'm leaving and landing you with a whole pile of trouble — I really am rather embarrassed.”

“You have your difficulties. I understand.”

Zhang Hailu smiled but did not answer.

Old Zheng said: “If you really do start a company later, you can go on cooperating with Xinghuan. You know our products, and you know which of our projects need technical support. Whenever there's suitable business, we can sign a proper contract. Running your own company gives you flexible hours too — no need to cut off all the old ties.”

“That would be fine, of course. If there's a suitable project, I'd certainly be willing to cooperate.”

“All right. We'll leave that for later.”

The two talked a few more words about the details of the handover. Zhang Hailu produced a list and went through it item by item, explaining the current state of several projects, where the technical materials were stored, and what the remaining staff could continue to complete. Old Zheng listened attentively, asking an occasional question or two, just as in the old days. When Yuanwang came up, Zhang Hailu suddenly remarked: “Actually, Yuanwang's technical approach these days isn't quite what it used to be.”

Old Zheng looked up. “How so?”

“One of Zhao Bin's subordinates got in touch with me recently. I asked about a few things.”

“What architecture?”

“It's not the old approach of a single model outputting results directly. They've now split the front-end image preprocessing, target-feature extraction and back-end recognition-and-decision into relatively independent modules, doing multi-scale feature fusion for different scenarios, and then outputting results through a unified inference and post-processing chain. The model itself can be pruned and deployed according to the computing capacity of different devices; the algorithm team handles the core model, while the engineering team handles adaptation for each platform.”

Zhang Hailu had not gone into detail, but it was enough for Old Zheng to grasp the general direction. He had already formed a judgement. Yuanwang's present technical edge lay not merely in the more mature algorithmic models possessed by Zhu Yiwen's team, but more importantly in the fact that they had split algorithm development and engineering deployment into a relatively clear system. The core model could be iterated continuously, the deployment end adapted to each hardware platform, without having to reorganise the whole development effort every time the device changed.

Xinghuan's past technical system, by contrast, had relied precisely on key people like Zhang Hailu and Dr. Lan. Much engineering experience had never been fully captured; once the core personnel left, the existing models, toolchains and device-adaptation experience became hard to replicate quickly.

Nothing showed on Old Zheng's face; he only nodded.

Zhang Hailu watched him, studying his expression. He hoped that by this one piece of information he had shown that he was not in league with Yuanwang, and further dispelled Old Zheng's doubts.

Old Zheng pulled open the drawer on one side of his desk and took out a document. “One more thing. I rechecked with finance the year-end bonus I promised you few. Yours is 300,000 yuan, Dr. Lan's 150,000, and Xiao Chen and Xiao Li 60,000 each.”

Zhang Hailu was taken aback. “Mr. Zheng, this...”

“A promise made should still be kept,” Old Zheng said. “But the company is rather short of cash at the moment and can't pay it all in one lump. Finance has arranged to pay it in fifteen monthly instalments, deposited each month into the original salary cards of you four.”

Zhang Hailu took his hand. “Brother, thank you for everything over the years. Take care.”

Their hands clasped and held for a few seconds, then parted.

Zhang Hailu walked out of the office and gently closed the door behind him.

Old Zheng sat back down in his chair and looked at the signed document on the desk; after a while he put it into the drawer. He knew that Zhang Hailu had not told him everything.

Zhang Hailu also knew that he had not fully believed him.

But by now that no longer mattered.

Between adults, there is often no need to puncture the last layer of window paper. Once what needed saying has been said and the formalities that needed doing have been done, with a little room left for each other, the matter may be considered past.

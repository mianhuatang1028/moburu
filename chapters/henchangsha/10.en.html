Biyi Bird

Chapter Ten: Biyi Bird

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Biyi Bird” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Western Mountains, Book Three”), there is a bird with one wing and one eye; only in pairs can they fly, and it is called the manman. As the saying goes: wing to wing they fly; apart, they cannot rise.

Old Zheng got back to his home in Fengtai dark and gaunt, his chin covered in stubble. The moment he came in he kicked off his shoes, headed straight for the bedroom, fell onto the bed and slept.

Zhang Jingyu was as cool as ever; the look in her eyes when she glanced at him showed neither sorrow nor joy.

He slept until it was completely dark. The family had already eaten and had left his dinner on the table under covered plates. Seeing no one in the living room, Old Zheng slipped through to the balcony, lit a cigarette and slid the glass partition door shut behind him. Quietly he took out his phone and made a call. Zhang Jingyu was in their son’s north-facing room across the flat, sitting with him while he did his homework; the home was all peace and quiet harmony.

On the balcony, he talked softly and tenderly with Diao Kexin on the phone. “Little Ke, are you okay? I slept all afternoon. I just couldn’t wake up.”

Diao: “Brother Yong, me too. I’m dead tired.”

Zheng: “But I already miss you again. I miss you like crazy.”

Diao: “I miss you too. I wish you were right here with me.”

Zheng: “We’re not actually that far apart.”

Diao: “Brother Yong, there’s something I have to tell you.” Diao Kexin hesitated.

Zheng: “What is it?”

Diao: “I think Professor Gao might know. He said he’s arriving tonight. Going by the time, he’ll be at my place soon.”

Zheng: “Know what?”

Diao: “In Changsha I went off with you, and for those few days I completely ignored him. He must have sensed something.”

Zheng: “Can’t you just not let Gao Fei come to you?”

Diao: “I can’t stop him. He’s the child’s father. Coming to see her is his right.”

Zheng: “Does Gao Fei already know about me?”

Diao: “If he knew about you and me, he’d kill you for sure, and maybe me too. He can’t find out.”

Zheng: “Don’t be afraid. Whatever happens, I’ll protect you.”

Diao: “I don’t need your protection. I just need you to grow up at the speed of flight. To prove your love for me.”

Zheng: “Little Ke, I love you. You know that.”

Diao: “I know you love me, but you don’t have what it takes to love me yet. You have to become strong enough to have what it takes to love me. I have to go. We’ll talk later. Bye, miss you.”

Zheng: “Me too — miss you. So, so much.”

She hung up. Old Zheng went into the living room; it was still quiet, his wife still in their son’s room helping with homework. His in-laws weren’t home — probably out for a walk. He uncovered the food, which was still a little warm, popped open a can of beer, and ate the leftovers with it.

The next day when Old Zheng got to the office, he saw Diao Kexin’s sales assistant, whose eyes darted away when she saw him; she sidestepped and went the long way round. He sat at his desk. After a while Gao Fei and Diao Kexin came into the office together, both wearing relaxed, easy smiles. When Diao Kexin passed him, there was no eye contact whatsoever.

Around noon the two of them went out again, side by side. Old Zheng stood at the window for a while and watched the red Audi pull out of the company parking lot and speed off. His heart was full of jealousy.

Toward evening he got a call from Gao Fei. “Mr. Zheng, I didn’t get a chance to talk with you at the office today. There’s something I’d like to discuss. I’ll send you a location — come find me.”

They met at a Japanese izakaya. Seeing Old Zheng, Gao Fei looked relaxed and genial. He said, “Mr. Zheng, your work lately has far exceeded my expectations. Zhao Bin, Ms. Yan’er — everyone sings your praises. I have an idea I’d like to talk over with you.”

Old Zheng looked at his boss nervously. “Mr. Gao, go ahead.”

Gao Fei went on, “I’ve been at this more than ten years. To say I’m not tired would be impossible. The most precious thing in building a company is the partners you build it with. For the last few years I’ve been looking for someone who could take over this company as CEO someday. What I want to tell you today is that Ms. Yan’er and I have talked it over. You’re already COO, and we want to start grooming you as CEO now. In a few days we’ll give you an options agreement for 0.3 percent to sign, and if you move into the CEO role later, we’ll add more.”

Ms. Yan’er was Gao Fei’s wife. Her younger brother ran production and delivery at the company, and his wife ran administration and finance; Ms. Yan’er herself was the company’s second-largest registered shareholder. Not long ago, when they were pulling all-nighters at the office, Ms. Yan’er had often dropped by to check on them; once she’d even had an ox-head feast packed up to reward Zhao Bin, Old Zheng and the whole team working overtime — which showed that from then on, she had taken note of Old Zheng’s work ethic and ability. You could say that with her approval and Gao Fei’s both, his career path at this company was now fairly secure. As for 0.3 percent equity — Zhao Bin, the CTO, who had worked at the company for ten years, had roughly that number. As for Diao Kexin, according to what she’d told him, Gao Fei had promised her two percent, but it was a verbal promise and she still didn’t have an agreement.

At that moment Old Zheng’s feelings were extremely complicated. If he could become CEO of a company valued at 1.5 billion and standing so high in its industry, the career wanderings of the first half of his life would have come to a good end at last. And this 0.3 percent was only a starting point; if it gradually grew, one day he’d have gains in the tens of millions, and then the Financial Street school-district apartment Jingyu wanted would be no problem, and his wife’s respect would come back too.

And yet the darling Diao Kexin, with whom he’d spent those tender days so recently — that reproachful look, that bashful look, that scolding look, that adoring look — kept replaying in his head, over and over. That woman had stolen his soul at this point, and had also given him enormous drive and encouragement, a very clear goal: “If you want to have me, you have to beat Gao Fei in your career.” Even in their moments of lovemaking, lost and entangled in each other, she always said to him, “I have no choice but to give myself to Gao Fei for now, and I want you to take me away from him.”

“You have to be my knight. For the first half of my life I’ve been a love-brained woman tossed around by fate. My husband Li Fulang betrayed me; Gao Fei tricked me into having a child and still won’t marry me. I’ve finally met the love I’m destined for — you, only you. You’re the only man I’ve ever completely, utterly opened my heart to and fallen in love with. You’re my perfect lover, and I only want you, only you. But you have to beat Gao Fei, achieve something bigger than him, before I can go with you.” At the thought of it, Old Zheng wanted to take that woman’s body in his arms and go at it with everything he had — but at this moment, where was she?

Old Zheng’s heart surged. He raised his sake cup and said to Gao Fei, “Thank you for your trust, Chairman. I’ll give it everything I’ve got.”

After a good while, having eaten and drunk their fill, the two men having opened their hearts to each other, Old Zheng said, “Chairman, my car’s right outside. I’ll call a designated driver and take you wherever you need to go.”

Gao Fei said, “No need. It’s still early. In a bit Ms. Diao and I are meeting a friend — oh, one of the higher-ups wants a game of guandan. Ms. Diao’s waiting for me in the car.”

Gao Fei got into the red Audi; the driver was never visible, and the car drove off. Old Zheng’s designated driver hadn’t arrived yet. Feeling he hadn’t had much to drink, he drove off himself and followed the red Audi at a distance. Sure enough, from far off he saw the Audi drive into Diao Kexin’s compound, turn a corner and disappear.

Old Zheng felt a vast, heart-piercing sense of loss; he wrenched the steering wheel hard and drove home.

The moment he walked in, there was Zhang Jingyu, quietly making tea in the living room, her phone lying flat on the dining table.

Old Zheng said, “Have you eaten?”

Zhang Jingyu said, “Yes. Mom and Dad took our son out for a stroll. Let’s talk?”

Old Zheng sensed something subtle in the air. He took off his shoes, pulled up a stool and sat down across from her. “I had dinner with the chairman tonight.”

Zhang Jingyu said, “Your chairman — his name’s Gao Fei, right?”

Old Zheng drew in a breath inwardly and answered with feigned calm, “Yes, that’s right, Gao Fei. I told you — a former Xiangnan University professor who went into business. Pretty well known in the industry.”

Zhang Jingyu poured a cup of tea, pushed it over, and continued. “Zheng Zhiyong, is there anything you want to tell me?”

Old Zheng was panicking inside, but he put on an innocent face. “Huh? Honey, what’s wrong?”

Zhang Jingyu said, “That day our son and I waited for you at the station for hours. It was pouring, cars were impossible to get, and we walked a long way through the downpour before we finally got one home. A few days later you went to Changsha and were basically unreachable. At your company — who is it?”

Old Zheng said, “Oh, honey, I was wrong. I’ve just been so busy with work lately, I haven’t taken good care of you.”

Zhang Jingyu said, “Is it really work keeping you busy? You’ve got someone, haven’t you?”

Old Zheng waved his hands over and over. “No, no, honey, I’m innocent, I don’t.”

Zhang Jingyu said, “Give me your phone.”

Old Zheng’s heart lurched. The past few days’ passion with Little Ke was all still there in WeChat, including a stretch where the two of them had talked about Zhang Jingyu. Diao Kexin had asked, “Someday you’ll have to face Zhang Jingyu. Will you tell her the truth?” Old Zheng had replied on WeChat, “I will. I’ll face her.” Diao Kexin had asked, “Between me and Zhang Jingyu, who do you choose?” Old Zheng had replied, “I choose you.” Diao Kexin had asked, “Between me and your child, who do you choose?” Old Zheng had answered, “I choose you.” Diao Kexin had sent three rose emojis.

There was no way Zhang Jingyu could be allowed to see chats like that — if she saw them, wouldn’t she go mad and take a knife to Old Zheng? Old Zheng said, “Babe, my phone’s dead.”

Zhang Jingyu said coldly, “Give it to me.”

Old Zheng looked pained. The phone was in his pocket, and he was afraid Zhang Jingyu would come over and snatch it out. The passcode was still the family passcode they’d shared for years; once she had the phone in hand, she could unlock it effortlessly and read the chats.

Zhang Jingyu made no move to grab Old Zheng’s phone. Instead she gently pushed her own phone into the middle of the table and pressed play…

“Little Ke, are you okay? I slept all afternoon. I just couldn’t wake up…”

Zheng Zhiyong panicked on the spot. He jumped up and went toward her, saying, “Honey, honey, listen to me.”

Zhang Jingyu turned her face away, and suddenly the tears came. She said, “If you’ve found someone, you can tell me. We’ll part on good terms.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I haven’t. I lost my head for a moment. Honey, I was wrong. I love you. I love you and our son. I can’t leave you and our son.” At that moment Zheng Zhiyong’s heart was a routed army. He meant every word: found out so suddenly, he realized he simply didn’t want to leave this home, didn’t want to leave Zhang Jingyu and their child.

Zhang Jingyu said, “I’ll give you two choices. Either we divorce right away, or you cut it off with her completely, right away, and leave your current company. Choose.”

Old Zheng said, “I’ll cut it off with her.” He took out his phone, opened WeChat in front of Zhang Jingyu, deleted Diao Kexin on the spot, and deleted all their chat history of the past few days.

Zhang Jingyu said, “There’s an agreement here. Sign it.”

It was a postnuptial property agreement, and it stated clearly: “Because Zheng Zhiyong has committed infidelity during the marriage, as a sign of his repentance, all property of the marriage — including one apartment currently paid off in full, all 2.4 million yuan in savings held in both their names, one BYD car, and all rights to the proceeds of insurance policies in each of their names — shall belong to Zhang Jingyu. Zheng Zhiyong promises never again to commit infidelity during the marriage. Should any further infidelity occur, the two shall divorce, and on divorce the property shall be divided in the manner already agreed in this agreement; in addition, after the divorce he shall pay Zhang Jingyu and her son maintenance of fifteen thousand yuan a month until the child reaches the age of twenty-two, and over the following ten years shall pay a penalty totaling three million yuan.”

The agreement also said: “If henceforth Zheng Zhiyong completely ceases infidelity during the marriage, Zhang Jingyu will not again raise his past wrongdoing during the marriage and will refrain from mockery, insult or other verbal abuse. Both parties will work together, on the principle of continuing the marriage, to look after their little family and protect their child’s happiness.”

“Open your salary account,” Zhang Jingyu said, “and transfer the whole balance to me.”

Old Zheng transferred the money; all he had left now was 370 yuan and 60 fen of loose change in WeChat. He read the agreement over several times, then took the pen Zhang Jingyu handed him and signed.

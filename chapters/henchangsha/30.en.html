Zhuhuai

Chapter Thirty: Zhuhuai

“Zhuhuai” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Northern Mountains”), on Mount Beiyue there is a beast shaped like an ox with four horns, human eyes and a pig’s ears; its cry is like a wild goose’s, and it eats people. As the saying goes: four horns facing one another, each pointing its own way.

Once that bottle of red was open, neither of them drank fast.

The white marble table was two meters long, and they sat at just one end, very close. Cong Shasha’s knee was almost touching Zheng Zhiyong’s; she didn’t move it away, and he didn’t shift either. The six dishes and a soup sat before them, the yellow bone china glowing soft and warm under the light. She picked up a bite with her chopsticks, put it down again, raised her glass and took a sip, her eyes on the bit of dark red light in the glass.

Shasha said, “Brother Yong, you’re an interesting one.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Interesting how?”

Cong Shasha said, “When you’re out talking projects with people, you talk in whole speeches — technical roadmaps, supply chains, delivery milestones. You sound like a real tech guy. But the minute you come back to this room, setting out the dishes, pouring wine — you’ve actually got a real sense of mood, you know how to live. You’re like two different people.”

Zheng Zhiyong smiled and didn’t answer. These were all skills that long years of married life had trained into him. They say that if a man makes a woman comfortable in every way, he’s very likely been well trained by someone before her, and Zheng Zhiyong was a textbook case. He raised his glass, clinked it against hers and drank. As the wine went down his stomach warmed a little, and he loosened up a little too. He looked at her and suddenly felt this woman was no longer the slightly silly girl he’d first met. Since she’d started building the business with him, she’d grown sharper and more capable by the day, and a good deal more guarded, as if she’d thought a lot of things through. The woman she was now was capable and brisk, and had that softness peculiar to women too — a softness that was a little deliberate, and a little sincere.

Cong Shasha put down her glass and turned to look at him. Her eyes were very bright, and the wine had brushed a faint flush onto her cheeks. She said, “Brother Yong, what I’m about to say isn’t the wine talking.” She sighed softly and said, “I don’t want to leave.”

The room went quiet for a moment. Outside the window the streetlamps in the park were on, their light coming in through the floor-to-ceiling glass and falling on one side of her face.

Zheng Zhiyong looked at her for a few seconds. A great many things flashed through his head — Diao Kexin, Zhang Jingyu, Wu Peng, Wu You, the stuck Sixth Academy project, gold bars, payment terms, Wang Renhou. All of it churned together and had kept him from a single full night’s sleep these past days. But just now, all of it drew back a little, back to somewhere far away. All that was left in front of him was this woman, sitting very close to him, her eyes on him, not dodging, not flinching.

He reached out, took the glass from her hand and set it on the table. Then he leaned over and kissed her.

Cong Shasha didn’t pull away. Her hands came up and rested on the back of his neck, her fingertips slipping into his hair. Her lips were soft, with a bit of the wine’s sweetness and astringency on them. Zheng Zhiyong’s kiss went from light to hard; her hands slid from the back of his neck to his shoulders, then to his back, and through his shirt he could feel the warmth of her palms.

He stood up and pulled her up from her chair too. There wasn’t much room by the table, and the two of them pressed together. Her body was very soft against his chest; he could feel her heartbeat through the thin knit top, beat after beat, very fast. His hand went up from her waist, found her back, found the clasp of her bra, and stopped for a moment.

Cong Shasha said in his ear, “Don’t stop.”

And his hands moved. He undid the bra, began to fondle Shasha’s breasts, and kissed them.

What came after, the two of them moved toward that two-meter bed together. The screen got bumped, swayed and steadied again. Her knit top was tossed carelessly over the arm of the sofa. She undid his shirt buttons one by one; halfway down she grew impatient and simply yanked, and a button popped off and rolled onto the carpet. Neither of them went to pick it up.

The bedding was dark gray, and as her body sank into it her skin looked very white against the gray. Zheng Zhiyong knelt beside her and looked at her for a while. She lay on the bed, her hair fanned out, her eyes on him, her chest rising and falling, her lips slightly parted. She reached out, took hold of his wrist and pulled him down onto her.

“Brother Yong,” she said, her voice a little hoarse, her eyes fixed and steady.

He bent down and kissed her face and her lips.

This time was different from all the others before. There was no drunken haze, no half-pushing-away-half-giving-in, no one pulling the covers over anyone. Both of them were clearheaded, both knew what they were doing, both wanted it. She was very forward, her hands roaming his back, his waist, his chest, sometimes hard, sometimes light, as if testing his reactions, or telling him what she wanted. His movements, urgent at first, slowly grew steady, found their rhythm. He bent his head to kiss her neck, her collarbone, and heard her make a very soft sound in his ear, a sound like a thread that drew out, bit by bit, all the tangled mess in his heart.

She rolled over and pinned him beneath her, her hair falling and sweeping across his chest. She looked at him, her eyes glinting in the dimness. She said, “Zheng Zhiyong, look at me.”

He looked at her.

She said, “It’s me now.”

He said nothing, reached up and pulled her head down, and kissed her.

After that, neither of them said anything more. There was only the rustle of the bedding in the room and the two of them breathing heavier and heavier. The dark gray comforter slid to the floor, and nobody picked it up. Beyond the floor-to-ceiling window behind the screen, the park’s streetlamps stayed on all night, their light coming in and falling on the foot of the bed, on the two of them’s ankles.

The next morning Zheng Zhiyong went into the city on his own.

When he left, Cong Shasha was still asleep. He dressed quietly, put the popped button in his pocket, went downstairs and drove off. The white Toyota Prado pulled out of the park onto the ring road and headed into town.

Today he was going to take care of the gold bars. Zhang Hailu had told him that with this sort of thing you couldn’t make a transfer, couldn’t use a card, couldn’t leave any record — it had to be physical, ideally gold bars. Back at Yuanwang he’d heard Diao Kexin mention it once in passing — with this sort of thing, “it has to be the real thing, no transfers” — and he hadn’t thought much of it then. Now that it was his turn to do it, he understood just how heavy those words were.

First he went to an old gold shop near the Bell Tower. The shopfront wasn’t big. The counters held gold necklaces, gold bangles, gold padlock pendants; an incandescent bulb hung above the glass counter, its light striking the gold so that the whole case glowed yellow. He stood at the counter looking for a while without speaking. The salesgirl behind the counter asked him, “What can I show you, sir?” He said, “Just looking.” Then he turned and left.

He walked out of the shop, stood by the street and lit a cigarette. His hands were shaking a little. He cursed himself: Zheng Zhiyong, for fuck’s sake, you signed the divorce agreement and you’re scared of this?

The second shop was down an old lane, its front even smaller, the sign reading “Lao Feng Xiang Silver House.” He went in. Behind the counter sat a man of about fifty with reading glasses on, looking at a newspaper. When he saw him come in, he put the paper down and asked, “What would you like, sir?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Gold bars.”

The man glanced at him. “What size?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Fifty-gram, hundred-gram — let me see both.”

The man took a velvet box out from under the counter and opened it. Inside, neatly lined up, were several gold bars, the small ones fifty grams and the large ones a hundred, their faces stamped with patterns and their weight. The man said, “Gold’s at six hundred eighty-seven a gram today. Fifty grams and a hundred grams. How many do you want?”

Zheng Zhiyong did the sums in his head. Fifty-gram ones, he planned to take four to start. Hundred-gram ones, two to start. All together it came to a bit over three hundred thousand. Have it ready first, and if it wasn’t enough, deal with that later.

He said, “Four of the fifty-gram ones, two of the hundred-gram.”

The man glanced at him again and asked nothing more, but turned and went into the back to get the goods. Zheng Zhiyong stood at the counter and heard the sound of a safe opening in the back, metal clinking against metal, then footsteps. The man came out, laid six gold bars on the counter, then brought out an electronic scale and weighed them for him one by one.

Zheng Zhiyong looked at the gold bars lying on the counter, glowing yellow, stamped with patterns, and suddenly it all felt a little unreal. These things were what he was going to give away. Give to someone he’d never met, whose background he didn’t know — all he knew was that his surname was Wu. He remembered last winter in that office in Xi’an, the dead fish floating in the tank, when he hadn’t even known where next month’s rent would come from. And now here he stood, putting down two hundred thousand at once on gold bars to give to a stranger.

He asked the man, “Can I pay by card?”

The man said, “You can, but for large amounts we have to register your ID.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Register it, then.”

He swiped his card, signed, and put the gold bars into a black velvet pouch the man handed him. The pouch wasn’t big. With six gold bars inside it was heavy, and carrying it in his hand it had a very solid weight. He tucked the pouch into the inside pocket of his jacket, against his chest, and walked out of the shop.

The sun in the street was lovely, warm on his body. He stood by the street and lit another cigarette. His hands were still shaking a little. He took two drags, stubbed it out on a trash can, and drove back to the park.

When he got back to the fourth floor, Cong Shasha was already up, her hair tied in a careless ponytail, sitting at the table looking at her phone. When she saw him come in, she looked up and smiled. “All done?”

Zheng Zhiyong took off his jacket, took the black velvet pouch out of the inside pocket, put it on the table and pushed it in front of her.

Cong Shasha opened the pouch, glanced inside, then looked up at him.

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Two of the hundred-gram ones. You take these for now, and if it’s not enough, we’ll see.”

Cong Shasha closed the pouch, looked at him and said, “Mr. Zheng, you know once this goes out, it’s not coming back.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I know.”

Cong Shasha said, “And you know that once it’s gone out, I’ll have to go and take care of that thing.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I know.”

Cong Shasha looked at him for a while, then put the pouch away in her bag, stood up, came over to him and reached out to straighten the collar of his jacket. She said, “Then I’ll ask to meet him this afternoon.”

Getting a meeting with Director Wu went more smoothly than Shasha had imagined.

She sent Director Wu a WeChat message, worded very politely: she was Cong Shasha, who’d come out of Yuanwang and was now working at Xinghuan; she happened to be in Beijing on business and had some things she’d like to ask his advice on in person, if it was convenient for him. About an hour after she sent it, he replied: “Seven tonight, Jinma Club, east side of the city.”

The Jinma Club was a members-only club on Jinbao Street, with a membership fee of eighty thousand a year — but the good part was that it wasn’t open to the public and there weren’t many people about. Shasha had seen Director Wu here the last time too. Inside it was done up Chinese-style, with rosewood tables and chairs in the private rooms and calligraphy and paintings on the walls. When Cong Shasha arrived, Director Wu was already there. He was just past fifty, not tall, a little heavy, his hair combed very neatly, in a dark jacket — he looked like an ordinary government cadre, nothing special about him.

Cong Shasha went in and greeted him with a smile. “Director Wu, it’s been a long time.”

Director Wu looked up, saw her, and a little smile appeared on his face. “Ah, Shasha. Sit.”

Cong Shasha sat down across from him. A waiter came in, poured tea and went out again. That left just the two of them in the private room.

Director Wu said, “You’ve left Yuanwang?”

Cong Shasha said, “I’ve left. I’m at Xinghuan now.”

Director Wu said, “Xinghuan — I know it. The Sixth Academy project — you won the bid.”

Cong Shasha said, “That’s right, we won the bid. But right now it’s stuck in the notice period. We handed in the supplementary materials and nothing’s happened since.”

Director Wu picked up his teacup and took a sip without answering.

Cong Shasha said, “Director Wu, I’ve come today to ask your advice — where exactly is this stuck?”

Director Wu put down his cup, looked at her and said, “Shasha, you were with Ms. Diao at Yuanwang for seven years. You should know these things better than I do.”

Cong Shasha said, “I was with Ms. Diao at Yuanwang for seven years, it’s true. But there were some things Ms. Diao wouldn’t let me touch.”

Director Wu smiled — a smile with something in it — and said, “If she wouldn’t let you touch them, it was for your own good.”

Cong Shasha said, “Director Wu, I’m twenty-eight this year. I worked at Yuanwang for seven years, my salary barely went up and my position never moved. I came out because I want to move up a bit. If you could give me a little guidance, that’s how I’ll make progress. I want to make progress too.”

Director Wu looked at her for a few seconds and said, “Shasha, you’re younger than Ms. Diao, and more genuine than her too.”

Cong Shasha said, “You flatter me, Director Wu.”

Director Wu said, “This Sixth Academy business — it’s not that I can’t take care of it. It’s that someone passed word that your Xinghuan has problems with its qualifications, not enough project experience. That word reached the top and got pushed down, so the project office has to follow procedure. And procedure takes time.”

Cong Shasha said, “The person who passed word — was it Ms. Diao?”

Director Wu didn’t answer directly. He picked up his cup and took another sip.

Cong Shasha said, “Director Wu, I followed Ms. Diao for seven years, and I know some things about her. When she was at Yuanwang, her relationship with Gao Fei wasn’t a matter of a day or two. They have a daughter who’s seven this year. To marry her, Gao Fei divorced his first wife and transferred all the equity into his own name. Did you know any of this?”

The hand in which Director Wu held his teacup stopped for a moment; he was clearly stunned by the news. “You’re saying Gao Fei and she are a couple, and the kid’s already seven? That’s explosive! How could Wang Yan possibly agree to a divorce?”

Cong Shasha: “Right — all of us thought it was explosive. And Ms. Diao has lots of other lovers too, not just Mr. Gao. The company’s head of technology got together with her as well, and even got divorced for her.” Shasha deliberately kept back the fact that the man who’d had a thing with her was none other than Zheng Zhiyong himself.

Director Wu’s face changed slightly. He took a tactical sip of tea, then went on to ask, “This I truly never imagined. When Little Diao came to see me, she always played the single woman. She told me the child was her ex-husband’s — she was already pregnant when they divorced, and she kept it. I always thought she was an independent, strong-willed single mother, so I looked out for her quite a bit. Is this news of yours all true?”

“Ms. Diao knows exactly what she wants. With the chairman, it’s purely about what she can get. She had the child to tie the chairman down, and later she forced her way into the top spot by squeezing out his wife. Everyone at our company knows that.”

Director Wu’s face didn’t look like someone listening to gossip; if anything, it looked like someone who was part of the gossip. Shasha went on, raising the stakes: “When she was at Yuanwang, she kept all the client resources clenched in her own hands. Her sales commission was more than two million a year, and she could get at the money on the company’s books too. She raked in a lot of money, and she has a lot of lovers. She doesn’t care about any of it at all.”

Director Wu said, “A lot of lovers — and Gao Fei would put up with that?”

Shasha said, “Director Wu, Ms. Diao has been living in Beijing for years. Gao Fei doesn’t spend more than a few days a year at her place. The rest of the time she’s on her own. Her lovers must be in Beijing — and some of them are our clients.”

Director Wu’s expression became a little unnatural.

Cong Shasha said, “Director Wu, I’m not telling you all this to stir up trouble. I want you to know that Ms. Diao passing word to block us isn’t for Yuanwang’s sake — it’s for her own. If Yuanwang loses this order, she can use it as a bargaining chip to negotiate with Gao Fei. But you’re different. You’re in this position; if the project stays stuck and the people above you come asking, the first person they’ll ask is you. She’s using you as her gun, and you’re counting the money for her on top of it — that’s a bad deal. And besides — Yuanwang, the state Yuanwang’s in, I don’t know if you’re aware: two hundred million in receivables, four hundred million in buyback debt, no hope of an IPO. That company’s funding chain is about to snap.”

Director Wu picked up his teacup but didn’t drink. He turned it in his hand and said, “Little Cong, is this information reliable?”

Cong Shasha said, “Director Wu, my information is absolutely reliable. I was at Yuanwang seven years, I know people up and down the company, and I’m very close with people in finance too. The company’s situation is — you could say there’s nobody in the whole Xiangcheng scene who doesn’t know. The company’s already been through several rounds of layoffs. Even if we didn’t do this project, Yuanwang taking it on would really just be planting a mine for the project owner.”

Director Wu looked at her for a good while, then suddenly smiled and said, “Little Cong, thank you for telling me all this. It’s enough for me to chew on for a while.”

Cong Shasha said, “Director Wu, I’ve known you for a few years now, and I don’t want to see the project owner deceived by Ms. Diao. Coming out of Yuanwang, I’m more or less starting my own business. Landing this order would definitely be a very important beginning for our company. Whoever helps us get started is definitely my great benefactor — and a benefactor like that is someone I could never forget as long as I live. We’d have to share in the good fortune together.”

Cong Shasha raised her glass and said, “Director Wu, here’s to you.”

Director Wu clinked glasses with her and drank. As he put his glass down, he said, “Little Cong, you’re not like Ms. Diao.”

Cong Shasha said, “Director Wu, the way I’m not like her is that I’d never use you as my gun.”

As the dinner was about to break up, Cong Shasha handed over a small paper bag and said, “Director Wu, this is some green tea I carried all the way from Xi’an especially for you. Please don’t make me take it back — it takes up too much room. It’s fresh pre-Qingming tea. Try some when you get home.” With that she gave Director Wu a wink, thinking that his driver was waiting downstairs. Director Wu didn’t decline. He took it, peeked inside, rolled up the top of the bag and kept hold of it, and said, “Thank you for the tea.”

Cong Shasha saw him to the door. Before he got into the car, Director Wu looked back at her and said, “Little Cong, about those materials — I’ll have someone take another look. Don’t go far these next few days. Wait for my call.”

Cong Shasha said, “All right. I’ll wait for your call.”

The car drove away. Cong Shasha stood at the restaurant entrance, the March night wind pouring in at her collar, and shivered and pulled her coat tight around her. She took out her phone and sent Zheng Zhiyong a WeChat: “Mr. Zheng, word’s been passed. He says he’ll have another look.”

Zheng Zhiyong replied very quickly: “What was his attitude?”

Cong Shasha replied: “There’s a chance. He told me to wait for his call.”

Zheng Zhiyong replied: “Good. Come back, and be careful on the way.”

Cong Shasha put her phone in her pocket and stood at the roadside waiting for a car. The wind lifted her hair, and she raised a hand to smooth it. She thought of the look in Director Wu’s eyes just now when he said, “You’re not like Ms. Diao,” and the thing held tight in her heart loosened a little. She thought: what Ms. Diao can do, I’m not necessarily unable to do. But then she thought at once: I won’t be another Diao Kexin. Diao Kexin uses men as ladders and throws them away once she’s climbed them. I won’t throw him away.

The car came. She opened the door, got in and gave the hotel’s address. The car headed east, the streetlights sweeping past one after another. She leaned back in the seat and closed her eyes, going over in her head every word and every expression of Director Wu’s at dinner. She thought: if this order can be turned around, she’ll have a firm footing at Xinghuan. And once she has a firm footing, then there’ll be something to talk about for what comes after.

When the car reached the hotel she got out, went into the lobby and up to the tenth floor. The corridor was very quiet. She went to her own door, swiped it open and went in, leaving the main light off and turning on only the bedside lamp. She sat on the edge of the bed, took off her heels, set her feet on the carpet and let out a long breath.

She took out her phone, went to Zheng Zhiyong’s WeChat and typed a line: “Mr. Zheng, I’m back. Tomorrow morning I’ll go to the project office and deliver the supplementary materials again.”

Zheng Zhiyong called her right away. “How come it’s ‘Mr. Zheng’ again?”

Shasha said, “Got it — from now on, to your face, I’ll call you Brother Yong.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Shasha, thanks for all your hard work today.”

Cong Shasha looked at this line for a few seconds and replied “It was nothing,” then put the phone down, lay back on the bed and looked at the ceiling. The lamplight fell on the wall, warm and yellow. She thought: I’ve been in this room three days now. Three days ago, when I checked in, I was only Zheng Zhiyong’s sales director. Three days later, I’m Zheng Zhiyong’s woman. But she knew that wasn’t enough. What she wanted to be wasn’t just Zheng Zhiyong’s woman. She wanted to be Xinghuan’s sales director, the one who could get things done for Zheng Zhiyong.

She rolled over and buried her face in the pillow, which smelled of hotel laundry detergent. She thought: Diao Kexin, back then, probably came up step by step just like this too. But she was different from Diao Kexin. In the end Diao Kexin had trampled everyone underfoot and climbed up by herself. She didn’t want to trample anyone. She wanted to climb up together with someone.

She closed her eyes and slept.

The next morning Cong Shasha went to the project office and delivered the supplementary materials again. Supervisor Zhou took them and said, “The earlier supplementary materials have already been reviewed. The leader said in person this morning that the qualifications issue isn’t a big problem. Give us another two or three days, and we’ll speed up the contract process on our end.” The string in Cong Shasha’s heart went slack for a moment, but it didn’t show on her face. She only said, “Thank you, Supervisor Zhou. Sorry for the trouble.”

When she came out of the project office she called Zheng Zhiyong. “Mr. Zheng, I delivered the same materials again. Supervisor Zhou says the leader has reviewed them and doesn’t think there’s much of a problem. Wait two or three days, and they’ll speed up the process.”

Zheng Zhiyong was silent on the other end for two seconds, then said, “Good. Shasha, come back, and let’s talk about the next step.”

Cong Shasha said, “Okay.”

When she hung up, she stood at the door of the project office. Beijing in March, and the wind was still cool, but the sun had come out, and it felt a little warm on her skin. She thought: this step was the right one.

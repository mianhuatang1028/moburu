Chapter Thirteen: Fei

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Fei” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Eastern Mountains, Book Four”): shaped like an ox with a white head, one eye and a snake’s tail; when it walks through water the water dries up, when it walks through grass the grass dies, and when it appears, a great plague sweeps the land. As the saying goes: wherever it treads, all withers — and no one yet knows it is the plague.

One telling sign of a boss’s ability to run a company is how good he is at getting the gossip from the bottom of the organization. A company runs by its own set of rules: once anything is known to more than three people inside it, it is no longer a secret but common knowledge. Yet the boss often sits inside a natural information cocoon — what people call “darkness right under the lamp.” Excellent managers are different. They have their own confidants and informants, and every matter, big or small, flows through their own secret reporting channels into the hub of their information network. Across history and around the world, it is outstanding women who seem to have set the outstanding examples here — Wu Zetian, say, or Catherine the Great.

Sister Yan’er really did well on this front. Because she controlled the company’s finance, personnel and administration, and because her information was so timely and complete, at times she seemed more like the company’s real manager. Every matter large or small, the state of every employee, even details about employees’ families — she knew far more than Gao Fei, and in far finer detail.

Of course, that is also exactly why, when Diao Kexin had a child right under her nose a little over a year after joining the company, Sister Yan’er only found out ten months after the baby was born — which still galls her to this day.

Eight years ago, Diao Kexin was introduced by her husband to the professor couple, Gao Fei and Sister Yan’er. Diao Kexin’s husband, young Professor Li, was then a junior lecturer in the same lab as Sister Yan’er’s husband, Gao Fei; in a sense Gao Fei was his academic mentor, as much teacher as friend. When Diao Kexin learned that her husband’s colleagues, this professor couple, had resigned to start a business, and that their company, Yuanwang, had that year reached a valuation of 1.5 billion and taken 200 million in investment, she looked at herself — at the time just an ordinary sales specialist at a foreign-trade company — and hoped her husband could get her into a job with better prospects. Her shy husband did his best to arrange a get-together to grant his wife’s wish. When the two couples met, Gao Fei and Sister Yan’er both took a great liking to this young, sweet couple, and so Diao Kexin got into Yuanwang in its early takeoff, starting as a junior salesperson.

In Diao Kexin’s first year the sales department was still at headquarters in Changsha. At the start of the second year Gao Fei said Beijing had more client resources and needed a sales team on the ground to respond quickly to service work, so he set up a Beijing branch, rented an office, and also rented a luxury villa in Beijing as the company’s hospitality clubhouse. Diao Kexin was transferred from Changsha to Beijing at that point and promoted to department manager. But not long after, she asked the company for leave, saying she’d broken her right leg and needed several months to recover. At first Sister Yan’er thought nothing of it. Some time later she heard that Diao Kexin’s husband had jumped from a building at the National University of Defense Technology; not knowing the inside story, Sister Yan’er sighed with her husband for quite a while over how such a young man could have depression and so on — and still thought nothing of it. Until one afternoon, while her husband was napping, Sister Yan’er saw his phone light up: Diao Kexin had sent a video of a baby.

The baby girl in the video was already ten months old, and that day she had learned to say “Daddy.” In the video Diao Kexin said happily to Gao Fei, “Gao Fei, listen — our daughter’s calling you Daddy!”

Only then did Sister Yan’er wake as if from a dream. She raged and fought with her husband, but the child born outside their marriage was already here; it was all a done deal.

Only afterwards did she learn that when Diao Kexin went to work in Beijing she was already six or seven months pregnant. Before that, when she went into the Changsha office every day, she’d bound her belly with a band, so tight that no one could see any sign of a pregnancy; when the belly got too big to hide, Gao Fei had launched the Beijing office so she could await the birth in Beijing under the cover of work; and when her due date came, she got a sick note saying her right leg was broken and stayed away from the office for months. The holes in the story were first spotted by people at the Beijing office — bold guesses, careful checking — and later the rumors spread to Changsha, but they never reached Sister Yan’er’s ears.

The irony couldn’t have been thicker: Diao Kexin got several months’ leave in the name of a broken right leg, while the person who had really broken his right leg was her husband, who had jumped from a building seven months before she gave birth. Poor Professor Li — that day he leapt, full of longing, from the high teaching building; not only did he shatter his right leg, he was left disabled for life, and limped from then on. But fate still smiled on him a little: who would have thought there’d be a large stand of woods below the spot where he jumped? The trees caught him and saved his life.

Having had her home base raided — a child born behind her back because her information was poor — Sister Yan’er learned her lesson and planted her own eyes and ears in Beijing. And since, as they say, a company’s secrets never stay between just three people, right as Old Zheng’s wife Zhang Jingyu was making her scene at the Beijing office, Sister Yan’er was getting complete live commentary, even illustrated with photos of the three-way standoff taken on the spot.

Sister Yan’er was riding to the office with Gao Fei at the time, reading it all in high glee. Gao Fei noticed and asked her, “What’s got you so happy?”

Sister Yan’er of course couldn’t say a thing, so she teased Gao Fei with a smile. “What business is it of yours what I’m happy about? Let me ask you instead — how’s your little lover these days? Is her heart still yours? Are you still sure of her?”

Gao Fei didn’t know why Sister Yan’er had said that. He thought back over Diao Kexin’s attitude toward him lately. Whenever they met, nothing seemed different; they were as intimate as often as ever, and their time together was still sweet. Apart from the times she couldn’t help pushing him to divorce again, most things were as usual. Though, now that he thought about it, there was something a little odd: these days, when she came to Changsha, she wasn’t as clingy as before; she often didn’t check in, and was often unreachable for days on end.

Gao Fei looked at Sister Yan’er and asked, “What else do you know?”

Sister Yan’er was determined never to tell Gao Fei. Since time immemorial, the enemy of my enemy has been my friend; what Sister Yan’er was thinking was that from now on she ought to make even better use of Zheng Zhiyong, and must not let Gao Fei find out what had happened at the Beijing office. “Zheng Zhiyong, oh, Zheng Zhiyong — who’d have thought you’d turn out this good? All these years of bad karma may get cleared away for me by this new COO.” The thought raised her fondness for Zheng Zhiyong by several more degrees.

So Gao Fei and Sister Yan’er arrived at the company, each with a scheme of their own. As soon as Gao Fei got to his office he called Diao Kexin. “Fly to Changsha right now.”

Gao Fei summoning and dismissing Diao Kexin at will had long been the norm in their years together. Ever since having the child, Diao Kexin had been set on stepping up to become his wife, and never crossing Gao Fei was the foundation she lived by. So she threw a few things together and went straight to the airport.

But right at the boarding gate for Changsha, she felt she still couldn’t let go of Zheng Zhiyong — this foolish man who loved her so. Now that his wife had hauled him home, would he still be her spare tire forever, as before? Diao Kexin couldn’t bear to give him up either; she was even afraid his wife would forgive him and take him back, afraid he’d be won over by twenty years of feeling and choose to go back to his wife and son. She knew all too well what kind of news hit a man madly in love the hardest. She had tried this very move on Gao Fei once, but back then she’d been one move short: Gao Fei’s wife had too firm a grip on the company, he couldn’t divorce her then, and it had dragged on for seven years until now.

So she picked up the phone and said that line to Old Zheng: “I think I’m pregnant.” It was, of course, an out-and-out lie, and Diao Kexin knew it; she didn’t even stop to think whether Old Zheng would believe it. She just wanted to use the line to buy herself some time with him — to drop one more weight on the scale of his feelings.

But a man fallen into foolish love would rather believe a lie. Or perhaps, deep in his heart, he had already made his choice.

When Diao Kexin landed it was already four in the afternoon. Gao Fei had sent a driver to meet her and booked her a hotel room in advance. When Diao Kexin arrived, she took the key card and walked into the room; Gao Fei was already there, waiting for her.

“Has something changed with you lately? Are you hiding something from me?” Gao Fei asked bluntly.

Diao Kexin said, “I’m not hiding anything from you.” She walked in with feigned ease, put down her LV handbag and sat on the sofa with her legs crossed; she was wearing a skirt, and the view beneath it was half-hidden, half-revealed. She looked at Gao Fei and said, “But I really am unhappy with you, and I can’t take it anymore. Our daughter starts primary school this fall — when are you going to sort out her hukou and send her to school? You promised before — this Mid-Autumn Festival — will you have the divorce done by then?”

Gao Fei said, “Every time we see each other, it’s this. Is this the only thing left for us to talk about?”

Diao Kexin: “No. I’ve just waited too long. When I had this child, you promised to marry me, promised to take good care of our daughter, but I’ve waited too long and you still haven’t kept your promise. I don’t want to wait anymore. I don’t want to be lied to by you anymore.”

Gao Fei said, “I haven’t lied to you. I’m in a difficult position. I’m working hard on it.”

Diao Kexin said lightly, “If you can’t get divorced, then set me free.”

Gao Fei: “So you’ve been cold to me lately — have you found someone?”

Diao Kexin: “I’ve never been short of admirers. You have no idea how many clients, how many of your friends, have told me they’re interested and come after me.”

Gao Fei showed a cold disdain. As an old man of nearly fifty-five, no one understood better than he did what a young woman’s few words of admiration were worth.

Diao Kexin: “If you don’t care, then I’ll find myself a boyfriend — find your child a stepfather and let her take someone else’s surname. I can find a man with a Beijing hukou and marry him; then my daughter will have a registered birth, a Beijing hukou, she can go to school, and I’ll have someone to lean on.”

To Gao Fei her tone didn’t sound like she really had someone else, but more like an attempt to goad him. He heard the bitterness in it and smiled with relief; he walked over, bent down in front of her, and said gently, “Aren’t I the one you lean on?” And with that he took her in his arms.

The moment Diao Kexin was taken into Gao Fei’s warm, smiling embrace, she let out a quiet breath inside. Eight years with this man — she knew his character like the back of her hand. He was domineering and headstrong, with an extreme possessiveness and confidence when it came to women. He was tall and handsome; even past fifty he was still a very handsome, lean and healthy scholar-gentleman. How many women had truly admired him, how many beauties had thrown themselves at him out of worship for success? He believed any woman he loved could only love him. Take his first wife, Sister Yan’er — so what if she knew he’d had a child outside? Their shared business, the billions in shared wealth: she made a fuss for a few days and then it all quieted down, and as before she cooperated with Gao Fei in putting on a show of the loving couple and happy family in public. Especially when Gao Fei’s parents died, five years and three years ago, Yan’er saw to everything, dressed in mourning white, without the slightest misstep. Even to their child, now at university, the two of them were still the most loving couple in the world.

So he believed he had Diao Kexin wrapped around his finger too. Back then he had bought the expectant Little Diao a flat of nearly two hundred square meters in Beijing, hired a nanny to look after her family and household, and each year, under the label of sales commission, paid her more than three million yuan in support; he had entrusted her with managing his most important networks and relationships, and in fact she had long been the mistress of his Beijing operations. What more could she want? He believed this woman had long since been completely mastered by his money and his charm. He was still basking in how this little lover had first declared her love for him — that blindly worshipful look, that utterly devoted love, those confessions of being besotted with his looks, that wild sex. He felt this little lover was a plaything in his palm; hearing her talk sourly about finding his child a stepfather, he heard nothing in it but jealousy and resentment. After all, this was a woman he had fed with perfect sex, day and night, and more than three million a year — how could she not be satisfied?

And she had borne him a daughter, now nearly seven; her career, her body, her time and her freedom were all entirely in his hands. What woman could break free of shackles like that? Besides, even if she wanted to see something of the wider world, she was thirty-five now, divorced, with the hair-raising dark history of an ex-husband who had jumped off a building over her and been left disabled for life, plus an illegitimate daughter whose father nobody knew. What kind of foolish man would truly fall in love with a woman like that?

Yet this time he truly underestimated Diao Kexin. She seemed to be firmly in his grasp, but she had not only an ambition for money and for climbing the social ladder far beyond anything he could imagine; she had also been honing the art of controlling men’s minds since middle school. Starting with her very first boyfriend, she had discovered life’s shortcut: if a man fell for her body because of his hormones, he would, for as long as it lasted, willingly give her lots and lots of money and gifts. She had spent her four years at university putting her gift into practice; from an extremely poor girl to where she was today, she would seize the heart of every man she could seize, to help her realize her burning dream of rising in the world.

Through her first romance, the countless romances at university, and all the years of her first marriage, she had pushed and pulled, advanced step by step, and her techniques for stirring a man’s emotions and physical passion had been refined to the point of perfection. So when she met Gao Fei and found herself, for the first time in her life, so close to a real hundred-millionaire, it had taken her only a few months to make Gao Fei hers and get pregnant with his child, and she kept steering him to give more and more — a home, a car, an ever larger yearly allowance. She had also given birth to the child. To her, this child was her stake, her wager: she was betting that through this child she could hold this man fast, become his wife, and marry into wealth for good.

That consummate skill was why, when Gao Fei put his arms around her, Diao Kexin first put on a little show of pique and pushed him away a little, which very quickly roused his male drive. Before long Gao Fei had pushed her down onto the sofa and pulled down her skirt, and once again she delivered a Best Actress–worthy performance of utter intoxication and satisfaction, leaving Gao Fei more besotted with her than ever.

Huan

Chapter Twenty-Five: Huan

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Huan” — In ancient times the word named a beast, and it also names worry and trouble. Trouble is born in the heart; it does not come from outside. As the saying goes: the one in the thick of it suspects nothing; the onlooker already sees it all.

The day after the dinner with Zhu Yiwen, Zheng Zhiyong went back to Xi’an. By now he was brimming with confidence about blocking Zhu Yiwen’s partnership with Gao Fei and turning Zhu Yiwen into his own partner. That clink of cups when Zhu Yiwen gave his answer — Zheng Zhiyong already counted it as a toast to their alliance. He was sure that within three days Zhu Yiwen would give him a firm yes.

That day Zheng Zhiyong saw on WeChat Moments that a very important friend of his was in Xi’an, so he called him to ask about his plans and invited him to come see his company the next afternoon and then have dinner together.

The friend was Lu You, a business owner with more than ten years of experience building companies. In Xi’an he had a machining company in the defense industry, founded with a few of his university classmates, in which he was by far the largest shareholder. So Lu You usually spent a week or two in Xi’an every month anyway. It was both his alma mater’s city and the home base of his business — a second hometown of sorts.

And it was this same friend who, three months earlier — when Zheng Zhiyong was more than a hundred thousand in debt, with no prospects at all and no idea where his income would come from — had lent him 100,000 yuan.

And it was also this same friend, Lu You, who was the completely unsuspecting cousin of Zheng Zhiyong’s ex-girlfriend’s ex-husband. He had no idea that the woman in Old Zheng’s hair-raising love affair was his own cousin Li Fulang’s wife. Still less did he know that his onetime cousin-in-law had now married his cousin’s own colleague and mentor — and that the person who had brought the two of them together in the first place was his cousin himself.

Lu You had heard Zheng Zhiyong mention it briefly on the phone the moment he got this job in Xi’an, and he was more than a little happy about the reversal in his friend’s fortunes. Now Zheng Zhiyong called to invite him himself. Zheng Zhiyong said Xinghuan had given him a car for everyday use — a white Land Cruiser Prado, supposedly, that usually just sat at the company and was now assigned to him. Old Zheng said he insisted on driving over himself to pick Lu You up at his hotel, bring him to the company for a while first, and then they’d go to dinner together.

Lu You gladly agreed, and the two of them arranged to meet at three in the afternoon at Lu You’s hotel.

At three o’clock Lu You’s phone rang. It was an unfamiliar number, and as usual Lu You hung up without thinking, without answering, until the same number had rung several more times. Then he picked up.

“Hello, is this Mr. Lu? I’m Wu Peng, Mr. Zheng’s assistant. Mr. Zheng sent me to pick you up.”

“Why didn’t Mr. Zheng come himself?” Lu You asked.

“Something came up for Mr. Zheng at the last minute and he can’t get away from the office, so he’s arranged for me to pick you up first.”

Right after that, Lu You saw the little assistant, Wu Peng, walking toward him — a hundred and ninety centimeters tall, slim, handsome, with a fine bearing.

Lu You followed him and got into Wu Peng’s car, which was that very white Toyota Prado. At first neither of them said anything. Then Lu You had a coughing fit, and Wu Peng immediately adjusted the air conditioning and asked considerately, “Is this temperature all right?” Lu You thought the young man was surprisingly thoughtful, so he struck up a conversation with him and learned that he was twenty-eight this year and had studied finance at King’s College London, a combined bachelor’s and master’s. After coming back from abroad he’d joined a quant trading firm, and he had been working with Wu You for more than four years now; before this he had been Wu You’s business assistant.

“That’s one hell of a school you graduated from — truly one of the top universities in the world. Then you’re too good for this, young Mr. Peng. Being an assistant is a waste of your talent!”

“No, no, working with Mr. Zheng I’m learning too,” Wu Peng answered modestly.

“Young Mr. Peng, your surname’s Wu too — you wouldn’t be related to Mr. Wu You somehow?” Lu You asked.

Wu Peng answered, “No, no, it’s just a coincidence that the boss and I are both named Wu. The year I graduated and was looking for work, my family did happen to know Mr. Wu, so they introduced me, and I was lucky enough to get to work and learn under him.”

Lu You asked again, “So how did you end up as Zheng Zhiyong’s assistant?”

Wu Peng answered, “Well, when Mr. Zheng joined the company he needed someone to help him get to know how things stood, and since I’d been with Mr. Wu before and know the company’s affairs fairly well, they assigned me to be Mr. Zheng’s assistant.”

Lu You tested the waters now. “Do you know about all that stuff with Old Zheng?”

Wu Peng answered, “Ha, I know a bit. When Mr. Zheng first came to the company he wasn’t in a good way, and for a few nights I went drinking with him. He told me some things. Oh, and back when we were doing business with that girlfriend of his, I was the one handling it, so I’ve met her too.”

Lu You said, “Is Zheng Zhiyong doing a bit better lately?”

Wu Peng said, “He should be doing better. Mr. Lu, I’ve heard Mr. Zheng say you’re his most important friend. When you see him in a bit you can ask him yourself.”

Wu Peng soon drove Lu You to the small building Zheng Zhiyong had once told Lu You about. Before getting out, he looked at his phone, then started the engine again and said to Lu You, “Sorry, Mr. Lu, Mr. Zheng wants us to go to a hotel nearby and wait for him to come over. It’s probably still not a good time for him right now.”

Lu You had no choice but to go along with his host’s arrangements. He took one look at the outside of Xinghuan’s small detached building, and then they were off again. Wu Peng took Lu You to a hotel nearby and said, “Mr. Lu, Mr. Zheng still needs to meet with Mr. Wu for a while. I’ll sit with you in the lobby bar here for now. Our company’s pretty remote, too far from downtown. Sorry, there just aren’t as many good places to choose from out here as there are in the city.”

It was a perfectly middling four-star hotel, a bit dated by the look of it but reasonably clean and tidy, and the lobby bar’s afternoon tea had a bit of character to it. Wu Peng ordered and paid, and sat down with Lu You, and the two of them got to gossiping about Zheng Zhiyong again. Lu You said, “Last time, from the way Old Zheng talked, he still couldn’t forget that girl. As far as you know, how’s he thinking about it lately?”

Wu Peng laughed. “I’d say he still can’t forget her.”

Lu You said, “I get the feeling that girl just treated him as a spare tire. She never really had feelings for him.”

Wu Peng said, “Actually, all of us think so. But when people are in love, they really just won’t see sense.”

Lu You said, “I tried to talk him out of it before, too. No use at all.”

Wu Peng said, “Every one of us has tried talking to him. It’s no use. Come nighttime, he still says he misses her.”

Lu You said, “You can’t wake someone who’s only pretending to be asleep.”

Wu Peng: “That girl’s a pro. You’re an amateur player, and you go in that deep — of course you’re going to get wiped out.”

Lu You nodded and thought maybe he’d better change the subject, so he said, “This morning on the phone Old Zheng told me the company’s business is going quite well — he may land a 20-million deal. That’s pretty good. I hope he just puts his whole heart into the business and stops thinking about other things.”

Wu Peng said, “Exactly. A man’s got to get things done. Feelings and all that are a sideline. When the career’s going well, the rest doesn’t matter so much.”

Lu You laughed. “Pretty sharp of you to see that at your age.” He paused, then went on, “How’s that order coming along right now?”

Wu Peng said, “It’s still moving forward. The competition is Yuanwang, and there’s a key subcontractor in the project, Gaoda. Mr. Zheng has already talked with them, and according to Mr. Zheng he’s already got Gaoda locked in.”

Lu You was in the defense industry after all, and though his company didn’t mainly do aerospace, Gaoda was a big name, so he’d heard of it too. So he said, “Locking in Gaoda — that’s a really impressive milestone. Mr. Zheng’s got some pull after all.”

Wu Peng, with his high EQ, chimed in, “Mr. Zheng really is extremely strong technically.”

The two of them made small talk back and forth for more than an hour in the coffee shop of this hotel near Xinghuan, and still there was no sign of Old Zheng coming. Partway through, Lu You said he was going to the restroom, meaning to pay the bill while he was at it, and found Wu Peng had already paid. When he came back he asked Wu Peng, “Mr. Zheng set the time for this afternoon himself, with me, this morning. It’s already six in the evening. How come he still isn’t here?”

Wu Peng said, “I’m really sorry, Mr. Lu. Let me apologize on Mr. Zheng’s behalf. It’s probably still about this project. Mr. Zheng got Gaoda as a subcontractor, and at noon, when he was reporting on the project to Mr. Wu in a meeting, I was there and overheard — I think Mr. Wu felt there was some problem with the commercial terms of the subcontract. After that I came to get you. When I left, Mr. Zheng was still in Mr. Wu’s office talking with him privately. I don’t know what happened after that.”

As they were talking, Zheng Zhiyong at last turned up, very late. The moment he saw Lu You he said, “Old Lu, I’m so sorry, so sorry. Look at me, this late — I kept you waiting so long.”

Lu You said, deliberately, in front of Wu Peng, “It’s nothing, nothing. Little Peng’s been keeping me company the whole time. We had a great chat. This kid’s EQ is off the charts, and he really knows how to look after people.”

Old Zheng said, looking a bit pleased with himself, “Shows how well I’ve trained him.”

Lu You glanced at Wu Peng. Across from him the young man’s face was still the same smiling face, without the slightest change. So Lu You went on, “You being this busy just means the business is going well. That’s a good thing. It’s rare for the two of us to get together in Xi’an tonight. We’ve got to have a good drink and a good talk.”

Wu Peng said he’d already picked a long-established restaurant near Mr. Lu’s hotel, so Wu Peng drove the three of them to the restaurant for dinner. They ordered a bottle of red wine. As they sat down, Old Zheng found he was out of cigarettes and sent Wu Peng off to buy him some. During the meal Zheng Zhiyong told Lu You over and over that there were only a handful of people who’d helped him when he hit rock bottom, at the lowest point in his life, and he’d never forget them as long as he lived.

When they got to the company’s business, Old Zheng was very happy and confident. “It took me, this old man, only a few months to hit bottom and bounce back, to rise again like the man from Dongshan. I’m going to show that woman what I can do. I’m going to get back at Yuanwang, beat the hell out of Gao Fei. I’m going to pin Yuanwang to the ground and pound it, beat Gao Fei till he’s hunting for his teeth on the floor.”

Lu You laughed and said, “Mr. Zheng, there’s actually something I don’t quite get. You’re the one who took Gao Fei’s girlfriend, aren’t you? And Gao Fei was the man who spotted your talent, back in your career. Where’s all this hatred for him coming from?”

Old Zheng said, “I just hate Gao Fei. I want revenge.”

Lu You and Wu Peng glanced at each other, and Lu You went on, “Then let me say something as a joke — don’t take it to heart. Gao Fei isn’t your enemy. If anything he’s the one who discovered you. Whereas you — didn’t you go and take the man’s woman? First you take the man’s girlfriend, and next you’re planning to take his wife.”

All three of them burst out laughing. Old Zheng said, laughing, “You know what — you know, you’re actually right. Looks like that’s how it is!”

After that they got to talking about Qing history, about some stories of the Qing emperors. Old Zheng and Lu You had both dabbled in history, so naturally the conversation never ran dry. By the time they’d gone from the Yongzheng Emperor to the Guangxu Emperor, Lu You took the opening to say, “Mr. Zheng, I think your situation right now is basically Guangxu’s, and Mr. Wu is the Empress Dowager Cixi ruling from behind the curtain at your back. I don’t know if the comparison’s apt.”

Zheng Zhiyong answered, “No. I’m the emperor, and he’s the Retired Emperor above me.”

Wu Peng had to drive Lu You back to his hotel later, so he didn’t drink during the meal. Old Zheng drank two-thirds of the bottle. After a few rounds he was in high spirits, a little tipsy and riding high on his mood — one moment full of boundless confidence in the future of his business, the next dreaming about beating Gao Fei and snatching that woman back. With the drink in him, Old Zheng said in a burst of excitement, “This project, going head-to-head with Yuanwang — it’s just fate. I’m going to beat them. I’m going to beat them again and again, take his business away from him, and take that woman back. Then the decision will be in my hands. Once I’ve made it, that woman will definitely come back to me like a good girl. And whether I still want her then — that’ll be my call.”

Lu You saw that Old Zheng was by now half drunk and half crazy, so he suggested that since they’d all had plenty to eat and drink tonight, they might as well call it a night. All their future home bases were in Xi’an anyway, and they’d be coming and going a lot; there’d be plenty of chances to meet. He called the waiter over for the bill and found Wu Peng had quietly paid already.

Wu Peng drove Zheng Zhiyong and Lu You back to Lu You’s hotel. The windows were open all the way, and the smell of liquor in the car gradually cleared. Soon they were back at Lu You’s hotel in central Xi’an. Lu You was staying at the Canopy by Hilton in the city center. When Wu Peng pulled up at the hotel entrance, Lu You said to him, “Mr. Wu Peng, thanks for all your trouble today. You head home. Mr. Zheng and I are two old friends — we’ll have another drink in the lobby bar at my hotel and catch up some more.”

Old Zheng waved for Wu Peng to go on and said to him, “Take a cab home. I’ll get a designated driver to take the car back later. Oh, and later on book my plane ticket for tomorrow’s trip and send it to me.”

Then Wu Peng left. It was half past ten by now, and the two old friends sat down in the hotel’s empty lobby bar. Lu You ordered a Coke and a cocktail. When the waiter brought them, Lu You passed the Coke to Zheng Zhiyong and said, “Old Zheng, you’ve had plenty tonight. Have this.” He himself picked up the house special, a brandy-based drink, between his fingers, took a sip and said, “Old Zheng, we’re good friends, and I can’t help saying a few more things to you. I don’t know if it’ll seem a little out of the blue.”

Old Zheng was by now a lot more sober than before, and said quickly, “Old Lu, with what we are to each other, you can say anything at all. You’re an entrepreneur yourself. I’m all ears, really.”

Old Lu said, “Today, when you couldn’t get out of the office for so long — was there some problem with Mr. Wu at the company?”

Old Zheng said, “Ugh, don’t get me started. I found a subcontractor, right? And this subcontractor is absolutely crucial to whether we can win the order and cut Yuanwang down. So I went and reported it to him. Mr. Wu felt that we’d gone and talked about the split before the work had even been divided up specifically, and that it wasn’t reasonable, so, so, so he chewed me out!”

Lu You said, “What split did you tell him?”

Old Zheng said, “I told him I’d give them forty percent of the revenue.”

Lu You asked again, “Roughly what proportion of the cost is each side putting in? And what’s the estimated gross margin on the project as a whole?”

Old Zheng said, “We’d put in roughly ninety percent of the cost. The project’s gross margin is around 60 percent.”

On an earlier phone call Old Zheng had already told Lu You how he’d won over the technical heavyweight Zhang Hailu, so Lu You knew that Zheng Zhiyong had long since promised the whole team a bonus pool of 10 percent to share out after the project. By now Lu You had run the whole project’s financials through his head in a flash, and he understood the disagreement between Old Zheng and his boss, Wu You, that afternoon.

The whole project’s estimated revenue was around 20 million, with costs at forty percent, or 8 million. Of that, Zheng Zhiyong’s side would carry 90 percent of the cost, about 7.2 million yuan, and Gaoda 10 percent, about 800,000 yuan. On the revenue side, Gaoda would take forty percent, about 8 million yuan, which put its gross margin on this project at an astonishing 90 percent, while the remaining revenue was 12 million, which for Old Zheng meant a gross margin of just 40 percent. And by the rules of the defense industry, the hidden costs during the early stages of a project ran to about 5 to 10 percent; team members would take 10 percent; outside collaborators would take 5 percent; VAT was 13 percent; and on top of that came fixed expenses — rent, salaries, travel and day-to-day costs. Lu You thought it over and understood exactly what Wu You was thinking. Done this way, never mind making money — one little slip and they could easily lose plenty. Lu You couldn’t help being shocked inside.

So Lu You said, “Old Zheng, do you remember something I once said to you? That the biggest challenge in being Xinghuan’s general manager this time would actually come from changing the way you think. And if you remember back further, to when we talked before, I told you straight out back then that the two of us think differently.”

Old Zheng asked, “Differently how?”

Lu You said, “The difference between a boss’s thinking and a professional manager’s thinking — or, the gap between a boss’s thinking and a wage worker’s thinking. Wu You gave you the post of Xinghuan’s general manager, and he was bold enough to break the mold and give you full authority over finance, personnel, everything. That really is a rare opportunity. You’ve got to make the most of it, and quickly adjust the way you think from the professional manager’s thinking you had before to a boss’s thinking.”

Old Zheng went on to ask, “How do you mean, specifically?”

Lu You continued, “Take this case of yours. I think you handled it very immaturely. If it were me handling a business partnership like this, I’d definitely establish intent first. Once both sides confirm intent, you do a careful costing, confirm the division of work and what each puts in, and only then set the rules for the split. In other words, the more flexible you keep what you say up front, the more room you’ll have to maneuver later. Giving away forty percent like this without doing the math — as business conduct, it doesn’t look very mature. If you take on a project this big and don’t make a cent, or even lose money, that’s definitely a sign of real immaturity.”

“Second,” Lu You went on, “even though Wu You has given you the power to run the whole show, so far you two have only been working together for two or three months, and there’s still a while to go before you land your first order. You need to set your attitude straight. Even if he’s made you general manager, you have to understand that you’re not the actual controller yet — he is. You need to show him enough respect. Something like this time, where you went ahead on your own without asking him first — that’s a serious taboo too.”

Old Zheng said, “Old Lu, you’re right. I really was in the wrong. I’d never have guessed that without knowing the full picture at all, you’d work out every bit of my disagreement with Mr. Wu. That’s exactly what we spent the whole afternoon on. I kept explaining my reasons for doing it, hoping he’d understand.”

Lu You answered, “Explain what? There’s no excuse at all. You were completely wrong on this one. If you could do things with an entrepreneur’s thinking, this would never have happened in the first place. You have to work out ahead of time, on the company’s behalf, what the best approach is, and do the math down to the last detail. One more thing: Wu You — giving you a heaven-sent fortune like that, pouring down on you out of the sky, when you had nothing at all — he’s the great benefactor of your life. In other words, whether you can turn things around from here depends crucially, for your whole life, on keeping things good with him. You could even say that at this stage, the only person who matters to you is Wu You. If I were you, I’d keep firmly in mind that right now nobody matters more to you than he does. Whatever it takes, in method and in attitude, you need to deepen your relationship with him further, deepen the trust between you.”

“You have to understand that technical ability is never indispensable, and running a business doesn’t look like your strong suit for now either. So right now your only value to him is loyalty — being someone he can trust.”

“Women, love and all that — I think you’d better stop thinking about it so much. Whether you get her back or not, all that crap — how much does it really matter? Strictly speaking you’ve only been at the company two or three months. By rights this should still be the honeymoon period. You shouldn’t be spending this much time arguing, getting into a stupid disagreement like this over something like this.” Lu You finished the rest of his cocktail and said leisurely, “I’ve had a bit to drink. If I’ve put it too strongly, I hope you won’t take it to heart.”

Old Zheng was completely sober now, as if waking from a dream. “Hearing you say all that, I truly, completely understand — I was dead wrong. All I had in my head then was my hatred of Gao Fei. I was so set on pulling the firewood out from under his pot that I never did the math properly. That really was too immature! I really was dead wrong! Do you think it’d be all right for me to call Mr. Wu now?”

Before Lu You could answer, Old Zheng took out his phone and dialed. “Mr. Wu, you’re still up?”

“Mr. Wu, I’m calling because I’ve been reflecting all evening on our discussion in your office this afternoon. I was really so stupid. I made a lot of mistakes, and I’m calling to admit them to you. Both in how I handled the business and in going ahead without reporting to you first, I really made huge mistakes. I sincerely ask your forgiveness.”

The person on the other end was clearly pleased with this sudden, sincere apology and began replying at great length on his end of the line. Old Zheng answered devoutly, “Mm. Mm, you’re right. It definitely won’t happen again.”

After a while more, Old Zheng said into the phone, “Mr. Wu, giving me a chance like this at the darkest time of my life — you’re the greatest and only benefactor I’ve ever had. Even if I spent the rest of my life serving you heart and soul, it wouldn’t be enough to repay you for recognizing me. Believe me, I’ll repay you, and all of Xinghuan’s shareholders, with everything I’ve got and complete loyalty.”

Lu You listened, then quietly gave a little wave and left Old Zheng, who was still on the phone. Old Zheng, still talking, waved goodbye back.

Lu You quietly settled the bill, then stepped into the elevator and went back up to his business suite on the thirtieth floor. After this evening, Lu You saw clearly that Zheng Zhiyong, who had become general manager by pure luck for now, still had a long road ahead of him. And deep in his heart he sincerely wished that he could grow up as quickly as possible.

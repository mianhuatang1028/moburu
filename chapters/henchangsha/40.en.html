Chapter Forty: Ao

“Ao” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Western Mountains”), on Jade Mountain there is a beast called the ao, shaped like a dog with a human face; when it sees people it laughs, and it moves like the wind.

The next day Shasha was planning to go to Beijing. In the morning she was still at the company receiving some leaders visiting from out of town, kept them company at lunch, and in the afternoon rushed off to Beijing. She’d asked Old Zheng for his hotel address, meaning to go straight there. But then Old Zheng said he had nothing on tonight, and offered to come to the airport and pick Shasha up himself — something he rarely did.

On the way back to the hotel, the two of them sat in the back of the car. Old Zheng said he’d seen Dr. Xiao the day before; Dr. Xiao had been pretty happy after getting her share of the money. He made no mention at all of her getting drunk or of him taking her home. Cong Shasha leaned against the car window, looking out at the streets; after a while she closed her eyes and leaned back in the seat. Old Zheng kept quiet and let her sleep a bit.

At the hotel, the two of them went into the room. Cong Shasha opened her suitcase, took off her coat and changed into comfortable loungewear. She sat in the chair by the window. Old Zheng sat on the edge of the bed and took off his shoes.

Cong Shasha spoke first. She said, “Brother Yong, what made you think of picking me up today?”

Old Zheng said, “I had nothing on tonight.”

Cong Shasha said, “There’s suddenly something I want to ask you.”

Old Zheng said, ask.

Cong Shasha said, “Maybe I’m overthinking it, but we’ve been together more than half a year now. We’ve never actually said we’re boyfriend and girlfriend. What I want to ask you today is — how do you see it?”

Old Zheng was silent for a while, then said, “At the start, I felt that you were the one who didn’t want to make it that kind of relationship. Has something changed in how you’re thinking now?”

Cong Shasha said, “We’ve never said in so many words that we’re boyfriend and girlfriend, but from the time I got together with you till today, the only man I’ve been involved with is you. Do you understand?”

Old Zheng said, “I don’t have anyone else either.”

Cong Shasha said, “If I asked you today to make it official that we’re boyfriend and girlfriend, what would you think?”

Old Zheng said, “How do you define boyfriend and girlfriend?”

Cong Shasha said, “A one-on-one relationship. Two people faithful to each other, who can make demands, who give things and compromise. But not public, and not thinking about the future. The future can wait till it comes.”

Old Zheng said, “We’ve been getting on really well this whole time, and I don’t have anyone else. As far as I’m concerned, that’s already been done.”

She stood up, went over to the bed and began undressing. She took off the loose loungewear, revealing a matching black lace bra and briefs underneath, turned her head and said, “Brother Yong, do you think I’m beautiful?”

Old Zheng said, “Very beautiful.”

Cong Shasha said, “Do you like doing it with me?”

Old Zheng said, “I love it. Otherwise why would we be together every single day on our trips? At the company, whenever you come to find me, you’re always welcome.”

Cong Shasha said, “Then why have you never asked me whether I want to be your girlfriend?”

Old Zheng said, “The first and second times, I thought that what you wanted was exactly this kind of relationship — no strings, nothing official. And the longer it went on, the more I came to feel that a no-strings, nothing-official relationship was pretty good too. Especially since we’re at the same company — our positions and our relationship are too delicate. If we didn’t keep the right distance, I was afraid it would hurt the company’s business. You know that running this company right now, I’m sitting on pins and needles, walking on thin ice. If Wu You ever found out I hadn’t done a good job, and that I’d eaten the grass by my own burrow on top of it, or that I’d handled a relationship badly and you’d left the company over something, I’d be finished. That’s how I’ve been thinking about it.”

Cong Shasha said, “Then why have you kept on doing it with me? And never turned me down.”

Old Zheng said, “At the start, I just wanted to look after you, make you happy. But later I felt we got more and more in tune. I like you too, and I’d like us to settle down like this.”

Cong Shasha said, “Do you remember what I told you back then?”

Old Zheng said, “What?”

Cong Shasha said, “I made only one demand: don’t hide anything from me — tell me.”

Old Zheng said he wasn’t hiding anything from her. In his mind he ran over Xiaoya, ran over Xiao Jing lying on the bed with her lashes flickering, and then said firmly, “That I can definitely do.”

Shasha looked steadily at Old Zheng. “Really?”

Old Zheng said, “Really.”

Inside she gave a cold little laugh, but she kept her expression under control. She said, “All right. I believe you.”

Then she went into the bathroom and shut the door. The sound of water started up. Old Zheng sat on the edge of the bed and smoked a cigarette. When he’d finished, he stubbed it out in the ashtray.

Cong Shasha came out of the bathroom wrapped in a bath towel, her hair blown dry, went to the bed and sat down. She didn’t look at Old Zheng. With her back to him, she pulled back the covers and lay down under them.

Old Zheng said, “Shasha.”

She stayed as she was, back to him. Old Zheng lay down beside her and pulled at her with his hand, and slowly she turned over.

Old Zheng kissed her.

She closed her eyes and, without a word, quietly went along with it.

Two days later, Old Zheng, Xiao Jing and Shasha had dinner, the three of them. It was Xiao Jing who’d suggested it, and she’d chosen the place too — a restaurant doing Shandong cuisine over in Xicheng, with a private room that wasn’t big, one round table just right for five or six. Old Zheng and Cong Shasha got there first, and Dr. Xiao came later. When she pushed open the door and came in, Cong Shasha looked up at her.

Dr. Xiao still had her short hair today, still no makeup, and wore a thin dark gray blazer over a white shirt and dark trousers — clean and crisp from head to toe. She saw Cong Shasha, nodded, and said, “Ms. Cong.”

Cong Shasha said, “Dr. Xiao, you’re here. Come sit down.”

Shasha did the ordering: squirrel-shaped mandarin fish, four-joy meatballs, celery with lily bulbs, kung pao prawns, Yipin tofu and hibiscus chicken slices — not too many dishes and not too few, meat and vegetables both. When she’d finished, she handed the menu back.

Xiao Jing said to Zheng Zhiyong, “Mr. Zheng, I’ve read the technical proposal. There are still a few places that need changing. You’ve set the confidence threshold for target detection too high. At night, in low light, you’ll miss detections. It needs to come down — use a dynamic threshold that adjusts itself according to scene brightness.”

Old Zheng said, “I agree with the dynamic-threshold idea. But how much to lower it — there has to be some basis for that. Lower it too much and the false positives go up.”

Dr. Xiao said, “Between false positives and missed detections, you take a balance point. In your scenario, a missed detection costs more than a false positive. Miss one and it might be an accident. A false positive is a false alarm at worst. So push the threshold low — better a false positive than a miss.”

Old Zheng said, “Makes sense. I’ll tell Hailu and have him adjust it.”

Dr. Xiao said, “Also, the fine-tuning data for your dual branch is written too vaguely. If anyone asks at evaluation, you have to be able to answer with specific numbers. How many infrared images, how many synthetic images, what learning rate, how many iterations.”

Old Zheng said, “I’ll get Hailu to put together a detailed one.”

Dr. Xiao said, “When it’s done, send it to me and I’ll look it over.”

The two of them went back and forth, one line after another, talking smoothly. Cong Shasha sat beside them listening, and didn’t cut in. When the food came she patted Old Zheng on the back, put a bite of food in his bowl, then poured herself a cup of tea and drank.

Xiao Jing suddenly stopped talking and watched Shasha adding food to Old Zheng’s bowl. Watching the look on his face, she seemed to sense something.

Not that she saw anything specific — she saw a closeness and an understanding, the two of them like partners, without that layer of “boss and employee” between them.

She said, “Look at me, so busy talking business I’ve been neglecting our beautiful lady here. Let’s eat. From now on, no more talking about work.”

Cold sweat ran down Old Zheng’s back. These two women, each with a secret of her own — had they seen something in each other? Old Zheng looked at their two faces, and both seemed perfectly at ease. Dr. Xiao put a piece of fish in Old Zheng’s bowl too, and said, “I remember you’re quite fond of fish, aren’t you?” Old Zheng said he was.

The atmosphere was delicate, and time passed very slowly. At last the meal was over. Under that much pressure, Old Zheng felt as if every one of his insides had been shifted out of place.

When they’d finally finished eating, the three of them went out together. Cong Shasha and Old Zheng walked Dr. Xiao to the door and watched her get into a car and leave.

On the way back to the hotel, the two of them sat in the back of the car and hardly talked. Then Cong Shasha spoke first: “Brother Yong, there isn’t anything going on between you two, is there?”

Old Zheng said, “You mean Dr. Xiao? No, surely not.”

Cong Shasha said, “That’s right. I thought the looks on your faces later on were a bit interesting.”

Old Zheng said, “Shasha, I felt there was something on your mind yesterday too. You’re not suspecting me and Dr. Xiao of something, are you? How could I possibly! Bringing her into the team to work with us was your idea in the first place.”

Cong Shasha said, “It was my idea.”

Old Zheng said, “Stop thinking about it, my little girl. Dr. Xiao and I just talk technology. Kindred spirits, that’s all.”

Cong Shasha looked at him for a good while, and her eyes softened. She said, “Then that’s fine.”

She thought to herself: Then that’s fine. Otherwise — one in Chengdu, one in Beijing — what, does he think he’s Wei Xiaobao, the rogue with seven wives? At this thought she startled herself. She’d originally thought there was nothing much to being with her own boss — consenting adults, each getting what they wanted, and she could fight for a few more resources too. Since when had all this jealousy and sulking sprung up in her? This man in front of her — could she have developed deeper feelings for him too? Was that why she was so suspicious, half believing and half not? When had her feelings for him become so delicate, when had she started to worry? Was it the countless tender nights, or the two gold bars that no one else in the whole company had, hers alone, that made her feel his favor and care for her? Shasha felt her heart pounding with alarm.

But what she didn’t know was what the man in front of her was thinking. Old Zheng looked at the way Cong Shasha’s eyes were fixed on him, saw that she was completely soothed, and couldn’t help quietly reckoning things up. After the breakup with Diao Kexin, Cong Shasha had stepped seamlessly into his world. Compared with the one before, this girl didn’t need to make her presence felt every minute of the day, but once or twice a week, and almost every day on trips — in short, they did it very often. He could tell Shasha enjoyed him too. But she wasn’t like Diao Kexin, who liked expressing her feelings every moment, still less did she demand without end. If anything she was passive, and very accommodating. She was quiet in bed too, breathing hard but never loud. Her body was younger, its lines lovelier, but she was on the thin side — if only she were a bit plumper. And if they could stay in this kind of relationship forever, so much the better. If it came to going a step further, Old Zheng really wasn’t willing. All that flirtation and passion, all those girls showing their interest — it turned out that with the title of founder, of company boss, you ran into them endlessly. In all of Old Zheng’s life this wasn’t something he’d often felt — maybe it was the first time ever — being the center of things. He enjoyed this position of being in control no end. But he also reminded himself that in truth he still didn’t have a penny to his name. Only the business doing well — that was the only thing that mattered right now.

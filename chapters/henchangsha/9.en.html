Taotie

Chapter Nine: Taotie

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Taotie” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Northern Mountains, Book Two”): the body of a sheep and the face of a man, eyes under its armpits, a tiger’s teeth and human hands. Greedy without end, when there is nothing left to eat it devours itself too. As the saying goes: desire that knows no fill devours its master first.

Old Zheng felt as if he’d been clubbed over the head.

In the darkness the rain hammered torrentially on the car, rumbling; on the windows it was as if countless people were knocking, trying to open this little window and peer at the enormous secret hidden in this narrow space.

Diao Kexin tilted her head and asked, “What are you thinking?”

Old Zheng’s mind was in utter turmoil. If it weren’t for the rainy night outside, or if he weren’t sitting at that moment in some gas station in the middle of nowhere he couldn’t even place, he didn’t know whether he might have just taken off running.

Old Zheng said, “I’ll take you away. We’ll run off and fly far and high.” *

Diao Kexin’s face, turned toward him, flushed with fury. She twisted her body, raised her hand as if with every ounce of her strength, and slapped Old Zheng hard. She said, “You really are a shallow man. I told you the most important secret of my life, and you just tossed off something that irresponsible! You’re truly the most shameless, most bastardly, most idiotic man I’ve ever met. If we weren’t out in the middle of nowhere, I’d want you to get the hell out of this car right now, and I’d never want to see you again.”

Old Zheng drove, and the two of them sat in Diao Kexin’s red Audi, racing through the heavy rain in the dark. All the way, Diao Kexin kept cursing him in the ugliest terms, saying he’d thrown away everything she’d done for him, all the love she’d given him; he wasn’t worth loving, didn’t deserve love. When they finally reached the office, Old Zheng opened the door, and Diao Kexin said, “Get lost.”

Old Zheng shut the door in the pouring rain. Diao Kexin didn’t get out; she slid across into the driver’s seat inside the car, started it and roared away.

Old Zheng went into the office building to get his car, which was parked there, and sat in the driver’s seat. When he looked at the time, it was already eleven at night. At some point his phone had been set to silent, and from a little after eight until now there were more than twenty missed calls.

From Zhang Jingyu.

Only then did Old Zheng remember: today Zhang Jingyu had taken their son back to her hometown for her cousin’s wedding, and they were coming back to Beijing on a train arriving a little after eight in the evening. Ever since he and Zhang Jingyu had married — and especially since the child — he had dropped her off and picked her up every time she traveled, train stations and airports above all, almost never missing once. Thinking about it carefully, you could drop the “almost”: as long as he was in Beijing, he had never once missed. Even this time he’d figured that, getting back to Beijing around six-something, he could make it to the station by about nine to meet his wife and son, so they’d agreed on WeChat to meet at Beijing South Station when she came out, and if Old Zheng was late, Zhang Jingyu would just wait a little longer.

But today he had forgotten completely.

Those twenty-some missed calls ratcheted his panic up another notch. He hurried home. Zhang Jingyu’s room was dark, as if she were already asleep. Old Zheng rustled about showering outside, then felt his way onto the bed and lay down on his side of it, his back to Zhang Jingyu. Zhang Jingyu said softly, “Why are you so late? Where were you?”

Old Zheng sat bolt upright with a start and turned toward his wife. “The car broke down on the road, and then we had to get it fixed. Didn’t get back to Beijing till eleven.”

Zhang Jingyu: “Why didn’t you call? Why didn’t you answer my calls?”

Old Zheng said, “I forgot about my phone — it was on silent from work. Oh, honey, I’m so sorry!” As he said it he edged closer to her. Zhang Jingyu, still with her back to him, pulled the thin quilt tighter around herself and said flatly, “Go to sleep.”

Ten thousand horses were stampeding through Old Zheng’s heart. Had it blown over, just like that? Or not?

The next day, a calm Sunday passed as uneventfully as any other. At last Monday came, and Old Zheng went to the office early again. Again Diao Kexin didn’t appear, and Old Zheng’s mind was in chaos.

At one in the afternoon Diao Kexin appeared. She walked over to Old Zheng and said in a low voice, “Come to the meeting room.”

She walked off quickly and went into the meeting room first to wait for him. As soon as Old Zheng was inside and had turned to shut the door tight, Diao Kexin, sitting at one end of the long conference table, held out a hand as he came toward her and said, “Your phone.”

Old Zheng put his phone in her hand.

She handed it back and said, “Passcode.”

Old Zheng unlocked it with his face and handed it to her again.

She opened WeChat and wiped out every last bit of their chat history, then opened the call log and wiped out the record of their calls too.

Then she handed the phone back, took out her own, and in front of him wiped out every last bit of the past few weeks — the sweet nothings, the heartfelt words, all the lovey-dovey talk. Old Zheng’s body trembled slightly. Diao Kexin lifted her chin a little and said, “Sit over there!”

The spot she meant was at the other end of the room. It was a closed meeting room with only one long table, made of several tables pushed together, enough to seat a dozen or more people; the two ends of the long table were like the opposite banks of the Chu–Han border on a chessboard.

Old Zheng said, “Little Ke, I was wrong. I shouldn’t have been so flippant that day, I shouldn’t have said that—”

Diao Kexin said, “Between us — it’s over. No: between us, nothing ever happened. From now on you have nothing to do with me. Don’t come looking for me again.”

Old Zheng said, “What exactly do you want to hear me say?”

Diao Kexin said, “I don’t want to hear anything. Go.”

Old Zheng sank into a grief-stricken silence he couldn’t pull himself out of.

Just then the meeting room door opened.

And the person who walked in was Gao Fei.

Diao Kexin instantly put on a relaxed expression. “Professor Gao, what brings you here? Oh, we were talking about the Seventh Academy stuff.”

Old Zheng, too, instantly wiped the grief from his face and put on his business face.

Gao Fei looked at Diao Kexin, then at Old Zheng, then at the distance between where they were sitting. The meeting room was suffused with a subtle, unspeakable air of something between them, yet the distance seemed to prove there really was nothing going on. Gao Fei waved his hand. “Oh, I didn’t know you were in here. Carry on, I’ll find another room!”

Diao Kexin got straight up and said, “We’d just finished — right, Engineer Zheng?” and walked out first; passing Gao Fei, she even tilted her head and gave him a coquettish smile.

Old Zheng saw all of it. Then he too walked out.

After a few days of torment, Old Zheng couldn’t stand it any longer. One day after work he bought a huge bouquet of red roses and drove to Diao Kexin’s building to apologize. Diao Kexin made him wait downstairs for a full hour and more before she’d come down to see him.

When at last she came sauntering down and saw Old Zheng with the flowers, she tilted her head and looked at him without a word. Old Zheng said, “Little Ke, I understand now what you want me to say.”

Diao Kexin said, “I don’t expect anything from you anymore.” With that she started to walk away; Old Zheng quickly caught her arm. She turned back, Old Zheng held out the flowers, she took them, walked to the trash bin beside them and threw the flowers in.

Old Zheng came after her, watched the whole huge bouquet of roses go into the trash, and forced a smile. “Little Ke, don’t be mad at me anymore. I really do know what you want now.”

Diao Kexin rolled her eyes and looked at him sidelong. Zheng Zhiyong went on solemnly, “You want me to build a company, bigger than Yuanwang. You want me to be more successful than Gao Fei, and take you away from him.”

Diao Kexin drew a deep breath of air in through her nose, then pushed it out, long, from her chest through her nose. Her chest seemed to open out. She turned and looked steadily at Old Zheng.

Old Zheng knew he had finally said the right thing, so he went on, “I really care about you. I’m willing to work hard for you. I want to give you everything you want.”

Diao Kexin said, “It’s not about you giving it to me. It’s about you giving it to yourself. If you don’t have a man’s breadth of spirit, you’re not the man I want.”

Old Zheng hurried to put his arms around her; Diao Kexin let her arms hang by her sides — no struggle, no resistance, no response. Old Zheng said, “Little Ke, give me a little more time. I’ll work hard at it.”

Diao Kexin said, “Go.” With that she turned and went back in, leaving Old Zheng staring dumbly after her alluring figure.

Not far away, at the end of the courtyard by the side of the driveway, a black Nissan was parked, and a woman’s gaze had been fixed on this man and this woman through its brownish-black window glass from start to finish. The car was a ride she had called from home. Several days earlier, a GPS tracker had been quietly placed in Old Zheng’s car. This location was about 1.3 kilometers from her home, an upmarket residential compound in the neighborhood, and she couldn’t understand why Old Zheng’s location had stopped here for more than an hour. She had taken a car over and immediately spotted Old Zheng’s white BYD; after waiting a while, she saw Old Zheng get out of the car holding bright red roses and give them to a young woman; and at last she saw her husband embrace that woman — and the woman did not look warm.

Carrying humiliation and doubt, Zhang Jingyu said quietly to the driver, “Let’s go. Take me back where we came from.”

A while later Zheng Zhiyong came home. Dinner was already on the table; his father-in-law warmly called him to sit down and eat, and his son eagerly told him funny stories from school. The family sat around eating dinner, and Old Zheng was a little absent.

Zhang Jingyu’s expression was cool and flat; she didn’t say much — the same as any other day.

Old Zheng was staring at his phone when Diao Kexin’s WeChat suddenly lit up: “Zhao Bin’s getting married the day after tomorrow, in Changsha. You going?” Old Zheng’s eyes flicked to Zhang Jingyu; she was concentrating on her food, her eyes not lifted.

Old Zheng replied: “Yes.”

A few days later Old Zheng and Diao Kexin attended their colleague Zhao Bin’s wedding together. When the bride and groom were giving their speeches to each other, Old Zheng looked over at Diao Kexin; tears hung on her lashes, as if she were moved by this warm, happy scene. “What’s she thinking right now? Is she hoping she’ll have a grand, blessed wedding of her own someday?” Old Zheng couldn’t stop wondering.

After the wedding Old Zheng followed Diao Kexin out, and she said, let’s take a walk. The two of them didn’t say much on the pedestrian street, walking, stopping, looking; Diao Kexin didn’t seem to be in high spirits, and Old Zheng watched her face closely.

As they walked, Diao Kexin said to him, “Come on, let’s go back?”

Old Zheng asked, “Back where?”

Diao Kexin said, “The hotel.”

They got into a taxi together, and from the moment they got in Diao Kexin kissed Old Zheng, and the kiss hardly stopped from getting in to getting out. At the hotel the two went up in the elevator hand in hand, and Diao Kexin opened the door with her key card and went in first.

Old Zheng followed her in and shut the door, and Diao Kexin turned and threw herself at him, wrapping both legs around his waist. Old Zheng carried her over and tossed her onto the bed, and in an instant the two of them became two white fish in the Xiang River, leaping and frolicking, changing positions.

Apart from opening the door to take in their food deliveries, the two didn’t leave the room for three days and did it more than twenty times, hardly putting on any clothes, trying all kinds of unimaginable positions. Diao Kexin said, “You’re so good at this — what a pleasant surprise for me.”

“I think I’m not going to be able to leave you,” Diao Kexin said.

“I’ll be even less able to leave you,” Old Zheng said.

Chapter Forty-Nine: Aoye

Translation first published: 2026-11-11 18:21 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-11 18:21 (UTC+8) Last updated: 2026-11-12 16:39 © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Aoye” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Western Mountains”), on Mount Sanwei there is a beast shaped like an ox, with a white body and four horns, its bristles like a woven straw cape, and it eats men. As the saying goes: four horns, a white body, a mild look — but the fangs kept in the dark are never bared at strangers; they go for the throat of a familiar face.

Diao Kexin was still in a teahouse in Xi'an with Zhu Yiwen, drinking tea.

Faced with the question Zhu Yiwen had thrown at her, Diao Kexin answered frankly: “Mr. Zhu, I did think about going back to England. I took my master's there, and I still have some ties with old classmates and teachers. But in the end Gao Fei more or less protected me — even if I stayed on in this country, there would be no problem. Still, if I stopped running this company, there is no one else who could take on the mess of the liquidation and restructuring. And besides, the little I put aside in earlier years would not go far in England with two children to raise — it falls well short of the standard of living I want.”

Zhu Yiwen took a sip of tea and said nothing.

Diao Kexin went on: “Mr. Zhu, to be honest with you — I married Gao Fei, but I never got any real money out of him. What Gao Fei gave me was an empty shell. Even now I still have the drive and the need to keep fighting for something. But for me, he gave me two beautiful daughters, he gave me freedom, and he left me the standing of the boss's wife — that alone is enough. The opening is not perfect: most of his shares belong to the City Investment Group, and the company has become state-controlled. But that has its advantages too — when we take on government and system projects, we have a much better title to stand on. I still hold ten percent, and the board has appointed me general manager, so this company is more or less mine to call.”

Zhu Yiwen looked at the woman in front of him — thirty-seven years old, a face that drew people in, a light, quick body, a black fitted cashmere dress wrapped around a figure full of curves. She knew what she wanted, and she knew how to get it. Compared with Wang Yan at fifty-three, her ambition was entirely undisguised, her vitality brimming and full. In so short a time she had come to see him again and again; the purpose was plain — to deepen her tie with him and win his trust. But apart from her company's machining capacity, which might give her a slight cost advantage over Zhu Yiwen's existing manufacturing units, what else did she have? Zhu Yiwen had already shown his hand: he had sent his best team to back her with a complete algorithm package, somewhat different from the one in his own bid. With his support behind her, her chances this time were not small — but the outcome was unsettled, the game between the two of them still unclear, and Zhu Yiwen was quietly waiting for her to play.

Beyond the floor-to-ceiling window on the fourth floor of the science park in Xi'an's high-tech zone, the winter cold was seeping in, a little at a time, through the double-glazed vacuum glass.

Old Zheng sat at the tea table, rolling a string of walnuts worn smooth and glossy with age in his hand, his eyes fixed on the document on the desk — the Non-Compete and Confidentiality Agreement for Core Technical Personnel, just brought back from the legal department.

A few days earlier, Wu Peng had taken this document — pushed by the company, nominally to “meet the Sixth Academy's qualification review” — and had already had Zhang Hailu and every technical person sign it, all without Old Zheng knowing a thing in advance. Only today, back at the office, did he see these agreements; Wu Peng had prepared the same document and now required Old Zheng to sign as well. It struck Old Zheng with a jolt that everyone but him had already signed. Young as Wu Peng was, the depth of his scheming made Old Zheng's heart jump: this agreement that everyone was required to sign — was it Wu You's idea, or his own? Whoever was behind it, it built a wall around Xinghuan to keep the technicians it had trained from leaking away. Only, for Old Zheng himself, signing it would set one more obstacle in the way of his leaving, one day.

Wu Peng pushed the door open, glanced at the agreement on the desk without touching it, took in Old Zheng's face, and pulled the door shut behind him: “Mr. Zheng, word came over — Diao Kexin's people have been camped in the Sixth Academy's project office for three days straight. Zhu Yiwen's simulation algorithm is said to have a clear edge, and no one knows who is backing him over at the head institute. Some of the old contacts in the branch have started speaking in the flat, by-the-book tone of official business. The situation feels complicated.”

Old Zheng lifted his eyelids: “Have you found out who carries weight on this at the head institute? This is a military-civilian integration project after all — the decision power here in Xi'an should still be the leading one, no? Diao Kexin and her people may be underestimating Mr. Wu You — and underestimating how deep the water runs here.”

“My uncle has a dinner tonight,” Wu Peng lowered his voice. “A few key people from risk control are invited. Uncle's meaning is: on the surface we don't lose ground, but in the dark, some cards should be shown when it is time to show them.”

Old Zheng's heart stirred faintly. He knew what the “cards” in Wu Peng's mouth meant. In this triangle of many players — Diao Kexin holding the ties to the Beijing head institute; Zhu Yiwen with the technical edge in algorithms but seemingly not much advantage in the information chain; Wu You and Wu Peng, uncle and nephew, holding Xi'an's local political and business resources and the capital behind them — and he, Old Zheng, holding a bomb no one knew about: Zhang Hailu and his Dr. Lan were his loyal team, with a hard-core technical scheme of their own, and he also held the dirt from back then between Yuanwang and Diao Kexin, the kind that never sees daylight. Victory in this game was, to him, a certainty — especially when the opponent was Diao Kexin.

What was more, he had put aside a sum of money — the confidence that let him straighten his back against the squeezing that capital might bring, step by step; and that dirt was the blade that could, at the decisive moment, destroy Diao Kexin's name for good.

“Tell Boss Wu — count me in for tonight's dinner.” Old Zheng stood up and straightened his shirt collar.

Wu Peng looked at Old Zheng, a flicker of something complicated passing through his eyes, barely visible: “Mr. Zheng, tonight's dinner is only two or three people. I'm not even sure I'll be going.”

Zheng Zhiyong walked to the window and looked out at the grey street. Zhang Hailu's and Dr. Lan's technology was the only card he had left. As for Wu You and Wu Peng — Old Zheng was no longer an indispensable man in this project.

He turned around, his face without a ripple, and said slowly: “Actually, to deal with Diao Kexin, I have one more card. I can roughly guess who her backer in Beijing is — though I'm not certain. I'd guess that person is still willing to help her because he does not yet understand what she is really like.”

Wu Peng looked at Zheng Zhiyong: “You mean if the dirt on Diao Kexin gets out, she won't be able to land this project?”

Old Zheng said this in the hope that he too might have a part to play in the game over the whole project — after all, only if the project came in could Xinghuan be sure to survive. But at this moment his heart was full of confusion: when had Zhu Yiwen and Diao Kexin got together? Why had the two of them bid separately this time and yet met in private? Was it competition on the surface and collusion underneath — which would mean the two of them were rigging the bid against Xinghuan? So he told Wu Peng: “I saw Diao Kexin and Zhu Yiwen meeting at a teahouse. My guess is they may be cooperating in this tender.” Wu Peng said, startled: “If that's so, the situation gets even more complicated.” Old Zheng said: “Young Mr. Wu, you had better pass this on to Mr. Wu You. And if your meeting tonight can smoke out which person in Beijing it is, I'll have some cards to play later too.”

Wu Peng nodded and quietly withdrew.

The night deepened, and the cold wind off the Qinling swept across the empty streets of the high-tech zone.

In a private room of that discreet club in the south of the city, the scent of camphor wood mingled with the mellow aroma of good Moutai. The dinner had just broken up; the old leader, Wu You's friend of two generations, had been seen into his car, and only uncle and nephew were left in the room.

Wu You wiped his hands with a hot towel and took a sip from his tea cup.

“Uncle,” Wu Peng poured tea quietly at his side and asked in a low voice, “tonight, about Old Zheng — should we bring him up to date on what we know? He was fishing around in a roundabout way this afternoon, and he said he needs us to find out who the key person in Beijing is. He said this afternoon that if the dirt on Diao Kexin got out, the people in Beijing might stop helping her, and that would improve our odds in this round.”

Wu You did not even raise his eyelids, his tone level: “The dirt means those old scores between him and Diao Kexin? What damage could that do? If Diao Kexin has already secured the relationship, it cannot have rested on emotional attachment alone.”

“Understood.” Wu Peng answered, then added, “By the way, Old Zheng told me today that he saw Diao Kexin and Zhu Yiwen meeting privately at a teahouse; he warned us that Yuanwang and Gaoda may be working together, rigging the bid from behind.”

Wu You raised his head, surprised: “If Diao Kexin really is working with Gaoda, then the situation has indeed become complicated. Zhu Yiwen's algorithm is the strongest of the three; Diao Kexin's advantage was only her network of relationships. If the bid documents and the technical scheme behind the scenes are also a joint effort by the two of them, then this time it really is hard to say. You heard Uncle Jiang tonight too: relationships are never the basis for the core evaluation now — relationships are of no use, they only give you an information gap on the first day. In the end the scoring rests entirely on strength; no amount of phoning round will help. Originally we had the cost advantage, our algorithm was not bad, and we had the Shenzhen subcontractor putting our own brand on their capacity, so our supply-guarantee system looked good too — every item was an eighty-five. Originally we had a chance of winning, and not a small one. But if Zhu Yiwen fills Diao Kexin's technical gap in algorithms, and you add her advantage in information sources on top of that, then it really is hard to judge. Zhu Yiwen's weakness has always been that he prices high and disdains to do anything under the water. Now Diao Kexin has filled his gap too. It looks like this has become interesting — genuinely interesting.”

Wu You raised his eyes to Wu Peng again and asked, unhurried: “The matter I gave you — is it done?”

“Rest easy, Uncle. Zhang Hailu and Dr. Lan have signed the confidentiality and non-compete agreements.” Wu Peng said in a low voice. “Zhang Hailu and Dr. Lan, with the core technical team, all signed as you required — the agreements are in my hand. But Old Zheng has not signed yet.”

Wu You gave a noncommittal “Mm”: “Old Zheng has to sign as well. He has been away from the company a lot lately — you don't know where he goes either, do you.”

Wu Peng said: “Zhang Hailu and the others don't know either.”

Wu You said: “Going forward with this project, we should still encourage Mr. Zheng to get more deeply involved and contribute more; if it comes off, he gets a bigger share. A man like him is never short of opportunities. Being away from the company so often, most likely he has something new outside — so the non-compete agreement should be signed as soon as possible. This time I intend to vest part of the equity in him, but the accounts have to be laid out clearly to him as well. He has not made money these two years, but he did train a good team. Use him well, and there is still opportunity ahead.”

……

At that same moment, on the fourth floor of Xinghuan Technology.

Almost every light on the floor was out; only Old Zheng's office still burned a single, lonely desk lamp.

Old Zheng sat alone at his desk, the Confidentiality and Non-Compete Agreement Wu Peng had left lying in front of him, the water in his cup long gone cold. He looked out at the heavy black of the night, and felt as if a great stone were wedged in his chest.

Tonight's dinner at Wu You's had had no place for him, and Wu Peng had locked the core technical team down with agreements as lightly as if nothing were happening. That combination of blows had landed without a sound, and yet it made him see his position completely: Wu You did not treat him as an equal partner at all — he did not even have the interest to fall out with him openly; he simply used him like a tool, gently and coldly marginalising him, hollowing him out.

“Moistening things so quietly that no one hears a sound...” Old Zheng laughed at himself, but in his eyes there came a ruthless hardness born of a corner with no way out.

If capital would show no feeling and treat him as a piece to be thrown away, then he would not sit and wait for the end. Old Zheng turned, plugged the encrypted hard drive into the computer, tapped a few times at the keyboard, and brought up the dirt he had kept on Diao Kexin — not the history between him and her, but a recording she had left behind in the past, one he had secretly taped when the two of them were thickest. In it Diao Kexin told Zheng Zhiyong about her great backer in the Sixth Academy, and about how she had climbed to him, seduced him, and afterwards paid bribes. In the sweetest moment of their love, Zheng Zhiyong had kept this in reserve. He had never been some innocent, sweet-natured technical man; he had always had a ruthless, dark side.

If they were all still one crew, then he would use Xinghuan as his platform and beat Diao Kexin once again, and make her his defeated opponent once again.

At the same time, Diao Kexin had quietly played her last card at the Beijing head institute — a hidden card aimed straight at Xinghuan's lifeline, pressing down on Xi'an along the cold wind from the Qinling. The situation was hurtling towards a direction more out of control and more fractured than ever.

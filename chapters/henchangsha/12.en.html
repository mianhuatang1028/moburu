Huang

Chapter Twelve: Huang

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Huang” — the female phoenix. In the Classic of Mountains and Seas, the phoenixes sing and dance of their own accord; they roost on nothing but the wutong tree and drink from nothing but sweet springs. From then on, the wutong tree was no longer just a wutong tree.

When Old Zheng reached Shanghai that night, he went straight to Diao Kexin’s room, never imagining that Gao Fei had stayed in that same room the night before. The moment they saw each other, they couldn’t wait to fall into each other’s arms.

A little after nine that night, just as the two were in the thick of battle, Old Zheng’s WeChat suddenly rang. It was a video call from Zhang Jingyu. “It’s Zhang Jingyu,” Old Zheng told Diao Kexin, making a shushing gesture.

They instantly composed themselves and sat up in bed to take the call. Old Zheng declined the video and answered by voice:

“Hey, honey, I’m out drinking with a client, can’t do video. I’ll call you back when I’m at the hotel, okay?”

“Oh, honey, I’m in Handan. Didn’t I tell you?”

“Okay, okay, you get some rest. I’ll call you back.”

Old Zheng got through this spot check with a scare but no harm done. He spent two nights in Shanghai with Diao Kexin, then went back to Beijing. What he didn’t know was that months earlier Zhang Jingyu had turned on family location sharing on his iPhone. And what he knew even less was that on those nights he slept soundly without ever changing his passcode — that first time he didn’t come to meet her at the station, that time he came back from Changsha and slept like the dead — before he had even gotten round to deleting Diao Kexin’s WeChat, Zhang Jingyu had long since opened his phone with the shared family passcode, forwarded all his chats with Diao Kexin to herself, and taken photos of the intimate selfies the two had taken on their outings.

So when Old Zheng came back to Beijing from Shanghai, his conscience was at ease. During this time he often volunteered to pick their son up from school and often cooked for the whole family, sometimes even leaving work early to go to the market and make his specialties — red-braised pork ribs, red-braised hairtail, pig’s trotters and the like. The atmosphere at home during those days was quite normal.

Before bed the couple always said good night to each other and fell peacefully asleep, back to back.

For those two weeks, Old Zheng watched the endlessly alluring Diao Kexin at the office every day, his heart full of tender love. Sometimes, when no one was around in the daytime, the two would fool around a bit in her office, loosening each other’s clothes to ease the ache of longing; but after work Old Zheng tried to leave on time and cook a few more dinners for the family, so his wife could feel the prodigal’s resolve to come home.

But after another two weeks, with each of them away on business for a few days in between, both of them felt they could hardly hold back the longing any more. They arranged to have dinner together after work, so that afterwards they could spend a little while in the car — a little sweet time for the two of them alone.

A little after nine that night they were outside Diao Kexin’s building, sitting together in the back seat of the white BYD — now kissing and embracing, now rubbing and caressing each other, now holding each other’s hands and sweetly confiding their plans for the future.

Then someone knocked on the car window.

Old Zheng’s heart jolted.

He rolled down the window. It was Zhang Jingyu’s cousin, a police officer in Tongzhou, Beijing. Behind him stood Zhang Jingyu and Old Zheng’s father-in-law. Even at that moment Old Zheng didn’t know that a GPS tracker had been in his car for months.

By the time Old Zheng had taken in who everyone was, Zhang Jingyu’s cousin had stuck his head into the car as well and seen the disheveled Diao Kexin. He took a photo of the two of them with his phone, then pointed a finger at Old Zheng; his silence was as good as saying, “Zheng Zhiyong, you just wait.”

Zhang Jingyu and the two relatives guarding her turned and walked away, resolute. They got into the cousin’s car together, and the car sped off. In the dark night no one’s expression could be seen, no word or sound heard. Old Zheng was left standing in the wind like a fool. Diao Kexin got out of the car and tugged at his arm.

“I have to get back right away,” Old Zheng said.

“Mm.” Diao Kexin nodded.

Old Zheng got in the car and drove after them.

Back home, every bedroom door was shut tight. Zhang Jingyu’s room was not only locked but dark. Every member of the family was home, yet no one was yelling at him; the silence was like the eve of a storm.

Old Zheng knocked softly on Zhang Jingyu’s door. “Jingyu, open the door, please? I’m sorry. Can I talk to you?”

Zhang Jingyu answered, “I’m too tired. We’ll talk tomorrow.”

Old Zheng spent a fearful night on the sofa.

The next morning Zhang Jingyu stood silently beside him. Old Zheng shot upright. “Honey, I was wrong.”

“Zheng Zhiyong, once my dad’s taken our son to school, I’ll talk to you.” With that she turned and went out.

Old Zheng watched his father-in-law help Zheng Ruixi wash up and eat. At breakfast their son asked, “Grandpa, where’s Mom?” His grandfather glared at Old Zheng with a dark face, then turned back to the boy and answered gently, “Mom had something at work, so she went in early.” Then Grandpa and Grandma went out together to take him to school, and the boy even called out a big, cheerful goodbye to his dad.

But Zhang Jingyu didn’t come back. Old Zheng waited and waited and she didn’t return; after going back and forth in his head, he decided he’d better go to the office.

When he got there, he found Zhang Jingyu waiting right downstairs. “Let’s go up together,” she said.

Old Zheng was flustered. “Can we talk at home?”

Zhang Jingyu said, “No. I want to see what kind of creature wrecked my family.”

When they walked into the office, Diao Kexin was already sitting in hers. Zhang Jingyu said sharply, “Diao Kexin, get out here!”

Old Zheng hurriedly pulled Zhang Jingyu into the meeting room. By now nearly the whole company had arrived, and everyone was watching.

Diao Kexin came into the meeting room too and closed the door, and the three of them stood facing off. Old Zheng stood behind Zhang Jingyu looking at Diao Kexin, then rushed to close the blinds against the eyes outside.

Zhang Jingyu said loudly, “I don’t want this scumbag anymore. He’s yours.”

Diao Kexin said, “Sister-in-law, isn’t there some misunderstanding? I can explain.”

Zhang Jingyu said loudly again, “I know everything about you two dogs. Let me tell you: even if the two of you really end up together, it won’t end well. I curse your future.”

Diao Kexin looked at Old Zheng, and Old Zheng hurried to pull Zhang Jingyu away. “Hit me, curse me, whatever you want — let’s go home and talk, all right?”

Zhang Jingyu went home with Old Zheng, her tears pouring like rain the whole way. “I want a divorce. Our property agreement’s already signed. Tomorrow I want you to come with me and get the divorce done.”

Old Zheng said, “I don’t agree to a divorce. Honey, I really know I was wrong. I can’t leave you, I can’t leave our son. Please, forgive me.” Old Zheng spoke earnestly, his voice choked, his own tears falling too.

Zhang Jingyu said, “I can’t forgive you. I want a divorce. I’ve seen your chats. So many times you told her how much you love her, the two of you such soulmates — you even said you’d rather walk away with nothing if it meant marrying her. I’m granting your wish.”

The two of them drove home. Zhang Jingyu shut her door and lay on the bed, and from outside the door Old Zheng couldn’t hear a sound.

Just then Old Zheng got a call from Diao Kexin. He panicked, rejected it, and ran downstairs to call her back. “I can’t talk at home.”

Diao Kexin said, “I have to go to Changsha on business today. Right after you two left, I headed for the airport. I’m about to board. Brother Yong, there’s something I have to tell you.”

Old Zheng said, “What is it?”

Diao Kexin said, “My period’s five days late, and my breasts are aching like crazy. I think I’m pregnant.”

Old Zheng felt as if he’d been struck by lightning. Upstairs was Zhang Jingyu, his love and his wife of twenty years; together they had brought the adorable Zheng Ruixi into the world, innocent, kind and lively. The way she had just wept in the car, like a pear blossom in the rain — Old Zheng’s heart still ached for her, he still loved her so much. Every word he’d said about not wanting a divorce, not wanting to leave home, not wanting to leave his son, had been heartfelt too; he truly couldn’t bear it! Twenty years! That was his whole youth, twenty years he had walked step by step from a naive boy to a middle-aged man. How many sweet memories they’d had together, how much living and growing up they’d witnessed in each other! Zhang Jingyu had seen Zheng Zhiyong in his prime; Zheng Zhiyong had seen Zhang Jingyu in her peerless beauty. Together they had built their little family from nothing, raised an arm-length baby into a schoolboy — how many happy moments there had been in all of that! How could he possibly cut it off? It was utterly impossible to cut it off!

But at this very moment, this Diao Kexin was telling him she was pregnant. They had made love so many times, so passionately — this child was without a doubt Zheng Zhiyong’s own. Minutes ago he’d been utterly determined not to divorce whatever the cost, but now that thought began to waver. The arrival of this new life suddenly gave Zheng Zhiyong endless fantasies about the future — fantasies of being with Diao Kexin.

His head nearly spinning, dazed and foggy, Zheng Zhiyong went back upstairs, sat on the sofa and smoked in silence.

When Zhang Jingyu came out of the bedroom, Zheng Zhiyong asked her, “Are you set on divorcing?”

Zhang Jingyu’s tears streamed down, and at the same time she nodded. “I’ll have a lawyer draw up the divorce agreement according to the agreement we already signed. Let’s get the divorce done as soon as possible.”

Zheng Zhiyong said nothing.

The silence was a kind of consent.

Chapter Two: Guanguan

“Guanguan” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Southern Mountains”), there is a bird shaped like a turtledove whose call sounds like scolding; it is called the guanguan, and whoever wears it will not be confused. As the saying goes: wear it and you will not be confused — nor led astray.

Zhang Jingyu really was a great beauty.

A meter seventy-two, long and slender, skin as white as snow, a gentle, gracious face; you couldn’t tell whether she wore makeup, yet her face was always flawless. Medium-length hair with a slight wave, elegance in every gesture. She was thirty-six this year, and her son was in first grade at a public primary school in Fengtai, less than three kilometers as the crow flies from both her workplace and their home. She had worked at a research institute under the Fifth Academy for more than ten years, yet visiting clients still often mistook her for one of the graduate students assigned there in the last couple of years, or for an unmarried young woman.

She came from Wuhu, Anhui, where her parents were minor officials in the local power bureau; by local standards the family was comfortably above average. She was even-tempered, mild-mannered and academically excellent, and was admitted to the mechatronics program at Northwestern Polytechnical University, the only girl among the thirty people in her class. The entire School of Mechatronics had only four girls among the ninety-three students of that year. On top of that she was the only girl in her year from an urban middle-class family. Zhang Jingyu dressed exquisitely, kept to herself, was beautiful and said little, and in a school full of boys she stood out enormously. Very quickly she became, in the minds of the boys of the whole university, that year’s most beautiful goddess, passed from mouth to mouth at the late-night talks in every boys’ dorm.

Zheng Zhiyong was the most unremarkable-looking of all that year’s students in the School of Mechatronics. He was a meter seventy-five and dressed plainly, but he had two outstanding traits: one, he could talk; two, he could run. At the autumn sports meet not long after he started university, he even won the 400 meters. The 400 meters is a magical distance — it takes explosive power and stamina at the same time. It was during that race that Zhang Jingyu first noticed there was such a boy in her class; standing in the crowd cheering for their class, she tugged the sleeve of the boy beside her and asked his name. Of course, in pursuing Zhang Jingyu, Zheng Zhiyong made the fullest use of his gifts for running and talking, and against the competition of thousands at the same school he somehow came out on top and won the beauty in the end — but that comes later.

So how exactly did he win her?

Zheng Zhiyong’s dorm was on the ground floor, right in the middle, and his bunk was the top one, so whether he was in bed or at the window he could see the entrance of the girls’ building opposite. That entrance was manned by a dorm auntie, but every time a girl came out of that door on her way to class, Zheng Zhiyong would glance over without thinking. Whenever Zhang Jingyu appeared, he would vault down from his bunk like a leopard, grab his bag and shoot out the door after her. Jingyu usually bought some takeaway breakfast at one of the breakfast shops along the street, and just as Zheng Zhiyong was about to catch up, he’d hurry to steady his breathing and pretend he’d just happened to bump into her.

“What a coincidence, you’re here too.” Zheng Zhiyong always said it with a show of nonchalance. Before long, the sight of the two of them walking into the teaching building together left an impression on the other boys, as if the two of them always arranged to go to class together. Some classmates who didn’t know any better started to wonder whether something was going on between them.

One day after class, Zheng Zhiyong hurried a few steps to catch up with Zhang Jingyu. “You free this weekend?”

Zhang Jingyu said, “What for?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Our dorm’s planning to go to the Big Wild Goose Pagoda this weekend. Want to come?”

Zhang Jingyu thought about it and asked, “How many people?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Just our dorm, four of us. You coming?”

Zhang Jingyu: “I’ve got something on this weekend. I won’t go.”

Zheng Zhiyong asked, “You’ve already been to the Big Wild Goose Pagoda?”

Zhang Jingyu shook her head. “Not yet.” Then she added, “But I do want to go.”

Zhang Jingyu walked off quickly, leaving Zheng Zhiyong in front of the teaching building, staring blankly after her.

The next day after class Zheng Zhiyong caught up with her again. He called out, “Zhang Jingyu!”

Zhang Jingyu: “What?”

Zheng Zhiyong: “Coming with us to the Big Wild Goose Pagoda this weekend? Tao Lina from your dorm’s going too.”

Zhang Jingyu: “Oh, she’s going? I’ll go back and ask her. If she goes, I’ll go.”

With that Jingyu quickened her pace again and left without looking back, and once more Zheng Zhiyong was left staring.

That weekend six young people boarded the bus to the Big Wild Goose Pagoda together. Zheng Zhiyong made full use of his gift of the gab, playing tour guide the whole way and narrating the pagoda’s history in leisurely detail. Though there were six of them, he kept close to Zhang Jingyu the whole time; as she wandered, stopping here and there to look, he stopped and wandered too, step for step. At just the right moments, and patiently, he explained the sights: “The Great Ci’en Temple was originally built by Emperor Gaozong of the Tang for Xuanzang, and the Big Wild Goose Pagoda was the sutra repository built to go with it. When Xuanzang came back from fetching the scriptures from the Western Heavens, the emperor was pleased, so he built him a temple, and a pagoda to keep his scriptures in. Later Xuanzang adapted the scriptures to spread among the ordinary people of the Tang — he didn’t just copy the Indian classics wholesale. The Great Ci’en Temple belongs to Mahayana Buddhism, the ‘Great Vehicle,‘ so you could say the ‘Big’ in Big Wild Goose Pagoda is the ‘Great’ of the Great Vehicle, not ‘big’ as in a big goose. Xi’an also has a Small Wild Goose Pagoda, which can tell you what the ‘big’ means — or rather, since Emperor Zhongzong of the Tang later built another goose pagoda for a different monk, to tell it apart from the one already built, the earlier, bigger one was called the Big Wild Goose Pagoda, and the later, smaller one the Small Wild Goose Pagoda—”

Zhang Jingyu laughed and joked, “The later one could be called the Little Gosling Pagoda.”

The few classmates who heard it burst out laughing. Zheng Zhiyong, still in full flow, went on: “They say Xuanzang’s relics are inside the Big Wild Goose Pagoda too.”

Zhang Jingyu listened with interest. “Can we see the relics in a bit?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I don’t know either. We’ll have to go into the pagoda and see.”

Talking all the while, the group reached the foot of the pagoda. Zheng Zhiyong asked everyone, “Going up?”

Zhang Jingyu said she wanted to go up and have a look, but the others all said they couldn’t be bothered. No doubt Zheng Zhiyong had arranged and deployed them beforehand, turning his fellow travelers into wingmen. Zheng Zhiyong seized the moment: “You lazy lot, honestly. Jingyu, I’ll go up with you. You guys wait for us at that shop over there and we’ll meet up when we come down.” He winked at his wingmen.

So the group split in two, and the two of them climbed the Big Wild Goose Pagoda alone. By the fourth story, raindrops began to fall. Zhang Jingyu, holding the railing, looked out from the pagoda, and Zheng Zhiyong said, “The wind fills the tower before the mountain rain arrives” — and just then the sky really did let loose a heavy rain. Late autumn turned cold and clear, with a thread of chill in the air. Zheng Zhiyong took off his jacket and draped it over Zhang Jingyu. Jingyu looked up and asked him, “Aren’t you cold?” Zheng Zhiyong grinned foolishly. “As long as you’re not cold, I won’t be either.” Zhang Jingyu blushed shyly.

After that outing the two returned to school with the others in tacit understanding. The following week Zheng Zhiyong secured a chance to see a film alone with Zhang Jingyu; as it happened, the cinemas in Xi’an were showing Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire. While waiting for Zhang Jingyu outside her building, Zheng Zhiyong had a single rose rolled up in newspaper and tucked into the inside pocket of his jacket. That day Zhang Jingyu wore a red sweater and a white skirt, and when she came out of the girls’ dorm Zheng Zhiyong felt that every boy in the world was looking at the two of them with burning eyes — so he didn’t dare take the red rose out of his jacket at all.

In the cinema, while Zhang Jingyu watched the magical wizarding world, Zheng Zhiyong kept turning his head to look at Jingyu. “What a wondrous world, this world of love!” he sighed inwardly. Jingyu’s profile still had a young girl’s baby fat, and when the light fell on it, the fine down on her face glowed with youth; Zheng Zhiyong gazed at the beautiful girl, lost, as if falling into first love. Coming out of the cinema, Zhang Jingyu was still immersed in imagining the wizarding world, the thrill of the Quidditch match still lingering in her heart; she was excitedly reliving the Nimbus 2000 and the Golden Snitch when Zheng Zhiyong seized the moment to pull from his inside pocket the red rose that had been hidden there for hours. The moment Zhang Jingyu tore open the roll of newspaper, the crushed rose inside dropped its petals one by one onto the ground. Zhang Jingyu tilted her head and looked at Zheng Zhiyong.

“So this is your sorry excuse for a flower.” Zhang Jingyu laughed happily.

In the spring term of their second year, Zhang Jingyu accepted Zheng Zhiyong and became his girlfriend. One early-summer day in their third year, the two came back from shopping and lingered over their goodbyes in the little grove on campus, until it was past eleven and the girls’ building had locked its doors. Zhang Jingyu was so distressed she was close to tears. Zheng Zhiyong took her hand and asked, “Do you trust me?”

Zhang Jingyu said, “I trust you.”

Zheng Zhiyong pulled Zhang Jingyu along, and they walked off campus onto the road. Two hundred meters outside the campus gate was the university guesthouse. Zheng Zhiyong went in, leaving Zhang Jingyu standing alone by the road. Her white skirt lifted in the wind and fell again; she felt a little cold just then and hugged her arms around herself. After a while Zheng Zhiyong came out again and took her hand to lead her inside. Zhang Jingyu felt the pull guiding her and froze for a moment, not stepping forward.

Zheng Zhiyong felt he couldn’t move her, so he turned and looked at her and asked, “Do you trust me?”

She nodded and followed him in.

The two went into the room Zheng Zhiyong had just booked, a standard twin with two beds. Zheng Zhiyong settled Zhang Jingyu on a seat by the window, knelt on one knee before her, took each of her hands in each of his and laid them flat on her knees. He kissed her hands, then raised his head and asked her, “Do you trust me?”

Zhang Jingyu nodded again.

Zheng Zhiyong held Zhang Jingyu tight and drew her up onto the single bed. He was very strong, with thick arms, and Zhang Jingyu could feel a powerful, scorching breath engulfing her body little by little. His kisses grew bold.

Zhang Jingyu had never felt anything like it. Her heart pounded as if it would leap out of her chest; her chest began to rise and fall, her breathing quickened bit by bit, her body grew hot and damp, and it seemed that, little by little, her slight body couldn’t help swaying faintly to the rhythm of the kisses. By now her pale yellow long-sleeved blouse had been unbuttoned. Zheng Zhiyong tried tentatively to undo her bra, but the hook was like some ingenious Golden Snitch of a catch, and Zheng Zhiyong’s impatient fingers couldn’t open it no matter what.

Zheng Zhiyong looked at the girl in his arms, her face shy and flushed; in it he saw neither anger nor refusal, only nervousness and shyness.

Zheng Zhiyong grew bolder. He saw that the girl’s face had turned completely red and that she had squeezed her eyes shut. He slid off her long white skirt, then wanted to go further and slide off her pink underwear.

Zhang Jingyu’s hand gently caught his — a faint, barely-there force of resistance. Zheng Zhiyong stopped and asked her, “Do you want me to stop?”

Zhang Jingyu said nothing, and didn’t open her eyes; she simply let go of his hand and laid both her hands flat on the bed, like a doll that would let itself be posed however one pleased.

That summer the two met each other’s families, and from then on they were inseparable for the rest of university. After finishing their undergraduate degrees they did master’s degrees in the same field together at Northwestern Polytechnical, and after graduating they both found jobs at aerospace research institutes in Beijing. Zhang Jingyu went to the China Academy of Space Technology, that is, the Fifth Academy; Zheng Zhiyong went to Institute 15 of the same organization. They rented a place together and began a shamelessly blissful life together, and within about a year of starting work they got their marriage certificate.

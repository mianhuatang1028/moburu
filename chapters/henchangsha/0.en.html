Prologue: Kunpeng

At last, Li Fulang stood on the seventh-floor rooftop of Building A, College of Aeronautics and Astronautics, facing the railing that overlooked the rear of the teaching complex.

The entire building had been constructed along the front slope of a low hill. It belonged to Xiangnan University, a nationally renowned institution in Changsha and one of China’s universities included in the elite “985 Project.” Below him lay a sea of green woodland. The heat of summer had deepened the color of the trees covering the hillside, turning the green darker, richer, and more remote. Like a warrior, like a lover, he wanted one last look at the colors of this world. Thirty-two years. The first half of his life swept past his eyes in a rapid succession of fleeting images.

Then, without a trace of hesitation, he leapt……

He had been born into a family of teachers in Rongjiang County, Guizhou—the family of a teacher at the county town’s only key junior high school. His father was the school’s principal and also taught physics. His mother came from China’s northeast. In her youth, she had moved south as part of a state program to support the less-developed regions of the country. There she met her brilliant husband, put down roots, and eventually ran a timber factory. From his mother, he had inherited neither her social finesse nor her refined, striking features. From his father, however, he had inherited an exceptionally sharp mind and a goodness almost without equal. Even as a child, he had shown astonishing gifts for mathematics and physics. There was no doubt about it: he was extraordinarily intelligent, and extraordinarily innocent. In high school, he had already set his heart on working in human spaceflight and space technology. His parents were delighted and gave him their wholehearted support. He won admission to Beiqing University, then went to Imperial College London in Britain, where he completed both his master’s and doctoral studies at its institute for space technology. And yet he was painfully inarticulate. He dreaded speaking in public, disliked socializing, and had no desire to expose his inner world to anyone.

None of this hindered his academic achievements. More strangely still, his command of written and spoken English soon surpassed his command of Chinese. He was particularly good at publishing, in English, his discoveries in the world of physics in international academic journals. Three years earlier, after earning his doctorate, he had returned to China to take up a junior teaching post at the university. Four months ago, he had been promoted to associate professor in its College of Aeronautics and Astronautics.

He had a wife as beautiful as a flower, a woman he had known since high school. She had once been his father’s student. Despite his thoroughly ordinary, even slightly unattractive appearance, she had fallen in love with the world of his mind and become his incomparable wife—the one person in the world to whom he could open his heart. Then, two months ago, she had suddenly asked for a divorce. She believed he had been unfaithful. When she saw him having a meal with a female visiting scholar from the university, she stormed into the restaurant, weeping and accusing him loudly enough to draw everyone’s attention. Later, she caused another scene in the office of his college, demanding that he give her justice. “You cheated on me with someone else. I’ve been with you since I was a girl, and now you’ve betrayed me. I want a divorce. You owe me compensation for my fourteen years. I never want to see you again.”

Did an ordinary-looking man not deserve a beautiful wife and children of his own? He thought of his wife: the warmth and softness of her body, her full breasts, the look in her eyes that seemed to invite him even as it held him at bay, her gentle, shy voice. She had given herself to him at eighteen. In bed, she had always been as tender as water, a bride in every sense. They had been married for four years and still had no child. He and his parents had long been waiting for the next generation to arrive, but he had not even had the chance to become a father. He could not put his grief into words, and there was no one to whom he could confide it. At that moment, the woods spread out before him seemed as good a place as any to make his final home. He did not want a divorce. He was desperate to prove to his wife that he had not cheated, that he had been faithful. He wanted to tell her: I will love you forever. Even at the moment of my death, I still love you.

His resolve to jump shot forward like a missile launched from its ejector……

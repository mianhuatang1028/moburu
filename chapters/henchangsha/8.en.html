Fortunately, she had the ability to work independently and the backing of her family, and her days, too, went along like a fish in water.

On this ordinary night she hadn’t sensed anything amiss. Only the thunder and lightning woke her. She got up and hurried to shut the window, which was already letting in rain, then went to check whether the windows in the child’s and her parents’ rooms were closed. It was half past midnight and Old Zheng still wasn’t home. She called him. “It’s so late — why aren’t you home yet?”

Old Zheng said, “I’m already downstairs. I’ll be up in a few minutes.”

The next morning Old Zheng left for work before eight.

But Diao Kexin still didn’t appear.

Old Zheng kept checking his phone; Diao Kexin didn’t message him either. He couldn’t concentrate. At the end of the day, he sent her a message: “Why didn’t you come in?”

After a long while Diao Kexin replied: “I was out on business today.”

On the third day Old Zheng got up a little after six, meaning to get to the office early, but Zhang Jingyu said, “What are you up so early for? It’s Saturday.” She rolled over and went back to sleep.

“Are you tired? How about my dad goes to buy groceries in a bit, I’ll take the kid to English class, and you rest at home today?” Zhang Jingyu said to him as she tidied away toys.

Old Zheng answered, “Okay.”

On Sunday Old Zheng forced himself to perk up, and the whole family went to Shougang Art Park to stroll and have a meal. Old Zheng kept looking at his phone, but all day long nobody contacted him for any reason at all.

Then it was Monday, and Diao Kexin still didn’t appear.

After work, as dusk was falling, Old Zheng drove straight to her building. He called Diao Kexin and told her to come down. Diao Kexin, languidly dressed, strolled slowly out through her building’s security door.

She wore a white silk slip nightdress with a thin light-yellow cardigan over it, and she drifted up to Old Zheng like that. She reached out, took his hand, and led him to the green belt at the side of her building, where a hedge of tall green pines screened out the cars and eyes beyond. Once they’d stopped, she first raised her head and gave Old Zheng a reproachful look, and that one look made him go numb. Old Zheng said, “Little Ke.”

Old Zheng seemed to understand what that meant.

For the whole week the two were on WeChat with each other every moment. After work Diao Kexin and Old Zheng had dinner together and shopped for gear, snacks and supplies for the grasslands. When they weren’t talking face to face, they were chatting on WeChat — even lying in bed at night, they were chatting.

On Friday morning, the moment Old Zheng opened his eyes, a WeChat came in from her: “I miss you.”

Old Zheng looked at Zhang Jingyu, sound asleep, and replied: “See you at the office in a bit.”

At the office it was several hours before Diao Kexin appeared. She was wearing the light-purple couples’ sun-protection jacket the two of them had bought together at Decathlon two days earlier, and as she walked in she gave Old Zheng a look full of tenderness. Old Zheng was wearing the matching dark-blue one, and he felt his heart melt, because he knew that at noon they’d be setting off.

When they left, they split naturally into two groups. One was Diao Kexin’s whole Beijing sales team, thirteen or fourteen young people, men and women chattering away as they climbed onto a minibus; the other was Old Zheng, driving Diao Kexin’s red Audi. The two vehicles, one big and one small, set off one behind the other for the Zhangbei grasslands.

In the car Old Zheng drove one-handed; Diao Kexin’s hand stayed tightly clasped in his other one. “I like you holding my hand the whole time — I need this,” Diao Kexin demanded. Whenever Old Zheng took his hand away to shift gears, she immediately pouted and whined loudly, “Don’t let go of my hand! I want my man to keep hold of me all the time!”

Old Zheng felt that everything about Diao Kexin was the opposite of Zhang Jingyu. Zhang Jingyu hated it when Old Zheng touched her in public, let alone expressed love and admiration in any way. But this woman before him was expressing pure love and admiration for him completely, every moment of every day; his self-confidence had never swelled like this, and his pride as a man had reached its peak.

Toward evening they reached the yurt lodge on the grassland. Before the main group’s minibus arrived, the two went off on their own and climbed a low little hill, and stood on the endless green grassland looking at the sunset and the distant mountains, the huge white blades of the wind turbines dotted among them like pearls. As the sun went down, the scenery was beautiful beyond words.

Looking at the distant mountains, Diao Kexin said, “This sunset is so beautiful — the most beautiful sight I’ve seen in my whole life, bar none. More beautiful even than what I saw in England with Li Fulang.”

Old Zheng held her from behind, and that sentence sent ripples through his heart. He bent and kissed Diao Kexin’s loose hair.

“If one day you’re not by my side, whenever I see a sunset I’ll miss you terribly,” Diao Kexin said, turning her head slightly toward him. A flicker of foreboding crossed Old Zheng’s mind, as if this woman already sensed that they would soon be parted.

At night the team arrived, and under the local guide’s arrangements they sat around a bonfire eating mutton, then scattered to their rooms to rest.

Old Zheng went round and round, deliberately taking several detours to avoid everyone, and found the yurt Diao Kexin had to herself. She was already wearing a sheer, barely-there nightdress, as if she’d been waiting a long time. When she saw him, she quickly stepped aside to let him into her yurt, and as soon as the door was shut the two of them were kissing madly, rolling onto the huge bed in the middle of the yurt.

Diao Kexin took off Old Zheng’s clothes one piece at a time until he lay there naked. She could see clearly that she had roused him. She playfully tapped him with a fingertip, whisked her finger away, and went on kissing his lips as if nothing had happened.

“Brother Yong, I love you so, so much, and I want so, so much to give myself to you, but we can’t do this last thing. You have a family.”

Old Zheng gradually calmed down and lay on his side next to her. But Diao Kexin kissed his lips again and lingered tenderly for a good while. After a while she said, “You should think about how you’re going to treat me from here on — and how you’re going to treat her.”

“I’ll be good to you, I promise. I’ll marry you,” Old Zheng said to her.

“Then what about her?” Diao Kexin asked coldly.

“I’ll give her all the money, the apartment, all the property, as compensation, so she can live the rest of her life without worry,” Old Zheng answered.

Diao Kexin said, “Even so, you have to understand — I don’t want a penniless man. I fell for you because you have talent and, more than that, ambition. I need a man who can succeed in his career, and I want to help you build a career of your own.”

Old Zheng said, “The job at Yuanwang is just a transit station for me. Besides building up my first pot of gold, I’m here to learn how to start and run a company.”

Diao Kexin said, “You know what? You’ve been outstanding lately, and you’ve handled the people side really well. Zhao Bin is Professor Gao’s student, who followed him into the business; he was the company’s first employee. You obviously took credit that was his, and he still sings your praises. Professor Gao said that if the company ever needs to groom a CEO, he’d be willing to groom you.”

Old Zheng said, “Whatever comes my way, I always know in my heart that I’m going to start my own business. I told you, my grandfather founded a big machinery factory decades ago. Entrepreneur’s blood runs in my veins. I still think about all that land and those factory buildings in Xiangyang. Someday I’ll go back there.”

Diao Kexin kissed his lips again. “You know what? What I love most is that ambition of yours. But you have to succeed. If you don’t, if you stay penniless forever, I can only be your partner for a while — I still won’t choose you as my husband. Only when you succeed in your career and build up wealth to the number I have in mind will I be willing to be with you.”

Old Zheng said, “What’s the number?”

Diao Kexin said, “Do you know what I make now?”

Old Zheng shook his head. Diao Kexin said, “I started at this company as the most junior salesperson and became sales director a few years ago. My fixed pay is fifty thousand a month after tax. Last year I landed the vast majority of this company’s orders, and my commission came to 2.3 million yuan after tax. This year it’ll be even more.”

Old Zheng was secretly stunned. In his life experience, the very ceiling of what he could earn in this lifetime fell far short of her floor — even of her loose change.

Diao Kexin said, “I want you to build something as big as Yuanwang one day, even bigger. I believe that as long as we stick together, with your technology and my sales ability, we have a chance to do it.”

This small woman with her boundless ambition set Old Zheng’s entrepreneurial passion alight. He rolled on top of her; Diao Kexin said, “Get off. I said no.”

He had no choice but to roll off and lie back on the bed. Diao Kexin said tenderly, “Let me tell you the second reason I like you so much: you’re really handsome. You know what? My ex-husband was really, really ugly.”

As she spoke she picked up her phone from beside the bed, fiddled with it a while, and showed Old Zheng her ex-husband’s ID photo. “He looks like a kid with Down syndrome,” Diao Kexin said. “Look how regular and handsome your face is. I can’t get enough of looking at you. I never thought I could ever be with such a good-looking man,” and she bent down to kiss Old Zheng’s lips again.

That night the two went round and round in a cycle of kissing and talking, talking and kissing, and didn’t sleep at all until dawn. After daybreak they toured and played and took photos with the other colleagues, looking no different from close, fond colleagues.

That evening, when the team-building day was over, the two vehicles, one big and one small, drove back to Beijing separately again. On the way it began to spit with rain, and after a while thunder and rain came on in force. By then it was pitch dark and the rain was pouring down. Old Zheng pulled into a gas station, bought Diao Kexin a hot drink and a burger in the little shop, and the two parked in the farthest corner of the station, eating and talking. Before long Diao Kexin kissed Old Zheng again.

After Old Zheng’s hand had caressed her breasts for a while, Diao Kexin asked, “Do you have a condom?” Old Zheng said, “Yes.” He’d prepared one before leaving Beijing. Diao Kexin had Old Zheng make some room in her car, then lifted her skirt and straddled his thighs. She said, “I can’t take it anymore.”

Old Zheng said, “Yes.”

Old Zheng handed her a lit cigarette, and Diao Kexin said, “I have a secret to tell you.”

Old Zheng looked at her, puzzled, and she went on, “I have a daughter. She’s seven this year. Her father,” she took a drag, paused, and said, “is Gao Fei.”

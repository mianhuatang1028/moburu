Yingyu

Chapter Twenty-Four: Yingyu

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Yingyu” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Western Mountains, Book Four”), the Meng River on Mount Gui is full of yingyu, fish with the bodies of fish and the wings of birds, whose cry is like a mandarin duck’s; when they appear, their town is flooded. Two teams, both from the same old crew. As the saying goes: swimming in the same water, flying on separate wings.

Inside word soon came from the client. As expected, Xinghuan’s rival in this bid was none other than Yuanwang.

Xinghuan’s project team was made up of three people: Zheng Zhiyong, Zhang Hailu and Cong Shasha. Yuanwang’s was made up of three as well: Gao Fei, Diao Kexin and the CTO, Zhao Bin. Both teams came one hundred percent from the old core crew of the Yuanwang group. Teammates who had once fought shoulder to shoulder had now become out-and-out opponents. Deep in Zheng Zhiyong’s heart, in fact, Yuanwang wasn’t just his opponent: to him, Gao Fei, the man who had taken the love of his life, was his mortal enemy. And the reason all these people had become opponents and mortal enemies was, of all things, just this one woman. Because of this woman’s illicit relationship with Gao Fei, the former core of the sales team felt treated unfairly, the former head of technology felt wronged, and the next-in-line CEO who would have had a brilliant future had come to see Gao Fei as the archenemy he had to beat in this lifetime.

This woman, single-handedly and in her own peculiar way, had given every one of them a bellyful of resentment and hatred, and all that hatred had been shifted onto her current husband, Gao Fei. With her enormous energy she had split apart, utterly and completely, the core team that had once belonged wholly to Gao Fei, and had delivered the company’s most precious package of assets to the other side.

In the process, this woman had also very nearly killed the man she supposedly loved. On the night she cut him off without warning, she had left him with nothing, left him adrift and homeless, left him in utter despair, left him all but ready to end it all. Looking back on those nights now, Zheng Zhiyong was glad that even in the most heartbreaking moments he had not in the end chosen what Li Fulang had chosen years before — Li Fulang, who’d gone through the same thing, left with nothing, his wife and his money both gone — to leap from a tall building and be done with it. That flicker of will to survive and fight had made him decide, at the end of his rope, to try once more. So he’d gone to Xi’an to see Wu You, luckily caught the general manager’s post at Xinghuan that dropped out of the sky, and in just a few short months turned his life completely around.

The twists and turns of fate are strange indeed — enough to make you sigh!

The last round of talks with the academy’s technical departments before the proposal had ended smoothly. Then one day, at dinner with a classmate who worked at the academy, Zhang Hailu learned that for this proposal Yuanwang had a heavyweight partner company called Gaoda, which would co-develop with them and take a subcontracting role in the technical solution. The other company’s boss was Zhu Yiwen, formerly a professor at another well-known military academy in China, who had also left to start a business. Because his background was much like Gao Fei’s and their companies had been founded around the same time, the two of them had been friends for many years, and this was not the first time they had worked together in this way, one as prime contractor and the other as subcontractor.

The other important piece of news was that the contract value of the whole project had gone up further, to 20 million yuan. The next proposal meeting, where Xinghuan and Yuanwang would collide head-on, was coming soon, and that meeting might well decide which of the two companies would bring down the deer on this project.

Zhang Hailu passed the news to Zheng Zhiyong. Zheng Zhiyong had long since heard of Gaoda. The company was very famous in the industry, known for technical leadership; in some niche areas of remote sensing they were even absolutely ahead of their domestic peers. So their commercialization looked to be going very well too, with business booming year after year; there were even rumors that a brokerage had already come in and they were already on the road to an IPO. Zheng Zhiyong asked Zhang Hailu, “Any chance we could get a meeting with Zhu Yiwen, set it up so we can get to know him too?”

Zhang Hailu said, “My classmate knows him pretty well. I’ll ask and see if there’s a chance he can arrange it. Wait to hear from me.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “What’s your classmate’s position now?”

Zhang Hailu said, “He’s got the title of research fellow now, but when Zhu Yiwen first started his business, the launch was inseparable from the work the two of them did together back then. Two tech guys, kindred spirits — they traded ideas and between them cracked something that was extremely hard at the time, and that laid the foundation of their friendship.”

Zheng Zhiyong was brewing an idea of his own just then. In their overall technical concept and implementation plan as it stood, because of what he knew of Yuanwang, they had added an upfront simulation stage to the design of the whole solution, which would make implementing the whole product a great deal safer. Old Zheng was confident that compared with whatever Yuanwang might propose, he and Zhang Hailu had their own unique, even leading, advantages. But if they wanted to finish Yuanwang off in one blow, Zhu Yiwen’s backing would probably be more reliable. In other words, Zhu Yiwen’s company’s remote-sensing technology would be the dot in the dragon’s eye in this competition — the finishing touch that brought it to life.

The chapter on “Weak Points and Strong” in The Art of War says: “What the enemy wants is our vital point.”

Whether he could meet this industry heavyweight he’d heard about for so long, block his partnership with Yuanwang, even get him to partner with Xinghuan instead — that was the plan Zheng Zhiyong held deep in his heart. But it would be extremely hard to pull off. To say nothing of whether he could even get a meeting with the big shot: would the go-between who arranged it carry enough weight to vouch for him? How long would the meeting last? If he did manage to get a dinner, a round of drinks, maybe even a chance to briefly lay out the technical edge in Xinghuan’s overall solution — would Zhu Yiwen, thinking about the project’s odds of success, switch sides and partner with them?

Besides, his relationship with Yuanwang wasn’t a matter of a year or two; they had more than ten years of acquaintance and friendship. Just how close Zhu Yiwen and Gao Fei were personally — Zheng Zhiyong had no way at all to guess or judge.

Zheng Zhiyong felt enormous uncertainty about the project’s prospects, and huge pressure in body and mind.

Before long word came from Zhang Hailu: Zhu Yiwen had agreed to meet. One of the deep secrets of dealing with people in business is that the depth of a meeting is often inversely proportional to the number of people in the room. So Zheng Zhiyong wanted as few people as possible there when he met Zhu Yiwen. His plan was to get to know Song Li first, and then have Song Li come along to introduce him to Zhu Yiwen. So Zhang Hailu arranged it and went with him to meet this classmate of his at the academy, Song Li. The three of them had a barbecue-and-beer dinner, warmed up their old college brotherhood, and laid out clearly what was at stake in needing to meet Zhu Yiwen.

Three rounds in, Song Li said, “Zhang Hailu’s my dorm brother. On top of that, this time it’s his own business. Introducing a brother to somebody — and somebody I’m close with anyway — that’s no trouble for me. Naturally I won’t say no. Don’t worry, Mr. Zheng.”

Another week passed, and sure enough Song Li got a meeting with Zhu Yiwen. They arranged to meet at a classical little courtyard near the Summer Palace in Beijing, Huaxiang Cottage, and booked a small private room. Zhang Hailu’s classmate Song Li would keep them company. This time Zhang Hailu wouldn’t attend — only Song Li.

Zheng Zhiyong had two bottles of fifteen-year Moutai ready ahead of time, which shows just how seriously he took Zhu Yiwen.

On the day of the meeting, Old Zheng got there first, then Song Li arrived, and Old Zheng and Song Li agreed beforehand how the dinner would go. A little later Zhu Yiwen came gracefully into the private room.

Zhu Yiwen was a rather short middle-aged man who looked about fifty. He wore glasses, had kept himself in very good shape, and was dressed in a very well-fitted, trim casual suit, with half-rimmed silver-wire glasses. Though he wasn’t tall, he had an extraordinary air about him; the moment he entered the room, you couldn’t help feeling the sheer size of his presence.

Song Li stood to welcome him and led Zhu Yiwen to the guest-of-honor seat. Zhu Yiwen made a show of declining, then sat down. Song Li and Zheng Zhiyong took their seats on his left and right. The food had been ordered long before the guest of honor arrived, and when the waiter saw he had come, the dishes started coming one after another. First the cold platters: octopus in marinade, cucumber with termite mushrooms, iced cherry tomatoes, duck feet with mustard. After that came Shennong fish maw with abalone, steamed grouper, kung pao prawns and more.

Zheng Zhiyong opened the baijiu. As the cups went round between the three of them, at first it was mostly Song Li doing the talking. Later Zhu Yiwen gradually relaxed and told the story of how he and Song Li had worked together to launch the project back then, giving a brief account of how he’d built his business.

Zhu Yiwen summed up: “Teacher Song was an enormous help to me in starting my business, and from start to finish he was completely public-minded, fair and just. I admire his character from the bottom of my heart. A friend like that is very rare and precious.”

Song Li said, “Brother Yiwen, you flatter me. Being close friends with you is my good fortune and my blessing. Brother Yong here’s the same — he was a dorm brother of mine back in college, and we’ve been friends for twenty years. Now he’s starting his own business, and I hope you and Mr. Zheng get a chance to work together, so all our ties can grow deeper and deeper.”

The three of them drained their cups once more. Next they got on to Old Zheng’s recent experience starting his business, and to the project both sides were working on, which everyone knew about without saying so. Zheng Zhiyong was frank and open about his technical plans and the proposal he was writing, and Zhu Yiwen couldn’t help being startled inside. He’d never imagined that Yuanwang’s rival was sitting here drinking with him right now. More startling still: first, Zheng Zhiyong perhaps didn’t know that he was closer to Yuanwang, yet he’d laid his proposal out in full detail — wasn’t he afraid he’d pass the information on to Yuanwang? Second, this up-and-coming company, under Zheng Zhiyong’s leadership, apparently already had a clear lead over Yuanwang with its proposal, and the one module where it fell short of the other side was precisely his own company’s remote-sensing module. So from where Zhu Yiwen sat, looking at the contest between Yuanwang and the other side, if you factored in Yuanwang’s greater ability to work relationships inside the system, the two sides’ odds of winning were probably about even. Half the evening went by, and a whole bottle of Moutai was already empty. Just then Song Li’s phone rang. He stepped out of the room to take the call and came back and said to Zhu Yiwen, “Brother Yiwen, would you look at that. My wife just called — the kid’s got a high fever and she wants me home right away to take him to the hospital. You two carry on talking, all right?”

The other two expressed every understanding and sympathy. Zheng Zhiyong and Zhu Yiwen saw Song Li off, and the two of them sat back down.

By now they were into the second bottle. Old Zheng raised his cup and said, “Mr. Yiwen, getting to sit here with you today and have such an in-depth exchange, I really feel very lucky. I’ll drink mine first as a show of respect — you drink as you like.” And right after, he said, “Mr. Yiwen, actually I’d still like to keep asking your advice about the technical side of the project I was just telling you about. I wonder if it would be convenient for you to enlighten me a bit more.”

Zhu Yiwen said, “Mr. Zheng, since Teacher Song put in a word for you, I won’t stand on ceremony with you. The fact is, I’ve known all along that you’re Yuanwang’s rival on this project. And because I’ve already worked with Mr. Gao at Yuanwang a few times before, and we’re also discussing the possibility of our product coming in as a subcontracted product, further discussion really is a bit awkward. Still, the part you talked about tonight — I, Zhu, am very impressed. You really are a technical heavyweight. Your strength is no small thing.”

With that, Zhu Yiwen raised his cup and said with a laugh, “Mr. Zheng, here’s to you. Looks like I’ll need to warn Gao Fei to watch out, now that he has such a formidable rival.”

Old Zheng said, “Mr. Zhu, have you and Yuanwang gotten to the stage of a substantive link-up, or an agreement?”

Zhu Yiwen said with a laugh, “Not that. You know the nature of our industry — it’s always try before you buy.”

Old Zheng went on, “Mr. Zhu, I won’t hide it from you. I came out of Yuanwang myself. I used to be COO there.”

Zhu Yiwen was startled inside all over again and thought to himself: So that’s it, so that’s it. No wonder the whole technical plan pressed in step by step and beat Yuanwang hands down from every angle.

Zhu Yiwen asked, “When did you leave Yuanwang, Mr. Zheng?”

Old Zheng said, “More than half a year ago. The truth is, I’ve known for a long time that you and Yuanwang are very close. But I’ve held nothing back from you about the technical solution, either. You must see that on the overall solution alone we definitely have the advantage. So if you could work with me, we’d have an absolute advantage on this project, and we’d win this 20-million order.”

Zhu Yiwen said, “Our company’s module is only a very small subcontracted piece of the project. I’m afraid it isn’t all that important to you. And with my many years of friendship with Old Gao, I’m afraid I’ll have to say sorry to your proposal of working together.”

In fact Zhu Yiwen had already reached a verbal understanding with Gao Fei: Zhu Yiwen would come in on the subcontracted module with his company’s standard product plus some customization. His cost would be only 10 percent of the whole project’s cost, but he’d take 30 percent of the whole project’s revenue. And besides, six million in revenue meant nothing much to someone of Zhu Yiwen’s size these days. So why offend Gao Fei?

Old Zheng said, “Mr. Zhu, do you know why I left Yuanwang?”

Zhu Yiwen shook his head.

Zheng Zhiyong toasted him with another cup and then said, “Since we’ve come this far, I’ll let the liquor give me courage and not be afraid to tell you my own dirty laundry. Mr. Zhu, while I was working at Yuanwang I fell in love with a woman. This woman was also an important executive at Yuanwang. Because we loved each other so much, we agreed to get married. For her I divorced and walked away from my marriage with nothing, and promised my ex-wife three million in compensation down the road — all just to be with her.”

Zhu Yiwen was astonished. He raised his cup and said, “Come, Mr. Zheng, have one. Looks like you’re the type who’d give up the empire for the beauty!”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I loved this woman precisely because I loved the empire. She was the one who encouraged me to start a business, encouraged me to resign and leave Yuanwang, to give up the chance to be CEO and give up options worth tens of millions. She set up a company with me, introduced clients to me, let me take the first step in starting my own business. In my heart I’m grateful to her.”

Zhu Yiwen asked, “And that lady left Yuanwang with you too, and is building the business with you at your company?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “No. That’s exactly what I was about to tell you. About four months ago she cut me off without warning and married someone else.”

It seems that no matter how successful a man is, he has a gossip’s nature too. Zhu Yiwen asked, “Married whom?”

Zheng Zhiyong looked at Zhu Yiwen and said, “Gao Fei.”

Zhu Yiwen went pale with shock. “Gao Fei! How is that possible?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “It’s true. This woman was Gao Fei’s mistress. They’ve been together all these years. Seven years ago they already had a daughter outside his marriage. This woman’s been using every means she has to get Gao Fei to marry her.”

Inside Zhu Yiwen, huge waves were crashing now. “Mr. Zheng, I won’t hide it from you: the reason I know Gao Fei at all is his wife, Wang Yan. She was my classmate at university.”

Yes — Wang Yan was Sister Yan’er, Zhu Yiwen’s very own undergraduate classmate, the pure white rose of Zhu Yiwen’s heart in his college days. She was slender and tall, from a distinguished family. At school the two of them had been more than friends, less than lovers. He’d never expected that later Wang Yan would end up with Gao Fei, but the two of them had always stayed very close friends. So after Sister Yan’er founded Yuanwang, the cooperation between the two companies had in fact been built on that friendship. This part was something Old Zheng absolutely did not know and would never have a chance to find out; it was an absolute secret buried deep in Zhu Yiwen’s heart.

Zhu Yiwen went on, “So you’re saying Gao Fei and his first wife are already divorced? So Gao Fei betrayed his first wife as far back as seven years ago?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “As far as I know, yes.”

Zhu Yiwen shook his head with a bitter smile and said slowly, “The last time I saw Gao Fei was last year, when I was in Changsha on business. The two of them had dinner with me, and then the three of us went for drinks at a bar on the Xiang River. That day Gao Fei said this woman was the woman he would never let down as long as he lived. She’d started the business with him and carried him through the hard years; together they’d built the Yuanwang empire. This woman had given him a son and been so conscientious about the boy’s education that he hardly had to spend any effort on it himself. This woman had also helped him see his parents off — run around before and after, taken care of everything after they died — and stayed with him through the most painful stretch after his parents passed. He was going to treat her well for the rest of his life.”

Zheng Zhiyong didn’t know the deeper meaning behind Zhu Yiwen’s words. What he could understand was that successful people set store by an entrepreneur’s ability to keep his household in hand, set store by the stability of the first marriage, and set store by whether what a partner tells them is true and can be trusted. All of that was right, but none of it was enough to explain the deeper feelings stirring in Zhu Yiwen on learning all this — because Wang Yan was the woman Zhu Yiwen had always wished well in his heart.

Zheng Zhiyong sensed the change in Zhu Yiwen’s feelings and seized the moment to say to him, “Mr. Zhu, if our two companies work together, I’m willing to give you forty percent of the whole project budget — that’s eight million.”

Zhu Yiwen was quiet and didn’t speak right away. After a pause he raised his cup and said to Mr. Zheng, “Mr. Zheng, I need a little time to think it over. I’ll answer your proposal within three days. Come, let’s drain this cup.”

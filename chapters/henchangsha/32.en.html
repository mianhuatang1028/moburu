Jingwei

Chapter Thirty-Two: Jingwei

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Jingwei” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Northern Mountains”), the youngest daughter of the Flame Emperor drowned in the Eastern Sea and became a bird with a patterned head, a white beak and red feet, which forever carries wood and stones from the Western Mountains to fill up the Eastern Sea. As the saying goes: one stone, one grain at a time — the sea so deep you never see the shallows.

With the Sixth Academy contract now signed and the project in the bag, by rights he ought to have been able to breathe easy. But Zheng Zhiyong couldn’t. Because the money business still wasn’t over.

The Sixth Academy project needed six million fronted at the bare minimum, eight million on the looser estimate. Wu You had lent him two million personally, and the fan-light motor order from Wang Renhou in Dongguan brought in 1.2 million on delivery — 3.2 million between the two, still well short of the looser eight-million estimate. He sank every bit of that money into it, and had Zhang Hailu push the supply chain’s payment terms back wherever they could be pushed and negotiate them down wherever they could be negotiated, and just barely managed to kick off the start of work.

On the day work began, he stood in the factory building watching the first batch of outsourced parts come in, the workers starting to unpack, shelve and inspect them. Zhang Hailu was beside him going over drawings with the supplier’s people, and Cong Shasha was at the other end on the phone, chasing another material. He stood there, lit a cigarette, took two drags, pinched it out, and went over to look at the drawings with Zhang Hailu.

He didn’t dare smoke much. Every extra cigarette’s worth of time was one less page of drawings looked at.

Work had only just started here, a few days in, when something went wrong in Shanghai.

Wu You called, his tone heavier than usual. One of Xinghuan’s important shareholders had blown up using leverage in his investments, and there were already people at the court looking into his assets. In fact, on the day Old Zheng went to Shanghai to see Wu You last time, they had already been dealing with transferring that shareholder’s equity out of Xinghuan. To keep the company from being dragged in, they were going to use company money to buy back his equity, so that the company shell wouldn’t be affected by his debts and could go on operating cleanly afterward. Three million had been transferred off Xinghuan’s books to buy out the shares he held, and from now on those shares would become profit shares for the company’s key technical people.

Zheng Zhiyong held the phone and couldn’t say anything for a long while. Wu You went on, “Old Zheng, this money wasn’t urgent originally, but it’s urgent now — it’s helping an old big brother out in an emergency, and it’s for the safety of our own company and the project too. The money’s already been transferred out. The company changes are done as well. As for the partnership entity behind it — once we’ve delivered this project, we’ll change the partnership’s GP over to you, so you’ll have more shares in the company, and more say too.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Mr. Wu, I’ve only just started work on my end, and I need to front the money — six million minimum. Now there’s another three million gone from the books. I can’t carry it.”

Wu You said, “I know you can’t carry it. That’s exactly why I’m calling you. Old Zheng, listen to me. For this project, you find a way to keep it going for now. As for the money, I’ll figure something out later. But the project can’t stop. If it stops, everything we’ve put in up front goes down the drain. And this project is a beginning. After this, our reputation in the industry will be established — the door will be knocked open, and from there on it’ll get easier and easier.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I understand.”

When he hung up, he sat in his office and smoked a cigarette. When it was done he stubbed it out and called Zhang Hailu: “Hailu, come to my office.”

Zhang Hailu came, heard him out and said with a frown, “How much is left on the books?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “After this month’s salaries and the suppliers’ down payments, less than a million that we can actually use.”

Zhang Hailu said, “A million won’t last till next month.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I know. So we’ll have to find another way.”

The two of them sat smoking in the office and talking it over, going through every option they could think of once more. Banks? Xinghuan was an asset-light company, with nothing to put up as collateral. Friends? He still hadn’t paid back Lu You’s hundred thousand. Loan sharks? He didn’t dare touch them.

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Zhu Yiwen.”

Zhang Hailu said, “Didn’t he say the payment terms would have to wait until the contract was signed and the project locked in? Now the contract’s signed and the project’s locked in. You can go to him.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I may have to make a trip to Xiangcheng.”

Just as they were talking, his phone actually rang — and it was actually Zhu Yiwen, calling him first. Zheng Zhiyong held up the phone to show Zhang Hailu the caller’s name. Zhang Hailu waved his hands frantically, telling him to answer quick.

Zhu Yiwen’s tone wasn’t quite his usual one. There was a bit less of that business crispness and a bit more of something else. He said, “Mr. Zheng, the contract’s signed. Actually taking the project out of Yuanwang’s jaws — to be honest, I didn’t see that coming. You really are a young talent not to be underestimated. Whenever it’s convenient for you, come to Xiangcheng and let’s meet again, and talk about the payment terms.”

Just when you’re nodding off, someone hands you a pillow. Zheng Zhiyong said, “I’ll be there tomorrow.”

Zhu Yiwen said, “Good.”

What Zheng Zhiyong didn’t know was that the reason Zhu Yiwen had made this call was, of all things, that he had just seen Wang Yan in Xiangcheng — Sister Yan’er. Gao Fei’s first wife.

It turned out that just that afternoon, Zhu Yiwen and Sister Yan’er had met at a little teahouse in the south of the city. A teahouse, they called it, but really it was a place Zhu Yiwen ran himself. People in the defense business — a company of a certain size always needs a private club. When he wasn’t there it was open to the public too, though open to the public as it was, it hardly ever had more than a few guests.

The teahouse was down a lane in the old part of the city. The front wasn’t big; inside was a little courtyard with an old osmanthus tree, a tea table and a few bamboo chairs set out beneath it. Zhu Yiwen sat back in the host’s seat at a classical tea table, and across from him sat a woman with a great deal of poise. She was middle-aged, but her skin was fair and her bearing elegant, and she wore wire-rimmed glasses that gave her a cultivated, intellectual beauty.

This was Wang Yan — Sister Yan’er.

She was a little thinner than she had been a couple of years before. She wore a very light, soft pale purple cashmere cardigan, her hair simply pinned up. She seemed to have hardly any makeup on, but her skin was very fine-textured and her complexion rosy and glowing — every bit the air of a lady from a good family.

Zhu Yiwen and she sat on either side of the tea table, perfectly at ease. Zhu Yiwen chose a cup and handed it to Sister Yan’er — a celadon-green one inlaid with a ring of gold. Sister Yan’er raised the cup and said, “This is one of those Hongbao Ru-ware cups from Ruzhou, isn’t it? The crackle imitates the old Song porcelain. Very elegant.” Zhu Yiwen smiled and nodded that it was.

A waiter came to bring snacks and tea sweets; Zhu Yiwen waved a hand, and the waiter withdrew again. The courtyard was very quiet. The osmanthus wasn’t in bloom, and its leaves glinted in the sun.

Zhu Yiwen said, “I hear you and Gao Fei are divorced.”

Wang Yan said with a smile, “So you know too.”

Zhu Yiwen poured Sister Yan’er’s first cup of tea, picked up the cup with the tongs and tipped the tea into the waste bowl, then poured her another. Sister Yan’er took a light sip and said unhurriedly, “You want to ask why?”

Zhu Yiwen said, “If you’d like to tell me.”

Sister Yan’er said with a light laugh, “I don’t suppose this is any kind of secret in Xiangcheng’s whole defense circle by now. You must have heard it all.”

Zhu Yiwen paused and raised his own host’s cup — a collector’s piece of Jingdezhen blue-and-white. He looked at the cup, then drank a mouthful of tea. He looked at Sister Yan’er, waiting for her to tell it herself.

Sister Yan’er went on, “I knew long ago. When he first got together with the company’s salesperson, I sensed it. Later on, what year the child was born, what her name was, which kindergarten she went to — I came to know it all. Heaven sent every piece of information right in front of my eyes. Even when I covered my eyes myself, it was no use — it was as if he pried my hands away and put it all in front of my eyes. After that I kept pretending nothing had happened. I wanted to hold on to the marriage, because what was tangled up in it had long since stopped being just a matter between two people. Yuanwang’s equity, the assets, the debts, the relationships, the trust — it was all wound together. I put up with it for seven years. At the end of last year I’d gotten everything I was supposed to get. Meanwhile I’d been moving whatever money could be moved into an offshore trust, left for our son. And this year our son went off to university in England. I felt everything was more or less settled, and then I divorced him.”

Zhu Yiwen said, “So it was all part of your plan?”

Sister Yan’er said, “Yes, it was my goal. This divorce — on paper it was a split to protect the family’s assets for Gao Fei, but really it was the two of us genuinely splitting apart. For Gao Fei, he could be with his little lover and give the daughter over there her proper place. But for me — the debt from Yuanwang’s funding bet is about to come due, there’s no hope of an IPO, and sooner or later the creditors will come knocking. I divorced Gao Fei, the cash and the properties are on my side, and he keeps all the equity. On the surface I gave up the company; in reality I protected every asset that could be protected. What’s left on his side is an empty shell. The real state of Yuanwang right now is probably even worse than the rumors out there. The bank loans keep snowballing. Even if he sold Yuanwang Tower, it might still be insolvent.”

Zhu Yiwen: “Gao Fei doesn’t know what you really had in mind?”

Sister Yan’er said, “I’ve withdrawn from the company’s affairs completely, which is what he wanted too. That girl’s relatives have taken over the part my brother used to run, so it looks as if I’m gradually cutting myself off from him. I still hold a title at Yuanwang, but I haven’t been in for half a year. And though I don’t go in, everyone in the company from top to bottom is my person. I’m not planning to run anything anymore, but everything that happens on Gao Fei’s side still reaches my ears, all of it.”

She stopped here and glanced at Zhu Yiwen.

Zhu Yiwen asked, “Do you hate Gao Fei?”

Wang Yan said, “I don’t hate him. I think he couldn’t help it either; I think he’s pitiable. The day we signed the bet and took the funding, we’d already started down a road of no return. It wasn’t the road I chose. All I could do was protect myself step by step, and steer him into splitting from me the way I aimed for, so the assets were kept safe. And he was willing to go along with me in doing it. I think he loves our son too, and cares for the two of us.”

Zhu Yiwen poured Sister Yan’er another cup, and the two of them slowly drank. Sister Yan’er went on, “When you agreed back then to do business with him, I knew you didn’t want to, but you worked together all these years to help me. Now you’re not working with him anymore — that’s part of my goal too, it’s what I want as well. I hope you won’t get tangled up in Yuanwang’s business again either. Cut it off as early as you can. I know Yuanwang still owes you a few million in payables. I’ll get finance to pay you over the next few months, and after that I really won’t have anything more to do with the company at all.”

Zhu Yiwen said, “You know I didn’t ask to see you to talk about what’s owed.”

Sister Yan’er smiled. “I know. But this is what I have to tell you.” With that she drank off her cup of tea.

Zhu Yiwen said, “Wang Yan, you know I’ve been divorced for ten years now?”

Sister Yan’er said, “I know.”

Zhu Yiwen smiled. “Being divorced is fine. Free and easy. Though being married might be even better.”

Sister Yan’er said, “I know what you’re about to say. But do you know how long we’ve known each other?”

Zhu Yiwen said, “Thirty-five years.”

Sister Yan’er said, “I’ve known you longer than I’ve known Gao Fei. I hope we’ll always be the very best of friends. In life there have to be some relationships that can stay the same forever!”

The two raised their cups. “To thirty-five years of friendship.”

Then Zheng Zhiyong arrived in Xiangcheng too, and met Zhu Yiwen at his office. After a few light pleasantries standing at the door of Zhu Yiwen’s office, Zhu Yiwen said, “Mr. Zheng, for this collaboration of ours, I’m willing to back you with three months’ payment terms, no advance payment, and we’ll get started on our end in parallel as soon as possible.” Old Zheng was stunned. Three months’ payment terms meant Zhu Yiwen would front Gaoda’s own costs, and wait until the Sixth Academy’s first payment came back before collecting. That was real, solid help, and it also meant the two companies’ cooperation had gone from a contractual arrangement to a true strategic alliance.

Zheng Zhiyong said excitedly, “Mr. Zhu, I don’t know what to say. This is so unexpected, I’m so moved. But — why such a big change, all of a sudden?”

Zhu Yiwen said, “Two reasons. First, on this Sixth Academy project, your solution really is much stronger than Yuanwang’s. Working with you, the project has a better chance of success. Second—” he said, “I think your technical skills are solid. Together we can open up more markets and more opportunities.”

In the world of grown-ups, the truth is often the part left unsaid. Zheng Zhiyong would never learn of Zhu Yiwen’s meeting with Wang Yan the day before, still less that Wang Yan had become Gao Fei’s opponent — the one pulling the firewood out from under his pot.

Zheng Zhiyong was beside himself with excitement. “Mr. Zhu, I’ll do my very best, I won’t let your trust down. I’m going to make sure…”

Zhu Yiwen said with a smile, “Mr. Zheng, don’t just stand there. Let’s sit down and have some tea.” And he led Zheng Zhiyong over to the tea table. The two of them sat down, and they talked from the afternoon right through to evening…

When Zheng Zhiyong came out of Zhu Yiwen’s office, it was already fully dark. The Xiangcheng night wind was damper than Xi’an’s, a little cool on his face. He stood at the mouth of the lane, lit a cigarette, took two drags and called Zhang Hailu.

“Hailu, Zhu Yiwen’s agreed to three months’ payment terms. And he’s got a few suppliers on his side who can front materials first. Tomorrow you get together with his finance people, sign the agreement, get the list of suppliers, and we’ll line up the order to start work.”

Zhang Hailu was silent on the other end for two seconds, then said, “Mr. Zheng, you’ve won Zhu Yiwen over?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “It wasn’t me who won him over. I don’t know what happened. It’s as if some force gave him a push.”

Zhang Hailu said, “What do you mean?”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I really don’t know. Maybe heaven’s out to finish off Yuanwang.”

He hung up, finished his cigarette, stubbed it out in the trash can at the mouth of the lane, hailed a taxi and went back to the hotel. Early the next morning he flew from Xiangcheng back to Xi’an. When the plane landed and he turned on his phone, a WeChat from Wu Peng popped up: “Mr. Zheng, Mr. Wu says that of the three million that shareholder took out, he’ll put one million back first, and he’ll find a way for the remaining two million next month. He says for you to hang on for now.”

Zheng Zhiyong looked at the message for a few seconds and replied with one word: “Okay.”

Back at the company, he called Zhang Hailu and Cong Shasha into his office and told them how things stood. Zhu Yiwen’s three months’ terms, suppliers fronting materials, Wu You putting a million back first and finding a way for the rest next month. The money they could use on the books, plus this million, was just barely enough to hold out till the middle of next month.

Zhang Hailu said, “If we hold out till the middle of next month, the Sixth Academy’s first payment still won’t be back. There’s still a gap in between.” Zheng Zhiyong said, “I know. So in Dongguan, Wang Renhou’s orders — we’ve got to take another one. That fan-light motor of his, the second batch of orders is about to come through. I’ll talk to him and get him to pay part in advance.”

Cong Shasha said, “Mr. Zheng, you’re amazing. I think our luck’s turning.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Good. Shasha, you’ve done more than anyone this time — keep looking after the client relationships. Hailu, you keep an eye on sample confirmation for the Sixth Academy project, and the production scheduling and delivery after that. I’ll go talk to Dongguan again. The three of us, each keep an eye on one end.”

Cong Shasha said, “Okay.”

Zhang Hailu said too, “Right.”

The three of them went their separate ways to see to their own business. Zheng Zhiyong sat in his office and went over in his head what had to be done next. Then he lit a cigarette and thought of last winter, sitting in his old office with dead fish floating in the tank, drinking tea alone across from Lu You, feeling his life was over. Back then he had nothing; he didn’t even know where next month’s rent would come from. Now he had a company, a twenty-million project just signed, a group of people working with him, Zhu Yiwen’s payment terms, Wu You’s advance, and Cong Shasha and Zhang Hailu holding him up, one on his left and one on his right.

He thought of Jingwei filling the sea. That bird, every day carrying wood and stones from the Western Mountains and dropping them into the Eastern Sea. The sea so vast, the wood and stones so small — it filled for a whole lifetime and could never fill it level. And still it kept on filling.

Zheng Zhiyong stubbed out his cigarette, sat back down at his desk, opened his computer and started writing the talking points for negotiating Wang Renhou’s second batch of orders in Dongguan.

Outside the window the Xi’an sky was gray and murky, as if it were about to rain, or as if it couldn’t quite manage to. He glanced at it, lowered his head and went on writing.

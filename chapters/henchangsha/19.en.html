Dangkang

Chapter Nineteen: Dangkang

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Dangkang” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Eastern Mountains, Book Four”), on Mount Qin there is a beast shaped like a pig with tusks, called the dangkang; it cries out its own name, and when it appears, the whole land has a bumper harvest. As the saying goes: when it appears, the harvest is great — but even in a good year, someone has to till the fields.

Fortune is a roller coaster. Just like that, a ready-made company dropped out of the sky and landed, as if in a play, right on Zheng Zhiyong’s head. The turnaround came so fast that it seemed almost too good to be true.

The Book of Changes says: “When obstruction reaches its extreme, peace arrives.”

As if the dangkang had passed by, foretelling a year of plenty, the turning point of his life had descended on Old Zheng in all its glory.

Whether he was drunk on the liquor or dreaming, Old Zheng felt dizzy and dazzled, his heart racing, beside himself with excitement.

When he had heard Wu You out, Old Zheng answered earnestly, “I have no problem at all with any of it. At the lowest point of my life you’ve given me such enormous trust and placed this in my hands — you are the greatest benefactor I, Zheng Zhiyong, will ever have. As the saying goes, horses that can run a thousand li are common, but a Bole who can spot one is rare. Now that I have this chance, I’ll put everything I’ve got into repaying you and the other shareholders. Whether this is fate or fortune, I’ll give it my all to repay the trust you and the other shareholders have placed in me today.”

Wu You then gave him a quick introduction to the other four investing shareholders. Judging by their industries and backgrounds, each really did have a unique circle of competence in their own field; the problem was that everyone’s main business was going too well, so this business had no one dedicated to it. Once this winter was over, Xinghuan Times had been slated to shut down for good or be sold.

Once again Old Zheng was deeply shaken. This company truly had everything in place except the east wind; the only thing it lacked was an operator like him — technically up to the job, of solid character, seasoned. It seemed that Old Zheng meeting Xinghuan at his dead end, or Xinghuan meeting Old Zheng at its doomsday, had happened at precisely the right moment — the most fitting match for each other.

When they had eaten and drunk their fill, Old Zheng rode back with Wu You in his van to the little four-story building in the technology park. During dinner Wu You had had his secretary book Old Zheng a hotel nearby, but Old Zheng had said, “Could I go and look at the company again tonight? You said there’s a dorm on the fourth floor too. If it’s convenient, I’d rather stay in the company dorm you mentioned. Would that be all right?” So after dinner the two rode back.

Coming back to this four-story building now, Old Zheng felt as if worlds — whole oceans of stars — separated him from the man he had been a few hours before. The quirky little building stood alone in the dark of a cold winter night, and a mysterious orange light fell over it, as if a thin mist had risen around it; nothing seemed quite real. Old Zheng stood lost in thought for a moment: from now on, this would be the next battlefield of his life.

“Let me show Mr. Zheng the fourth-floor dorm again.” Wu You took Old Zheng up in the elevator to the fourth floor. On the tour a few hours earlier, since the floor was empty, they had only glanced at it in passing. This time Wu You switched on the light and unlocked a door with a key, opening onto a spacious room. Along the far wall stood a full row of bookshelves with a row of books on them. When he gave the rightmost bookshelf a gentle push, it turned out to be a folding hidden door; it swung easily on its pivot, and behind it was a quiet, spacious room of about sixty square meters. The first thing you saw was a whole wall of floor-to-ceiling windows; there was an imposing two-meter bed, a writing desk, a wraparound sofa, a simple pantry cabinet, a refrigerator, a wine cabinet and a wardrobe, with a slatted, striped screen dividing the sofa from the bed. Farther in was an en-suite bathroom and toilet, its walls and floor in black marble veined with gold. The bathroom had floor-to-ceiling windows too, and there was even a huge freestanding bathtub right by the window, seemingly of natural stone and uniquely shaped; beneath the metal shower head was an inconspicuous English mark: “Salvatori.”

Old Zheng followed him in and walked over to the tub. Through the floor-to-ceiling window he could see there were no office buildings behind, only an artificial water feature, with walking paths and pavilions in the distance connecting to the city’s public gardens and green space. The view was wide open, yet inside it was completely private and lovely. Once again Old Zheng felt a sense of unreality; he’d never have guessed the place would be fitted out in such extreme luxury.

Wu You said, “When we did this room, we had a designer friend do it with no budget limit. The plumbing and electrics are all done. Actually none of us has ever stayed here. If you don’t mind living at the company, this is your dorm from now on.”

After Wu You left, Old Zheng fell straight backward, stiff as a board, onto the two-meter bed; his body sank into the soft bedding, and he lay staring up at the ceiling.

And so the most magical day of his life came to an end.

For several days Old Zheng stayed on in Xi’an. During that time he met two more of the Xi’an shareholders; though in different industries, each was indeed a leader in their field. At the company, his name had already been submitted for business registration, the change of legal representative had been filed, and initial talks with the seven technical staff were done. Old Zheng worked out the organizational setup that would let them start doing business fastest, and for now they were still missing a sales lead and a technical lead for software development.

As it happened, a client in Xiangtan, Hunan, had invited him earlier, so Old Zheng decided to go there in a few days for talks. He booked his flight, and then saw on WeChat that Cong Shasha, a sales manager at Yuanwang responsible for the Hunan region, had posted a farewell on her Moments, saying she was leaving Yuanwang after seven years. Old Zheng immediately phoned her.

“Shasha, why’d you suddenly quit?”

“Oh, my health hasn’t been great lately. I want to go back home to Xiangtan and rest for a while.”

“What a coincidence — I’m arriving in Xiangtan the day after tomorrow.”

That evening the two met for dinner at a Chinese-style courtyard restaurant in Xiangtan called Fisherman’s Village Courtyard, to eat fish and talk. They ordered tube fish, steamed cured meats, frog with perilla and stone-pot farmhouse tofu, plus a bottle of Hunan’s own Jiugui liquor.

When Cong Shasha saw Old Zheng she said, “Mr. Zheng, I haven’t seen you in months. You’ve lost a lot of weight, but you look great — glowing.”

Old Zheng: “Shasha, you always know just what to say. Tell me why you quit.”

Shasha said, “Same reason as you. You were the company’s COO — you must know what I’m talking about?”

Old Zheng: “The receivables problem still hasn’t been solved?”

Cong Shasha said, “That’s right, and I don’t think it’s going to be easy to solve from here.”

In fact, there had been early signs of trouble with receivables while Old Zheng was still at Yuanwang, but finance was run by Sister Yan’er’s department, and as a COO who hadn’t been there long, Old Zheng had mainly focused on new business growth and hadn’t followed up much on older projects. Some things had reached his ears at the company back then, but always in scraps and fragments. Cong Shasha was in Sales Department Two, based locally in Hunan, so she was better informed than Zheng Zhiyong, who traveled all over and was based in Beijing most of the time.

“If the receivables can’t be collected, we salespeople never get our commission, and the company keeps using that as an excuse to pass the buck. I won’t hide it from you: I’m the sales manager who handles seventy percent of the company’s clients and all the day-to-day stuff, but because that part of my pay never comes through, here we are nearly at year-end and I’ve made less than two hundred thousand this whole year. I’ve been at this company seven years. My position hasn’t changed once, and my salary’s hardly changed either. Without commission, I’m making less than I did a few years ago.” Cong Shasha looked bitterly aggrieved.

Since Old Zheng knew that Diao Kexin’s commission last year had been 2.3 million after tax, he was secretly shocked by the gap between the two of them, but for now he didn’t let it slip.

He asked, “Shasha, didn’t Ms. Diao join the company around the same time as you? Did you know she has a daughter?”

Cong Shasha immediately lit up with excitement and said mysteriously, “I know about that. You know what? For the first half year we both lived in the staff dorm at Changsha headquarters — in our first year at the company we even shared a room for most of the year. She’s seven years older than me, but when we joined we were at the same level and made the same money. But then she got pregnant, and she got promoted too, and went to Beijing to run the sales team. I know she had a daughter — I’ve just always wondered whose kid it is.”

Cong Shasha went on, “She got divorced not long after joining the company, so the daughter isn’t her ex-husband’s. I’ve seen how she is with clients. I think the kid must belong to some big client in Beijing, but that’s just my guess — I’ve got no proof. Since the client isn’t divorced, Ms. Diao can’t say who the father is; but because the client’s so important, she went to Beijing specifically to look after his business, and the boss promoted her. Think about it — our results weren’t that different, and on paper I report to her. My pay’s probably not even half of hers, is it?”

Old Zheng asked, “Shasha, you don’t know how much Ms. Diao makes?”

Cong Shasha said, “Going by how results are calculated, at least double what I make, because I’m in her reporting line, so my numbers count toward hers too.”

Old Zheng said, “Shasha, did you know Ms. Diao’s commission last year was in the millions?”

“2.3 million” — summing it up lightly as “in the millions” was the last remnant of tenderness Zheng Zhiyong had for Diao Kexin. When he’d been with her, he’d known perfectly well that on top of that 2.3 million after tax there was a six-hundred-thousand base salary, plus cash she could ask the chairman for at any time for “business needs” and spend however she liked.

“What?” Cong Shasha’s eyes went wide. This was something she had never imagined.

Old Zheng went on, “And did you know Diao Kexin’s daughter is the chairman’s?”

Cong Shasha’s jaw dropped and her eyes went round; for a moment she couldn’t say a word.

“The chairman’s kid?” Cong Shasha repeated in disbelief. “Does Ms. Yan’er know?”

“She should.”

“That kid must be seven by now. Back then she’d wrap her belly in cloth every morning before work — I knew even then.” Cong Shasha said, “No wonder there are rumors at the company that Sister Yan’er’s divorced the chairman, and that’s why the company’s out of money.”

“The company’s out of money?” Zheng Zhiyong asked.

“Yes. The receivables can’t be collected, and some people in finance are saying a lot of the company’s money’s been transferred out, so a lot of us can forget about our bonuses and commission,” Cong Shasha said. “So when her numbers were the same as mine, she made several times what I did. Because she’s the chairman’s bit on the side!”

“More than ten times,” Zheng Zhiyong said silently to himself, but he only nodded slightly.

“Holy— shit—!” the twenty-eight-year-old exclaimed loudly. “So that’s how it is! That’s so unfair!”

Zheng Zhiyong pressed her: “The company having no money on the books — is that confirmed?”

“I don’t know. The atmosphere at the company’s a mess lately. Ms. Yan’er hardly comes in. You know how easygoing and approachable Ms. Yan’er is — but lots of colleagues say that a few months ago they saw her fighting with the chairman in the office, a huge, earth-shaking row. You know why everyone’s saying they’ve divorced? There’s something else: Sister Yan’er and her brother aren’t registered shareholders of the company anymore.”

“Oh!” Cong Shasha cried out, as if waking from a dream. “Did the chairman marry Diao Kexin? Is that why Ms. Yan’er left?” Her expression said that suddenly it all made sense; inside that twenty-eight-year-old head, a lot of threads, visible and hidden, had just joined up!

“I’m so angry!” Cong Shasha said. “I feel like a fucking idiot for busting my ass for them all these years! This is just too much — was it all an act? Did Ms. Yan’er really know they had that kind of relationship?”

“Supposedly she’s known for a long time!”

“Then she really should take all the money! You know what? This company was started by Ms. Yan’er and her brother. When I joined, before Mr. Zhao Bin there was a technical boss, Mr. Zhang Hailu. Ms. Yan’er and Mr. Zhang Hailu built this company with their own hands; the chairman only came in later, and not long after he came in the company hit a 1.5 billion valuation! That’s around when Ms. Diao and I joined.”

“How do you know what happened before you came?” Zheng Zhiyong asked.

Cong Shasha said, “Mr. Zhang Hailu was an especially good boss, and brilliant technically too. He left two years ago. The main money-making technology the company has now was all built by him. Mr. Zhao Bin, our current CTO, used to work under him. I’m close with Mr. Hailu because he’s from Xiangtan too — our hometowns are really close — so we’ve always talked a lot. I still chat with him on WeChat all the time.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “I see.” He paused, then said, “Shasha, let’s clink glasses. After this one, I have something important to tell you — something about me.”

Once again Cong Shasha’s eyes shot out a look of anticipation and surprise. She knocked back the Jiugui in front of her in one go and said, “Go on, go on.”

Zheng Zhiyong said, “Not long after I joined the company, Diao Kexin slept with me. We were together for most of a year. Because of it I got divorced and quit my job, but she dumped me so she could marry the chairman. And the chairman knows about it too.”

Once again Cong Shasha’s eyes went wide and her jaw dropped, and she cried out.

Over his drink, Zheng Zhiyong told her, in bits and pieces, the gist of what had happened with Diao Kexin; he also told her how, when they’d started a company together, Diao Kexin had walked off with all the money, what a wretched state he’d been in, and how he’d later met the shareholders of his current company. Told in detail, it all took a good while more.

Then Zheng Zhiyong said, “Right now I have no other thoughts. I’ve got a company of my own now. My goal is to beat Yuanwang. I’m going to take back what I lost, bit by bit.”

“Shasha, would you consider joining my company? I’m inviting you to come to Xi’an and see how things stand there.”

After a moment’s hesitation, Shasha agreed to go back to Xi’an with Zheng Zhiyong and take a look.

Zheng Zhiyong went on, “See if you can get your old technical director, Zhang Hailu, to meet me. I’d like to get to know him. Would that be possible?”

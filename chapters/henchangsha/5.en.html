Chapter Five: Diao

Translation first published: 2026-11-10 09:40 (UTC+8) First published here: 2026-11-09 21:27 (UTC+8) © 2026 Moburu · All rights reserved
“Diao” — the eagle, the great raptor of the Classic of Mountains and Seas. It watches from on high, circling without haste; when its talons drop, it is over in an instant.

Diao Kexin was a Miao girl, not tall — about a hundred and fifty-five centimeters — curvy front and back, with a smile like a flower in bloom.

She had turned thirty-four this year and was sales director at Hunan Yuanwang. She came from the Miao and Dong autonomous prefecture in Guizhou; her complexion was a healthy wheat color, and when she smiled, a faint, fine, barely-there dimple appeared on her left cheek.

Zheng Zhiyong first met her at Yuanwang’s clubhouse in the Western Hills, a detached villa with a stone facade, very elegant and dignified in style, set in its own walled courtyard full of trees, flowers and grass — secluded and beautiful — with a large open space that could park several cars. That day Yuanwang’s boss, Gao Fei, to thank Old Zheng for remotely solving a technical problem that had dogged his company for a long time over the past few months, had had his CTO invite him to the clubhouse for a banquet — a chance to meet in person and to introduce him to some people from the company and some outside friends. They had even prepared a whole roast lamb in the courtyard as the centerpiece of the day. After a quick tour of the clubhouse, Old Zheng sat on the sofa for a while and talked with Gao Fei, the CTO keeping them company. Gao Fei asked briefly about Zheng Zhiyong’s background, then told him about his own path as an entrepreneur.

Gao Fei was fifty-four and had founded this company eleven years earlier. Before that he had been the youngest professor in Xiangnan University’s School of Aeronautics and Astronautics. Later some business opportunities came along naturally, so he had his wife and his brother-in-law set up Yuanwang first. His wife was nominally the major shareholder and handled finance and personnel; his brother-in-law was a minor shareholder who dealt with clients and delivery; and he himself kept using the university’s resources to bring in clients while continuing to teach for three more years. Only eight years ago, when the business had grown bigger and bigger and annual sales passed two hundred million, did Gao Fei resign from Xiangnan University and become Yuanwang’s chairman. Thanks to Gao Fei’s influence in the industry, plus several solid technical results converted inside the company, it quickly took industrial investment from the local government — raising two hundred million that year at a valuation of 1.5 billion yuan. After that the company raised no more money, and the business grew very steadily. It had now built a ten-story Yuanwang Tower in Changsha as the main building of its industrial park, which covered more than a dozen mu, standing tall on the bank of the Xiang River. Through this conversation Old Zheng came to realize that Yuanwang had long since stopped being just a SAR company. It had positions up and down the whole industry chain — satellite manufacturing, satellite remote sensing, SAR equipment manufacturing, data collection, data applications and imaging, simulation and more — and its business had already reached several hundred million yuan.

Old Zheng looked at the chairman in front of him. Though he was fifty-four, he stood tall and straight and wore framed glasses; refined and courteous, he looked no more than his forties, had none of a middle-aged man’s greasiness, and carried instead the mild, cultivated air of an intellectual. The two talked to their hearts’ content, and when they had talked enough, Old Zheng got up to use the bathroom. Just at the door, he collided head-on with a young woman coming in.

She had curly hair past her shoulders and wore a knee-length camel cashmere coat, a small LV handbag in her hand. After bumping into Old Zheng she showed not the slightest displeasure; she simply nodded at him slightly by way of greeting and went on toward the many other guests, greeting everyone with a smile as she went. “Oh, Mr. Wu, you’re here too!” she called out, gay and loud. As if she were the mistress of the villa, she casually took off her cashmere coat and tossed it over the sofa; underneath was a fitted white Loro Piana cashmere dress, top and skirt in two pieces worn layered, showing off her curves just so; she looked round, small and plump in a coquettish way.

“Shall we play some guandan first?” she called out cheerfully.

“The lamb’s nearly done. Let’s eat the lamb and then play cards,” Gao Fei said.

Because of the roast lamb, the whole gathering was a loose affair of conversation, with people going off now and then to get lamb. Throughout, Diao Kexin never once turned her attention to Old Zheng again, but Old Zheng couldn’t stop looking at her as she laughed and chatted.

As it got late, those who had finished eating split into two tables for guandan, and Old Zheng got up and said goodbye.

A few days later, as it happened, Old Zheng’s work at Hanhai took him to visit a client at the Seventh Academy. Walking in through the main gate, he saw Gao Fei leading Diao Kexin out. Maybe because New Year was only a few days away, Diao Kexin was wearing a long bright-red wool coat and tight jeans.

Gao Fei saw Zheng Zhiyong and held out his hand first. “Engineer Zheng, hello! What a coincidence, running into you here!”

Zheng Zhiyong hurried to clasp Gao Fei’s hand in both of his. “Mr. Gao, this really is fate — only a few days, and here we meet again.”

A flicker of hesitation crossed Diao Kexin’s eyes. She tugged at Gao Fei’s sleeve, turned her face and asked in a soft, breathy whisper, “Who’s this?”

Gao Fei laughed. “Didn’t you just meet him at the clubhouse the other day? Engineer Zheng — the expert who solved our technical problem!”

Only then did Diao Kexin look as though it had all come back to her, and she too held out her hand and gave Old Zheng’s a light shake.

Gao Fei said, “Engineer Zheng is a real technical heavyweight. Something our company had been working on for two or three years, Engineer Zheng solved remotely in two weeks without even being on site. Truly amazing.”

Diao Kexin quickly, with her knack for reading the room, said, “Oh, Mr. Zheng, if we ever had the chance to invite you to join us at Yuanwang, wouldn’t that be perfect? Would you mind adding me on WeChat?” So the two scanned each other’s WeChat right in front of Gao Fei, said goodbye and parted.

Diao Kexin invited Old Zheng on WeChat to a Xinjiang restaurant — less than a week after their chance encounter at the academy. After work Old Zheng, laptop bag on his back, went to the appointed place. At the restaurant there wasn’t much small talk to make, so Old Zheng took out his laptop and explained to Diao Kexin in detail how he had solved the company’s technical problem.

They sat facing each other; the food hadn’t come yet. Old Zheng pushed the laptop to the middle of the table and earnestly explained, pointing at the diagrams he had drawn. Diao Kexin rested her chin in her right hand, both eyes fixed on Old Zheng; her eyes didn’t follow his explanation to the screen at all. Those big eyes simply fluttered and stared straight at Zheng Zhiyong, staring and smiling.

Zheng Zhiyong could feel that gaze pelting down on the top of his head. He glanced up: Diao Kexin, her eyes smiling, was watching him with a particularly soft, laughing look. She said gently, “Brother Yong — can I call you Brother Yong?”

Old Zheng didn’t dare look at Diao Kexin. Staring at the laptop, he said, “Better call me Engineer Zheng. Most people call me Engineer Zheng.”

Diao Kexin, still with her chin in her hand and her eyes hot on him, said, “Then what does your wife call you?”

Old Zheng said, “My wife calls me by my full name, Zheng Zhiyong.” Then Old Zheng added, “My wife’s very, very beautiful, tall, a really good person, good to me too. We have a son in primary school.”

Diao Kexin listened, smiled, and said, “Engineer Zheng, I’ll be honest with you. The chairman and I both have our eye on you. Would you be willing to consider coming over to our company? Our company really needs someone like you.”

Old Zheng said, “There’s no shortage of people like me. With my skills, you could grab a fistful at any academy or institute.”

Diao Kexin, fluttering her eyes, said, “You’re not like them. You have skills, and you have vision. Professor Gao has his eye on you. Just tell me — what are your salary expectations?”

Old Zheng felt a little panicked; it seemed this dinner had been planned in advance. As he packed the laptop into his bag, he looked up and answered, “I’ve been at Hanhai less than half a year. I’m very happy at this company and I’ve learned a lot. I have no plans to leave right now.”

Just then the waiter came over and asked, “Shall we bring the food?”

Diao Kexin said, “Yes.” Then she turned back to Old Zheng. “Whatever your current company pays you, we’ll give you a twenty percent raise on top. And if you join, we’ll give you the COO position, and if you make a success of it, after a year Professor Gao has promised you options worth tens of millions. Think about this opportunity.”

When the hand-pulled mutton was brought out, Diao Kexin was in no hurry to eat; she kept her chin in her hand and kept smiling at Old Zheng.

Old Zheng thought, “This girl is something.”

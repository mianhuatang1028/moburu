Chapter Forty-Six: Xingxing

“Xingxing” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Southern Mountains”), on Mount Zhaoyao there is a beast shaped like an ape with white ears; it crouches to walk and runs like a man, and its name is the xingxing; eaten, it makes a person a good runner. It is also said that the xingxing knows people’s names and can speak, but does not know where it is going. As the saying goes: it knows other men’s names but not its own going — it sees others clearly, and itself not at all.

Beijing, deep winter.

The teahouse stood in a lane behind the Seventh Academy, a small front, four or five tables inside. When Shasha arrived, Xiao Jing was already seated at the table towards the back. She wore a dark grey high-necked jumper, and her hair had grown past her shoulders. Xiao Jing, once the crisp, short-haired professional, had turned into a gentle woman with hair falling to her shoulders, and without anyone noticing, over these year or two, her temper had grown a little milder.

“Xiao Cong, over here!” Xiao Jing raised a hand to wave Cong Shasha over. “At work everyone calls you Director Cong; there’s nobody else here today, so I’ll call you Shasha — you don’t mind?”

Shasha answered warmly at once: “Of course that’s fine. Then may I call you Sister Jing?”

The two exchanged a smile. Xiao Jing said: “Shasha, you know what I want to ask you, don’t you?”

“I don’t, Sister Jing.” Shasha’s hand paused as she lifted her tea.

“What I actually want to ask is — you and Zheng Zhiyong, are you a couple?” Xiao Jing’s tone was very level. A woman’s sixth sense is a strange thing; half a year ago, when Xiao Jing had dinner with Cong Shasha and Zheng Zhiyong, she had already felt that these two were not simple.

Shasha set the tea down and gave a wry laugh. “Sister, honestly, no. I’m just a minor subordinate of Mr Zheng’s. Director, they call me, but in fact I’m a one-man band, no team at all. I’m just a wage earner in our company, cadging a living.”

Xiao Jing looked at her. “Then you’ve been with him over a year — do you know about the two of us?”

Shasha smiled, faintly. “Dr Xiao, Mr Zheng and I are senior and subordinate. Never mind that I follow him to Beijing all the time — about him and you, I honestly know nothing.” In her heart Shasha understood perfectly well: every time Zheng Zhiyong took her on a trip to Beijing, the two of them invariably stayed together. But there had also been times when he came early and left late, and every time she saw Xiao Jing and Zheng Zhiyong together she had had her own intuition then, and known that the relationship between them was not an ordinary one.

“You really don’t know?” Xiao Jing repeated it, in a tone that was neither believing nor disbelieving.

“I know a little, maybe. Because at the very beginning I said to Mr Zheng that you were the one person on the whole project who really understood the technology, that only if you helped us could we pull it off. And later Mr Zheng said it too — that you were one of us.” Shasha said.

Shasha had deliberately described the relationship as that of an invisible partner, and so Xiao Jing was confirmed in this: Shasha really had nothing to do with Old Zheng, and Old Zheng had told Shasha nothing about her own affair. Xiao Jing was silent for a while. She looked down and drank some tea, and when she raised her eyes again she had changed direction. “Then do you know that a year ago, Mr Zheng and I were together for a while?”

Shasha put on a show of astonishment. “Ah. When was that? I had no idea at all.”

Xiao Jing said, with deliberate calm: “I was with him as my boyfriend. Back then, every time he came to Beijing on business, he stayed at my home too. Altogether it was about half a year, and then we broke up.”

Shasha said: “Why did you break up? Honestly, Dr Xiao, the two of you — on technical strength alone, I’d have thought you’d get on rather well.”

Xiao Jing said: “He felt that between us it was a client-contractor relationship, and inconvenient to make public. I felt that what I wanted was a simple partner. So afterwards we separated.”

Shasha said, lightly, as if it were nothing: “No wonder. Later Mr Zheng took up with a girl from Chengdu.”

Xiao Jing was curious. “Oh? When was that?”

“When it started I don’t know. She came to our company last year. I’d guess after the two of you broke up? Twenty-nine, she does livestreaming, and she looks like someone.” Shasha said. “Dr Xiao, do you know Diao Kexin?”

Xiao Jing held her teacup, her finger pausing on the rim. Diao Kexin was the woman boss of Yuanwang; lately it was said she had taken over Gao Fei’s work and become the company’s actual controller, and recently she had been working the Seventh Academy line herself, which meant business dealings with Dr Xiao too. “I know her. I’ve even seen her recently.”

“Then what I’m about to tell you, you must keep secret. You can’t tell anyone it came from me. Sister, promise me, and I’ll tell you.” Xiao Jing nodded, and so Shasha told her, point by point, about Zheng Zhiyong’s departure from Yuanwang and about his love affair with Diao Kexin. When Xiao Jing had been with Old Zheng she had only vaguely known that he was divorced; of these details about Diao Kexin she had known nothing at all. She had not expected that a meeting with Shasha would dig up quite such a melon.

“Dr Xiao, this Chengdu girl looks exactly like Diao Kexin, down to the last hair. That’s probably the very thing that made Mr Zheng take up with her. Mr Zheng really can’t get Diao Kexin out of his mind.”

Over these few years Shasha had turned into a girl with a careful, close-woven mind. She knew that telling Xiao Jing all this today would kill at least three birds with one stone: first, it cut the relationship between Xiao Jing and Old Zheng off at the root; second, it made Diao Kexin a potential enemy of Xiao Jing’s — as far as the competition between Yuanwang and Xinghuan went, if Diao Kexin ever wanted to draw Xiao Jing over, this delicate bit of knowledge would build a wall in her way; third, it cleared her own relationship with Old Zheng absolutely clean in Xiao Jing’s eyes, while she herself, by sharing a secret with Xiao Jing, had raised their friendship a grade. “Sister, today I’ve told you gossip about my boss — you must keep it secret. You can’t say it was me who told you.”

Xiao Jing nodded busily, with the look of one who understood without being told.

The next evening Shasha was back in Xi’an. When she reached the company she went straight up to Old Zheng’s fourth floor. The code was her own birthday; she keyed it and walked in. Old Zheng was on the sofa looking at his phone. He looked up, saw her, and his face loosened.

“You’re back.”

“Mm.” Shasha sat down beside Zheng Zhiyong and told him she wanted to break up, with a very proper reason. “We’ve been together for years,” she said to Old Zheng, “but it’s a relationship with no name to it. I want to have a normal romance. One I can announce to the world, tell my parents and my friends about, one with a chance of marriage. An ordinary, ordinary romance. You can’t give me that.”

Old Zheng sat there without moving. Shasha was quite right: he could not give her those things. And yet this woman, for a year and a half, had given him the greatest comfort of body and mind; almost every week the two of them made love, sometimes staying the night. Old Zheng had taken a very great deal from this woman and given her very little. He had not given her a definite standing, nor had he kept the one promise he had made her: that from the very beginning all his secrets would be told to Shasha, that nothing would be hidden from her. That was the only thing Shasha had ever asked for, and he had not managed even that. He had not told her that he had slept with Xiao Jing, or kissed Xiaoya; nor had he told Shasha that over the past half year, through the whole spring and summer shipping season, he had quietly taken more than two million in cash out of the commissions paid by Wang Renhou’s company and by Qi Jin’s.

He was no longer the foolish Zheng Zhiyong. When Wu You discovered that the company was making no money at all and prepared to bring his own nephew in, Old Zheng was unwilling, but he had laid up a way out for himself half a year earlier, and none of this had he shared with Shasha, who was at his side. To him this woman was a capable sales commander, a weapon with which to take orders and reach the leaders, a prop to comfort his body at night — anything, in fact, but his wholehearted love.

That capacity for love which he had once poured into Diao Kexin — all of himself, everything staked — had vanished, completely and utterly.

Cong Shasha and Old Zheng held each other tight for a dozen seconds; then she turned and went, pulling the door shut behind her. Old Zheng sat on there. He reached for a cigarette; it took him two tries to find one. He lit it and drew on it, and his hand shook a little.

Then, a few minutes after Cong Shasha left, he sent Xiaoya a WeChat message.

“Come to Xi’an. I’ll buy you the ticket.”

Xiaoya answered quickly: “Huh?”

“Send me your ID card.”

The next evening Xiaoya arrived. Old Zheng drove out to meet her in his white Toyota Prado, took her to a buffet dinner, and over it they both drank a good deal. Then back to the company, straight up to the fourth floor.

The lift came up to the fourth floor; the corridor was very quiet. Old Zheng opened the door. No main light was on inside; only the park lamps beyond the floor-to-ceiling window let in a little. Xiaoya stood in the doorway. The last time she had come in she had looked around like a guest, and then left. Ten months had passed before she came back here again, and this time it seemed her standing was a little different. She saw the two-metre bed, the dark grey bedding, the screen, the bookshelves that filled a whole wall. Then she turned her head and looked at Old Zheng.

Old Zheng took off his clothes and laid them on the arm of the sofa, sat down on the sofa himself, and patted the place beside him. “Come here.”

Xiaoya sat down obediently on the sofa. Zheng Zhiyong looked at that face so like Diao Kexin’s and was lost in it for a moment: the round face, the sweet smile, the shallow dimples — a face identical to Diao Kexin’s.

But he knew she was not. Her eyes were cleaner than Diao Kexin’s, much cleaner, so clean that the fire in him burned hotter, and tasted worse. He reached out and took off her outer garment.

Xiaoya cooperated obediently. After a moment she reached out too, her fingertips touching his chest, and slowly, from the collar of his shirt downwards, she drew a line; and that one line opened every button, and the skin of Old Zheng’s chest showed. Xiaoya rested her hand on his chest, laid her palm flat on it, and could feel his heart going fast. She raised her head and looked at him straight, without flinching, and without that timid, testing look either. Her gaze was very steady — steady as though she had decided long ago what she would do tonight.

She turned her head aside and kissed him.

At first the kiss was light, like a trial. But in a moment it was not light any more. Xiaoya’s kiss was not Diao Kexin’s kind of sweetness, circling and hooking; her kiss was direct, pressing forward, her tongue probing in, with something peremptory in it. Old Zheng’s hand came up and settled on her waist; through the thin jumper he could feel the warmth of the body beneath.

She was pressed down onto the sofa. Zheng Zhiyong’s body was strong and broad, and his frame covered Xiaoya entirely; one of Xiaoya’s hands still held the front of his shirt, and Old Zheng, following the pull of her hand, bent down and down. She had fallen back completely on the sofa, her body sinking, and she raised her face to look at him.

“Take my clothes off,” she said.

Old Zheng knelt on one knee beside the sofa. He reached to lift her jumper; she cooperated, raising her arms, and the jumper came off over her head. Beneath it was a black close-fitting top, her chest rising and falling plainly. Old Zheng’s movements were not quick, but not hesitant in the least. She let him, silently, and now and then touched his skin with the fingertips that were free — hers were cool, and the skin of his body was burning. Then with both hands she pulled, and dragged off the shirt he had long since left open; Old Zheng’s upper garment fell away, and Xiaoya’s palm went straight onto his chest, and slowly moved down.

“Your body is so hot,” she said, under her breath.

Old Zheng did not answer. He took off all the clothes he had on, and kissed her, sealing her mouth. She looked at him, not covering, not shrinking, and met his lips.

After a while Old Zheng’s hand covered her breast and began a gentle stroking. She drew a soft breath, and a very light “mm” rolled out of her throat. The sound was not loud, but in that quiet room it was perfectly clear. Old Zheng’s hand tightened a little, and her breathing went ragged; her eyes half closed, her lips slightly parted.

“Gege.”

That one word, “Gege”, pulled a string somewhere in Old Zheng tight all at once. Diao Kexin had called him that too, but out of Xiaoya’s mouth it tasted different — not circling, not hooking, just called out straight, for him to hear.

He bowed his head and kissed her neck, the roots of her ears. Her neck was very sensitive; at a touch she shrank, and then came back to meet him. Her hand clutched at his back, hard enough to leave the mark of her nails. He gathered her up; her two legs wound round his waist of their own accord, and he carried her, from the sofa to the bed.

The bed was deep; when she was laid down into it her whole body sank into the dark grey quilt, her hair spread out, her face red, her eyes so bright it was frightening. She reached up and pulled him, pulled him down, and the two of them sank into it together.

Xiaoya was not the kind of woman who waits to be acted upon. She would reach for his waist of her own accord, would say very light things at his ear, would hook him back with her leg when he paused. Her body answered very directly: to every movement of his she gave a response — sometimes a sound in her throat, sometimes her fingers tightening on his back, sometimes lifting her own hips to meet him.

Old Zheng had not felt this for a long time. Not the kind of thing that comes with calculation, with probing, each giving back what the other gave; nor the kind of thing done to fill some hole. It was pure, physiological, unstoppable wanting. The names in his head — Xiao Jing, Diao Kexin, Shasha, Wu Peng — were all pressed down and away; there was only the body, only the person beneath him, only her every breath and every inch of warmth.

With this woman he pressed down the unease, pressed down the gloom, pressed down the pain. He knew Xiao Jing had gone, knew Shasha had gone; he knew Diao Kexin had come back, and though they had not yet crossed swords, he knew that Diao Kexin, coming back this time, would surely set herself against him more directly than before — and that this woman, this woman who looked identical to Diao Kexin, held in his hand, submitting to him, gave him a vast masculine dignity and satisfaction. Everything I have lost, I will take back, every last bit of it, in my own way, Old Zheng said to himself.

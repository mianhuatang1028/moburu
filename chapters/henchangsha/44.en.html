Chapter Forty-Four: Zhujian

“Zhujian” — In the Classic of Mountains and Seas (“Northern Mountains”), on Mount Danzhang there is a beast shaped like a leopard with a long tail, a human head, an ox’s ears and a single eye, called the zhujian. It is a great roarer, and when it walks it carries its tail in its mouth. As the saying goes: a leopard’s body and a long tail, a human head and ox’s ears, one eye looking out alone; when it walks, it carries its tail in its mouth. The zhujian has only one eye: when it looks left it can’t watch the right, when it looks ahead it can’t watch behind. When it walks it has to bite its own tail, for fear its head and tail won’t be able to look after each other.

Boss Lin in Shenzhen did his sums and called Old Zheng again. What he had to say on the phone wasn’t complicated, but there was a lot in it. Boss Lin didn’t beat around the bush on the phone; he went straight through the accounts. After this round of the Army Equipment Department’s assessment, although Xinghuan could only get a tertiary supplier’s share of around twenty percent, the Equipment Development Department, to guard against the risk of a single supplier cutting off supply, had recently issued supplementary guidance requiring that the components for main battle equipment have “multi-source supply assurance,” and tilting the budget toward backup suppliers with flexible production-line capability.

When he’d done his sums, Boss Lin told Old Zheng that he had enough factory floor space at present, but lacked a high-precision five-axis machining center to make the night-vision device’s structural parts. And because Xinghuan’s testing stage for the optoelectronic components had to meet military-inspection standards, he’d also need to put in a new high-and-low-temperature environmental simulation test bench. He hadn’t dared commit to these two fixed-asset investments before, but the Equipment Development Department’s “backup capacity subsidy” policy happened to cover roughly forty percent of the equipment purchase cost.

If Xinghuan could sign a framework agreement with the Seventh Academy for a “tertiary supplier’s guaranteed minimum volume,” fixing the basic procurement scale for the next three years, he would immediately use his own funds plus the government’s technical-upgrade subsidy to bring in the five-axis machine and the test bench. His exact words were: “As long as the base is stable, I’m not afraid to expand. What I’m afraid of is the policy up top changing every day. Now there’s policy backing and a guaranteed minimum, I’ll have finance start the process for equipment finance leasing right away.”

What it meant was that on Xinghuan’s “chokehold” problem in the heavy-asset link, Boss Lin was willing to lean in a bit more — take a bit of risk, but not rashly — and get it moving by way of “the outsourced factory upgrading its technology and expanding capacity.” When Old Zheng hung up, he sent Zhang Hailu a WeChat telling him to hurry to the Seventh Academy and keep on top of the details of the framework agreement, and he himself flew from Hefei to Beijing that same day.

Several groups of people were waiting for him in Beijing. On the one hand, even as tertiary supplier, there was still an agreement to sign. On the other, his two lady bodhisattvas were both still in Beijing, right where he’d left them, waiting for him.

When he came out of Capital Airport, the Beijing wind already carried the chill of late autumn.

Old Zheng sat in the back of the taxi looking at WeChat on his phone, his brows knitted tight. Old Zheng looked at the unread messages in WeChat.

Xiao Jing: “When are you coming to Beijing today? Last time I told you there are some things I need to go over with you — I need to say them in person.” The time showed it was sent before takeoff. The next message said: “Come to my place for dinner tonight.”

The more recent one was from Cong Shasha: “Brother Yong, you’ve landed, right? I’ve booked a restaurant — come straight over?” The time was half an hour ago, followed by the restaurant’s address.

These were two invitations, each sent by a woman who didn’t know the other existed.

Old Zheng didn’t know what Xiao Jing wanted to go over. Xinghuan’s share had been squeezed and her expectations had come to nothing — that was what Xiao Jing actually had in mind, but Old Zheng had no idea. On the last call she hadn’t said anything, only that they needed to meet. And Old Zheng was worried that she meant to force the issue again as his bedmate. He’d already told her when he was in Beijing before: being boyfriend and girlfriend was definitely out. If she still had to make it that kind of relationship, then at worst they’d just break it off. Xiao Jing’s work in the R&D stage was all done anyway, and from here on, whether the supply-stage volume was large or small, money would still go to her collaborating outside company as agreed. Neither would owe the other anything, and that would be that. On this trip to Beijing, if she tried to force the issue, Old Zheng planned to lay his cards on the table and cut off this physical relationship completely, and the two of them would go back to an ordinary client-and-vendor relationship.

As for Cong Shasha, before he set off, Old Zheng had complained to her about how hard he’d been working, running around Guangzhou and Shenzhen. He was thinking the two of them hadn’t seen each other for half a month; at last they could meet on the back of this business trip, and he could do his duty by his gentle, lovely companion.

He sent Xiao Jing a message: “I haven’t taken off yet. I’ll be in Beijing in the middle of the night. I’ll come see you tomorrow.”

“In a cab, on my way.” Old Zheng replied to Shasha.

Xiao Jing replied with an “Okay,” and followed up with another: “Then come to my place for dinner tomorrow night.”

Shasha sent the table number: “Table 20.”

At eight in the evening the two of them met at a Chinese restaurant near Zhichun Road in Haidian.

Cong Shasha was wearing a long dark green velvet dress, her makeup immaculate. When she saw Old Zheng her eyes lit up, and she got up herself to pour him a cup of tea.

“Is the business with Mr. Lin sorted?” Cong Shasha asked softly.

Old Zheng nodded. “There’s a chance now. He’s willing to put in some money, bring in equipment, and get the government technical-upgrade subsidy. That way we can dare to sign a commitment on supply capacity. As long as the Seventh Academy signs off on the guaranteed minimum, our supply chain can get going.”

“That’s wonderful. How are you so amazing?” Cong Shasha praised him, and added, “You’ve no idea — Zhang Hailu has obviously been anxious out of his mind these two weeks. This is great.”

Old Zheng drank some tea and looked at Cong Shasha across from him. The way she was now, she was every bit a sales director who could hold her own in the business world, who knew how to calm the team, who knew how to help him share the pressure. Old Zheng felt a touch of warmth in his heart, and a touch of guilt too.

“The guaranteed-minimum agreement for Mr. Lin — we’ll still need you to do the running around at the academy for it,” Old Zheng said.

“Sigh. Even as tertiary supplier, it’s this hard. I’ve talked with the few leaders and they’re all about the same — after all, the R&D is ours, so by custom we ought to get the most. But for things to have turned out like this, I suppose we can still live with it.” Cong Shasha looked at him. “Brother Yong, being out on the road these two weeks, your face has gone all thin and hollow. Eat more.” She didn’t finish what she was saying, but the hint in her eyes was already perfectly clear.

Then he looked up at Cong Shasha and said tenderly, “You’ve worked hard too, making you wait for me so long.”

Cong Shasha smiled, a smile gentle and proper.

That night, Old Zheng went back to Shasha’s room. While Cong Shasha was in the shower, Old Zheng stood on the balcony and smoked a cigarette. He looked at the night view in the distance, but floating up in his head despite himself was Xiao Jing’s meticulous flat and that cool, slightly commanding manner of hers. He quickly pinched out the cigarette and went back to bed. That night he was ferocious — two or three times in one night — as if venting all the pressure of the past two weeks, or as if making up in advance for some debt he couldn’t tell anyone about.

The next afternoon Old Zheng went to Fengtai.

Xiao Jing had steamed a whole rice cooker of white rice beforehand and ordered home-style dishes for delivery — chili stir-fried pork, greens with mushrooms, a mixed vegetable stir-fry, spicy diced chicken — and put them in bone-china serving plates and matching bone-china bowls ahead of time. The moment Old Zheng arrived she carried them out of the kitchen, so that when he came in there was a whole table of food, just warm.

Today Xiao Jing had the air of the lady of the house welcoming the man of the house home. Not only was the food still warm; she’d also got a small 100-milliliter bottle of Moutai and two proper Moutai baijiu glasses. She was wearing a long black slip dress with spaghetti straps, and walked up to Old Zheng with willowy grace and said to him, “Go wash your hands. Dinner’s ready.”

Old Zheng washed his hands and sat down at the little round table. Besides the elegant bone-china place settings, the table was decorated with a little pot of succulents. The baijiu had already been poured. Xiao Jing raised her glass and said, “It’s been twenty days since we’ve seen each other. Getting to see you at last really isn’t easy. Come on!” With that she gave Old Zheng a sweet smile and drained her glass without any fuss.

Old Zheng mulled it over: surely Xiao Jing wasn’t going to force the issue again this time, demanding a title? He was already completely caught between two fires as it was; he had no wish at all to make it official with her.

“Brother Yong, hurry up,” Xiao Jing urged.

Old Zheng drained his glass too.

Old Zheng sat down and looked at the table full of food, and did feel a touch of the warmth of home. Though he had a place to stay in Xi’an too, and a very luxuriously decorated one at that, day to day the stove there was cold and the pots empty, and now and then he’d order takeout — not like Xiao Jing’s home, so full of the smoke and bustle of real life. Little did he know that this meal of Xiao Jing’s was takeout too.

Xiao Jing picked up a piece of pork belly with her chopsticks and put it in Zheng Zhiyong’s rice bowl. Old Zheng said with feeling, “Xiao Jing, the way you are at home is such a huge contrast with how you are outside.”

Xiao Jing said, “A good contrast or a bad one?”

Old Zheng said, “A good contrast, of course.”

Xiao Jing said, “Then do you like it?”

Old Zheng said, “I like it, of course.”

Xiao Jing said, “Brother Yong, this time the R&D has been wrapped up and it’s moved into the supply stage. Nothing from here on has anything to do with me. So our client-and-vendor relationship has come to an end for now. Since you like me, can the two of us move on to a new stage now?”

Inside, Old Zheng thought: damn, now I’ve talked myself right into it. So he said, “Xiao Jing, I definitely like you very much. But I treasure what we have even more, so I don’t dare change things rashly.”

Xiao Jing said, “Then what are we, exactly? Fuck buddies?”

Old Zheng quickly said, “Of course not. You’re the only woman I have at this stage.”

Xiao Jing said, “Since my divorce, you’re the only one I’ve had too. But I’m thirty-five now, and I’ve got a practical problem: I have to find myself a boyfriend. If we can’t make it official, then I can’t stay in a relationship this unclear either.”

Old Zheng didn’t know that the main act of this Hongmen Banquet hadn’t even begun yet. He thought Xiao Jing wanted to negotiate about the relationship, and he’d long since worked out his response and what he’d say. So he said, “Xiao Jing, I like you very much — you can see that, can’t you? Every time I came to Beijing before and we were together, I found the warmth of home here with you, and found a reason to stay. But right now I’ve got real difficulties.”

Xiao Jing said, “What difficulties?”

Old Zheng said, “My work looks impressive from the outside, but really it’s still a mess. My personal debts are only just sorted out, and I could slide into new debts at any time. On top of that I still owe my ex-wife several million. And you’re in Beijing, and I don’t get to Beijing often, and never on a fixed schedule. If we became boyfriend and girlfriend, I’m afraid I couldn’t guarantee time with you. Wouldn’t you be unhappy then?”

Xiao Jing said, “I would. I would be unhappy. If you were my boyfriend, I’d hope we could be together often. Or rather, what I’d hope is that whenever you’re in Beijing, you can guarantee the time you spend the night here with me. And I’d hope the number of nights you stay over now could go up.”

Old Zheng said, “Right — and that’s exactly my real difficulty. I can’t do that. I can’t come to Beijing often, and when I do come to Beijing I can’t always come to you. And I’ve still got the burden of my ex-wife and child. I can’t offer you financial value the way a boyfriend should, and I can’t think about marriage and children down the line either. Those are my honest concerns.”

This was a down-to-earth conversation between two adults, with sincerity in it and helplessness too. Xiao Jing said, “I can understand that. So how are you thinking now? Do you think we ought to stop?”

Old Zheng said, “Like I said, Xiao Jing, I like you very much, and I like being with you very much. But I respect what you want. If you call a stop, we’ll stop.”

Xiao Jing said, “Since neither of us has anyone else, and I was never with you for the sake of marriage and children — what I want is a one-on-one relationship, and a more honest, deeper relationship with each other. If you never find a girlfriend, then as far as I’m concerned we’re already boyfriend and girlfriend, just not aiming at marriage. So I’ve never quite understood how you really see us. Or do you think every time we do it is just one more for free?”

Old Zheng leaned across and pressed his lips to Xiao Jing’s, then said, “Xiao Jing, you’re really very attractive to me, don’t you understand? I want what we have to be long-term. I don’t want to stop now.”

The kiss set Xiao Jing alight. She pulled Old Zheng to his feet and led him to the sofa. Old Zheng sat on the sofa, and Xiao Jing hitched up the hem of her black silk slip dress and straddled him, and the two of them began to kiss. Old Zheng held her waist with one hand, and with the other fondled Xiao Jing’s breasts, his mouth never stopping either. The food on the table had hardly been touched, and that little bit of baijiu wasn’t even finished.

They kissed for a while — fiercely, to be sure — but Old Zheng’s body never responded. Xiao Jing touched him with her hand, then stopped, puzzled, and looked at Zheng Zhiyong.

“Why don’t we go back and eat first,” Xiao Jing said, a little crestfallen.

The two of them went back to the table. Old Zheng said, “Xiao Jing, I’ve been under a lot of pressure lately. You know the company still hasn’t signed the supply-chain order.”

Xiao Jing said, “Of course I know. That’s why there’s something I want to tell you today too.”

Old Zheng said, “What is it?”

“Brother Yong, I won’t beat around the bush with you.” After all this back-and-forth testing what Old Zheng felt about her, Xiao Jing’s heart had become perfectly clear at this moment. “Xinghuan’s share with the Army Equipment people this time has been cut to under twenty percent. I’d always thought the five percent of the whole business you were giving me meant revenue. A few days ago I read the contract: it’s five percent that I get out of the gross profit. Worked out, that’s only a hundred or two hundred thousand. What I contributed, and what I ought to get, are far more than that.”

Old Zheng looked at her in astonishment; he’d never imagined she’d bring this up. “We agreed on this right from the start, and you agreed to the percentage right from the start too.”

Xiao Jing said, “What I said just now about being boyfriend and girlfriend was just a test. It looks like there wasn’t much sincerity in you being with me before — mostly it was just using me. You know very well that the technology on this project was almost entirely down to me. Without me you couldn’t have done it at all. Am I right?”

That was the truth. Without Xiao Jing, Old Zheng’s team couldn’t have managed the R&D on this project.

So Old Zheng said, “You and I are two adults who both wanted this. There was absolutely no using in it. You ought to know that. Your contribution to this project is big — I admit that. But five percent is what we agreed, and we signed an agreement on it. We’re still fighting for the supply chain right now; we ought to have a chance of getting twenty to twenty-five percent. What you get definitely won’t be as little as you’ve worked it out to be. And once this project’s commissioned, it’s long-term — there’s money every year, not just the once.”

“Five percent of all the revenue, or twenty percent of the profit.” Xiao Jing’s tone was very firm. “My assessment now is that the core algorithms I contributed to this project account for the absolute lion’s share of your gross margin. I always thought the five percent meant revenue; I never imagined it meant profit. Now the expectations of primary and secondary supplier are gone too, the pie’s shrunk, I’ve borne the risk, and I’ll still have to deal with delivery problems later. Twenty percent — that’s what I expect.”

Old Zheng narrowed his eyes and sized up Xiao Jing in front of him. When they’d negotiated back then, he really had deliberately blurred the wording of revenue versus profit, and when they made the agreement he’d made the wording clear: the share was explicitly on the basis of profit. He didn’t feel he’d done anything to be ashamed of, because a five percent hidden share meant five percent of the shareholders’ equity, so naturally it was a share of the profit — nothing wrong with that, right? It was only now that Xiao Jing had suddenly worked it out. He suddenly felt that the face that usually looked so delicate and haughty was, right now, full of demand. This and the woman who’d gone soft under his kisses in bed before, who’d acted coy with him — they were two entirely different people.

“Xiao Jing, what you’re bringing up now, I can’t do. The company isn’t mine. I’m not even the boss. The agreement’s already been signed — I can’t change it.” Old Zheng’s voice had gone cold too. “Xinghuan’s gross margin right now is under thirty percent, and our outsourced factory has to take half of it too — but they also carry several million in advance payments and the risk on top. Yours is really a business with no capital in, pure income. Take twenty percent of the profit on top of that, and it means the whole company is working on this project for you alone. If it really comes to that, then we can’t work together from here on.”

Xiao Jing clearly hadn’t expected Old Zheng to be so hard. “Nothing you want to do is ever impossible for you. What do you mean, we can’t work together?”

“What I mean is, if it’s twenty percent, then our collaboration won’t even begin.” Old Zheng stood up and looked down at her.

“Zheng Zhiyong!” Xiao Jing stood up too, a sheen of tears in her eyes. “You’ve been scheming against me?!”

“You’re the one scheming against me,” Old Zheng said coldly. “Feelings are feelings, business is business. That whole business just now about making it official — what were you thinking? Don’t use feelings to hold my business hostage. That doesn’t work on me.”

Xiao Jing’s eyes reddened. She looked at Old Zheng as if looking at a stranger. In the end she took a deep breath and pointed at the door. “Get out.”

Old Zheng picked up his jacket, went to the door, stopped and looked back at her. “Calm down a bit. There’s no need to be this angry. What we agreed was a five percent hidden share, and a shareholder definitely gets a share of the profit — think about it, isn’t that logical? Let your anger cool first. When it has, call me anytime, and I’ll come back and apologize. I’ll go now.”

He pushed open the door and walked out of that flat that had once stirred his heart, where he’d felt something of the warmth of home. The door slammed shut behind him. That affair was broken, but so was Xiao Jing’s idea of getting a bigger share — cut off completely. But then it occurred to Zheng Zhiyong that it was lucky things had fallen apart at that very moment — otherwise he might never have walked out of that door so smoothly.

At ten at night, Old Zheng went back to Shasha’s room.

Cong Shasha was lying on the bed playing a game on her phone. When she saw him come back she asked, “What’s wrong? Why do you look so awful?”

Old Zheng lay down beside her, grabbed her in his arms and buried his face in the hollow of her neck. At that moment he felt utterly exhausted. Xiao Jing was demanding her price, Wu You wasn’t satisfied, Zhang Hailu was counting on him, and Xiaoya was back in Chengdu, waiting for him to give her a rice bowl. He was like that zhujian, biting its own tail, head and tail unable to look after each other.

“Don’t talk,” Old Zheng said, his voice a little hoarse.

He raised his head and kissed Cong Shasha hard. Cong Shasha was startled for a moment, then answered him obediently and wrapped her arms around his neck. Old Zheng tore off her clothes and pressed down on her without any warning. He needed to be exhausted completely, to fill the hole that had been hollowed out of his heart.

In that room that night, the colors of spring hid the chaos of war beyond the door.
